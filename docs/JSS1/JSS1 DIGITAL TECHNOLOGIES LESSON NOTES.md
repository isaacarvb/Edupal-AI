MEANING OF DIGITAL TECHNOLOGY

What is Digital Technology? Digital technology refers to electronic tools and devices that use digital information (0s and 1s) to create, store, and share information.
Digital vs. Analog:

Analog: Uses continuous signals (e.g., old clock with hands)

Digital: Uses 0s and 1s (e.g., digital clock with numbers)

Why "Digital"? Computers understand only two states: ON (1) and OFF (0). All information is converted to combinations of 0s and 1s called binary code.

EARLY CALCULATING TOOLS

ABACUS (3000 BC)

Wooden frame with beads on rods

Used for counting and simple calculations

Still used in some countries today

NAPIER'S BONES (1617)

Numbered rods made by John Napier

Used for multiplication and division

Made calculations easier

PASCALINE (1642)

First mechanical calculator by Blaise Pascal

Used gears and wheels to add numbers

CHARLES BABBAGE'S ANALYTICAL ENGINE (1837)

Designed by Charles Babbage (Father of the Computer)

Never fully built but had all parts of modern computer
Ada Lovelace wrote first computer program for it

Expensive

Characteristics:

Smaller than first generation

Less heat

More reliable

Faster

Less expensive

Much smaller

Faster

TYPES OF STORAGE

PRIMARY STORAGE (Main Memory)

Characteristics: Inside computer, temporary, fast, loses data when power is off
RAM (Random Access Memory)

Temporary "working memory"

Holds currently running programs and data

Volatile: Loses data when power off

Fast: Very quick access

Sizes: 2GB, 4GB, 8GB, 16GB, 32GB

Analogy: Like your desk - cleared when you leave

ROM (Read-Only Memory)

Permanent memory

Stores startup instructions (BIOS)

Non-volatile: Keeps data when power off

Read-only: Cannot easily change

WEEK 9: COMPUTER BOOTING AND SHUTDOWN

MEANING OF BOOTING

Booting: Starting up a computer and loading the operating system.

Computer checks hardware

Loads OS from hard disk to RAM

Prepares computer for use

TYPES OF BOOTING

Cold Booting (Hard Boot)
Starting from completely powered-off state

Full startup process (slower)

Used: First start of day, after power failure

Warm Booting (Restart)

Restarting while computer is on

Faster than cold boot

Used: After installing software, fixing problems

BOOTING PROCESS (What Happens)

POST (Power-On Self-Test): Checks hardware, beeps if OK

BIOS Activation: Locates operating system

Load Boot Loader: Small program loads OS

Load OS: Windows/macOS loads into RAM

Load Startup Programs: Antivirus, etc. start automatically

Desktop Appears: Computer ready to use

Time: 30 seconds to 2 minutes

PROPER BOOTING PROCEDURE

Check all connections (monitor, keyboard, mouse, power)

Turn on monitor

Press computer power button once

Wait patiently (don't touch anything)

Login if required (username/password)
Wait for desktop to fully load

SHUTTING DOWN A COMPUTER

Why Proper Shutdown Matters:

Saves work and closes programs properly

Prevents data loss and file corruption

Protects hard disk

NEVER:

Press power button without shutdown process

Unplug while on

Turn off at wall socket directly

PROPER SHUTDOWN PROCEDURE (Windows)

Save work and close all programs

Click Start menu (Windows button)

Click Power icon

Choose Shut down

Wait for complete shutdown (lights off, fans stop)

Turn off monitor

Turn off wall socket (optional, especially during storms)

SUBTOPICS:

WHAT IS A COMPUTER NETWORK?

Network: Two or more computers connected together to share resources and information.

Simple Example: Computers in your school computer lab connected together.

WHY CREATE NETWORKS?

Share files and printers

Share internet connection

Communicate easily

Work together on projects

TYPES OF NETWORKS:

LAN (Local Area Network)

Small area (one building)

School, office, home

Fast and cheap

WAN (Wide Area Network)

Large area (city, country, world)
Internet is the largest WAN

More expensive

MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

Covers a city

Between LAN and WAN

COMPONENTS OF A NETWORK:

COMPUTERS/DEVICES

Desktops, laptops, tablets, phones

Called "nodes" or "hosts"
NETWORK INTERFACE CARD (NIC)

Hardware inside computer

Allows computer to connect to network

Has unique MAC address

CABLES

Ethernet Cable: Most common

Connects devices together

ROUTER

Connects network to internet

Directs data to correct destination

Wi-Fi routers broadcast wireless signal

SWITCH

Connects multiple devices

Allows them to communicate

MODEM

Converts signals for internet connection

Often combined with router

SERVER

Powerful computer that provides services

Stores files, manages network

NETWORK SOFTWARE

Operating system

Network protocols (rules)
Security software

HOW A NETWORK WORKS (Simple):

Computer A wants to send file to Computer B

File broken into small packets

Packets travel through network (cables/Wi-Fi)

Switch/router directs packets to Computer B

Computer B receives and reassembles file

All happens in seconds!

BENEFITS OF NETWORKING:

RESOURCE SHARING

Printers: One printer for many computers

Files: Share documents easily

Internet: One connection for all

Software: Share programs

Example: School computer lab - 20 computers share 2 printers

COMMUNICATION

Send messages instantly

Email within organization

Video conferencing

Group chats

DATA STORAGE
Central storage on server

Access files from any computer

Easy backup

COST SAVINGS

Share expensive devices (printers, scanners)

One internet connection

Shared software licenses

COLLABORATION

Work together on projects

Share ideas

Real-time updates

SECURITY

Central backup

Better virus protection

Controlled access

Monitor network activity

FLEXIBILITY

Access from anywhere in network

Add new devices easily

NETWORK TOPOLOGIES (Arrangement):

BUS TOPOLOGY

All computers on single cable
Simple and cheap

If cable breaks, network fails

STAR TOPOLOGY

All computers connect to central hub/switch

Most common

If one cable breaks, others still work

RING TOPOLOGY

Computers in circular arrangement

Data travels in one direction

NETWORK SECURITY:

Passwords and user accounts

Firewall (blocks unauthorized access)

Antivirus software

Encryption (scrambles data)

WEEK 2: SPAM AND ONLINE THREATS

Meaning of Spam

Spam refers to unsolicited, unwanted messages sent in bulk over the internet, typically for advertising, phishing, or spreading malware. It wastes time, clogs systems, and can pose security risks.

Types of Spam:

Email spam

SMS/text spam

Comment spam on websites
Social media spam

Search engine spam

Why Spam is Harmful:

Wastes time and bandwidth

Can spread viruses and malware

May lead to identity theft

Clogs email servers

Used for phishing attacks

Common Online Scams

Phishing Scams

Fake messages pretending to be from legitimate organizations to steal personal information.

Warning Signs:

Generic greetings ("Dear Customer")

Urgent language creating panic

Spelling and grammar errors

Suspicious sender addresses

Requests for passwords or financial details

Lottery/Prize Scams

Claims of winning money or prizes, requiring fees or personal information to claim.

Red Flags:

You didn't enter any contest

Requests for payment to claim winnings

Pressure to act quickly
Claims from unknown foreign sources

Romance Scams (Catfishing)

Fake online relationships developed to eventually request money for fabricated emergencies.

Prevention:

Be cautious with people you've never met

Never send money to online-only contacts

Use video chat to verify identity

Research profile photos using reverse image search

Fake Online Stores

Fraudulent websites that take payment but never deliver products.

Identifying Signs:

Deals too good to be true

Poor website design

No physical address or contact information

Only accepts untraceable payment methods

Recently created websites

Tech Support Scams

Scammers pose as technical support, claiming device problems and requesting remote access or payment.
Warning Signs:

Unsolicited calls about computer problems

Pressure to act immediately

Requests for remote device access
Payment demands for fake fixes

Preventive Measures Password Security:
Use strong passwords (8-12 characters mixing uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols)

Different passwords for different accounts

Change passwords regularly

Never share passwords

Email and Message Safety:

Verify sender identity before clicking links

Check email addresses for misspellings

Don't open attachments from unknown sources

Use spam filters

Report suspicious messages

Account Protection:

Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)

Use biometric authentication when available

Log out from shared computers

Monitor account activity regularly

Software and Device Security:

Keep all software updated

Use antivirus software

Enable firewalls

Download apps only from official stores
Scan downloads before opening

General Best Practices:

Trust your instincts

Research before acting

Don't rush decisions

Report scams to authorities

Educate family members

Keep personal information private on social media

Monitor financial statements

WEEK 3: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Meaning of Intellectual Property

Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the human mind that have commercial or artistic value and are protected by law. These include inventions, artistic works, designs, symbols, names, and images. IP rights give creators legal ownership and control over their work, preventing unauthorized use.

Why IP Protection Matters:

Encourages creativity and innovation

Protects creators' financial interests
Ensures proper credit to original creators

Prevents theft of ideas and work

Supports economic growth

Types of Intellectual Property

Copyright

Legal protection for original creative works including books, music, films, software, artwork, photographs, and digital content.
What Copyright Protects:

Literary works (books, articles, poems)

Musical compositions and recordings

Artistic works (paintings, sculptures, photographs)

Dramatic works (plays, scripts)

Films and videos

Computer programs and software

Architectural designs

Copyright Duration: Generally lasts for the creator's lifetime plus 50-70 years (varies by country).
Rights of Copyright Holders:

Reproduce the work

Distribute copies

Create adaptations or derivative works

Perform or display publicly

License others to use the work
Copyright Symbol: © followed by year and owner's name

Trademark

A distinctive sign, symbol, word, phrase, logo, or design that identifies and distinguishes products or services of one company from others.
Examples:

Nike's swoosh logo

McDonald's golden arches

Apple's apple logo

Coca-Cola's script font

Company slogans

Purpose of Trademarks:

Brand identification

Consumer protection

Business reputation

Market differentiation

Trademark Symbol: ™ (for unregistered) or ® (for registered trademarks)

Plagiarism

The act of using someone else's work, ideas, or words without giving proper credit or obtaining permission, presenting them as your own.
Forms of Plagiarism:

Direct plagiarism: Copying word-for-word without quotes or citation

Self-plagiarism: Reusing your own previous work without disclosure

Mosaic plagiarism: Mixing copied phrases with your own words
Accidental plagiarism: Forgetting to cite or improperly paraphrasing

Consequences of Plagiarism:

Academic penalties (failing grades, suspension, expulsion)

Legal action and fines

Damage to reputation

Loss of credibility

Career consequences

How to Avoid Plagiarism:

Always cite sources properly

Use quotation marks for direct quotes

Paraphrase in your own words

Keep track of sources while researching

Use plagiarism detection tools

Ask for clarification when unsure Respecting Digital Content Ownership Best Practices:
Give Credit: Always acknowledge original creators with proper citations

Seek Permission: Ask before using someone's work, especially for commercial purposes

Use Legal Alternatives:

Royalty-free images (Unsplash, Pexels)

Creative Commons licensed content

Public domain works

Understand Licenses: "Free to view" doesn't mean "free to use"
Avoid Piracy: Don't illegally download or share copyrighted music, movies, software, or books
Respect Social Media Content: Don't repost others' photos or videos without permission

Create Original Work: Develop your own content whenever possible

Creative Commons Licenses: Flexible copyright licenses allowing creators to specify how others can use their work:
CC BY: Credit must be given

CC BY-SA: Credit given, share alike

CC BY-ND: Credit given, no derivatives

CC BY-NC: Credit given, non-commercial use only

Digital Citizenship: Being a responsible digital citizen means respecting intellectual property rights, supporting creators by using legal sources, and promoting ethical online behavior.

Antivirus Software

Antivirus software is a program designed to detect, prevent, and remove malicious software (malware) from computers and devices. It acts as a shield protecting your device from various threats.

Types of Malware:

Viruses: Programs that attach to files and spread when files are shared

Worms: Self-replicating malware that spreads across networks

Trojans: Disguised as legitimate software but perform harmful actions
Spyware: Secretly monitors and collects user information

Ransomware: Encrypts files and demands payment for decryption

Adware: Displays unwanted advertisements

How Antivirus Works:

Scanning: Examines files and programs for known malware signatures

Real-time Protection: Monitors system activity continuously

Quarantine: Isolates suspicious files to prevent damage

Removal: Deletes or repairs infected files

Updates: Downloads latest threat definitions regularly

Popular Antivirus Programs:

Windows Defender (built into Windows)

Norton

McAfee

Avast

Kaspersky

Bitdefender

Best Practices:

Keep antivirus software updated

Run regular full system scans

Enable real-time protection

Don't disable antivirus temporarily

Scan external drives before use

WEEK 6: INTRODUCTION TO GRAPHIC DESIGN

Meaning and Importance of Graphic Design

Graphic design is the art and practice of visual communication that combines images, words, colors, and ideas to convey information to a specific audience. It's both creative and strategic, solving problems through visual solutions.
Definition: The creative process of planning and projecting ideas through visual and textual content.

Importance of Graphic Design:

Visual Communication: Conveys messages quickly and effectively without many words
Brand Identity: Creates recognizable logos, colors, and styles for businesses

Attracts Attention: Eye-catching designs draw people in

Professionalism: Quality design shows credibility and trustworthiness

Information Organization: Makes complex data easy to understand

Emotional Connection: Colors and images evoke feelings and responses

Competitive Advantage: Good design helps businesses stand out

User Experience: Improves interaction with websites and apps

Where Graphic Design is Used:

Advertisements and marketing materials

Websites and mobile apps

Social media posts and graphics

Logos and brand identity

Posters and flyers

Magazines and newspapers

Product packaging

Books and publications

Billboards and signage

Presentations and infographics

Elements of Design

Design elements are the basic building blocks used to create visual compositions.

Color

Color is one of the most powerful design elements, influencing emotions, behavior, and perception.
Color Psychology:

Red: Energy, passion, urgency, danger, excitement

Blue: Trust, calm, professionalism, security, stability

Green: Nature, growth, health, harmony, freshness

Yellow: Happiness, optimism, warmth, caution

Orange: Enthusiasm, creativity, confidence, friendliness

Purple: Luxury, wisdom, creativity, spirituality

Black: Elegance, sophistication, power, formality

White: Purity, simplicity, cleanliness, innocence

Brown: Earthiness, reliability, stability, comfort

Color Concepts:

Primary Colors: Red, blue, yellow (cannot be created by mixing)

Secondary Colors: Green, orange, purple (mix of two primaries)

Tertiary Colors: Mix of primary and secondary colors

Complementary Colors: Opposite on color wheel (red/green, blue/orange)

Analogous Colors: Next to each other on color wheel

Color Harmony: Pleasing arrangement of colors

Using Color Effectively:

Consider cultural meanings

Maintain contrast for readability

Limit color palette (3-5 colors)

Use color to create hierarchy

Test designs in black and white first
Shape

Shapes are defined areas created by lines, color, or texture. They give structure and meaning to designs.
Types of Shapes:

Geometric Shapes:

Circles: Unity, completeness, harmony, infinity

Squares/Rectangles: Stability, reliability, order, professionalism

Triangles: Direction, movement, energy, conflict, stability

Organic Shapes:

Natural, flowing, irregular forms

Found in nature (leaves, clouds, water)

Feel more friendly and approachable

Abstract Shapes:

Simplified versions of natural forms

Recognizable but stylized

Used in logos and icons

Uses of Shapes:

Create visual interest

Direct eye movement

Organize information

Convey meaning symbolically

Build brand identity

Typography
Typography is the art and technique of arranging text to make written language readable, legible, and visually appealing.
Font Categories:

Serif Fonts:

Have small lines/strokes at letter ends

Examples: Times New Roman, Georgia, Garamond

Feel: Traditional, formal, authoritative, trustworthy

Best for: Print materials, formal documents, body text

Sans-Serif Fonts:

Clean, without decorative strokes

Examples: Arial, Helvetica, Calibri, Roboto

Feel: Modern, clean, minimal, straightforward

Best for: Digital screens, headings, contemporary designs

Script Fonts:

Mimic handwriting or calligraphy

Examples: Brush Script, Pacifico, Dancing Script

Feel: Elegant, personal, creative, decorative

Best for: Invitations, logos, special occasions

Display Fonts:

Decorative, unique, attention-grabbing

Used sparingly for headlines

Feel: Varied depending on style

Best for: Titles, logos, short text
Typography Principles:

Hierarchy: Different sizes/weights show importance

Alignment: How text lines up (left, right, center, justified)

Spacing: Letter spacing (kerning) and line spacing (leading)

Contrast: Different fonts/sizes create visual interest

Readability: Easy to read and understand

Consistency: Use 2-3 fonts maximum per design

Additional Design Elements:

Line:

Can be straight, curved, thick, thin, solid, dashed

Creates structure, directs eye movement

Separates or connects elements

Texture:

Surface quality or appearance

Can be actual (physical) or visual (implied)

Adds depth and interest

Space (White Space):

Empty areas around elements

Gives designs room to breathe

Improves readability and focus

Shows sophistication

Size/Scale:

Relative size of elements
Creates hierarchy and emphasis

Guides viewer's attention

Balance:

Visual weight distribution

Symmetrical or asymmetrical

Creates harmony and stability

Meaning and Types of Digital Media

Definition: Digital media refers to any content created, stored, distributed, and consumed in digital electronic format using digital technology.

Characteristics:

Exists in binary code (0s and 1s)

Easily copied and distributed

Can be edited and manipulated

Accessible across multiple devices
Interactive and engaging

Types of Digital Media

Text-Based Media

Written content in digital format.

Examples: Blogs, e-books, articles, social media posts, websites, emails, e-magazines

Uses: Information sharing, education, communication, entertainment, marketing

Images/Visual Media

Still visual content in digital form.

Examples: Photographs, graphics (logos, illustrations), infographics, memes, digital art, screenshots
Formats: JPEG, PNG, GIF, SVG, TIFF

Uses: Visual storytelling, brand identity, social media content, documentation, artistic expression

Audio Media

Sound content in digital format.

Examples: Music, podcasts, audiobooks, sound effects, voice messages, radio streams

Formats: MP3, WAV, AAC, FLAC

WEEK 10: COMPUTER WASTE MANAGEMENT

Meaning of E-waste

Definition: Electronic waste (e-waste) refers to discarded electrical or electronic devices and their components that have reached the end of their useful life.

Common Examples:

Computing Devices: Computers, laptops, tablets, keyboards, mice

Mobile Devices: Smartphones, smartwatches

Display Equipment: Monitors, televisions, projectors
Peripherals: Printers, scanners, hard drives, USB drives

Home Appliances: Microwaves, refrigerators, washing machines (with electronics)

Entertainment: Game consoles, DVD players, cameras, speakers

Batteries and Accessories: Chargers, headphones, adapters

Why E-waste is a Problem Environmental Concerns
Toxic Materials E-waste contains hazardous substances:

Lead: Damages nervous system (in batteries, monitors)

Mercury: Affects brain and kidneys (in switches, screens)

Cadmium: Causes kidney damage (in rechargeable batteries)

Chromium: Can cause cancer (in metal casings)

Brominated Flame Retardants: Disrupt hormones (in circuit boards)

Soil and Water Contamination

Toxins leach into soil from landfills

Contaminate groundwater

Enter food chain through plants and animals

Air Pollution

Burning e-waste releases toxic fumes

Health risks for nearby communities

Health Concerns

Respiratory problems

Skin diseases and allergies
Neurological damage

Increased cancer risk

Particularly dangerous for children

Resource Waste

E-waste contains valuable materials (gold, silver, copper, platinum)

Throwing away means losing these resources

Requires more mining for new materials

Global Scale

Over 50 million tons generated globally per year

Fastest-growing waste stream

Only 17-20% properly recycled

Safe Disposal Methods

Return to Manufacturer

How it works: Check manufacturer's website for take-back programs; some offer trade-in incentives
Examples: Apple Recycling Program, Dell Reconnect, HP, Samsung

Benefits: Ensures proper handling, may receive discount on new purchase

Donation

Give working or repairable devices to those who need them.

Where to Donate: Schools, community centers, non-profit organizations, churches, libraries, charities
Before Donating:

Ensure device works properly

Delete all personal data

Include cables and accessories

Clean the device

Benefits: Extends device lifespan, helps others access technology, reduces e-waste

Certified E-waste Recyclers

Specialized facilities that safely process electronic waste.

What They Do:

Safely dismantle electronics

Separate hazardous materials

Recover valuable metals and components

Follow environmental regulations

How to Find: Search online for certified recyclers with R2 or e-Stewards certifications

Government Collection Centers

Features: Free drop-off locations with regular collection hours

Finding Centers: Contact local government environmental office or check municipal websites

Retail Store Take-Back Programs Examples: Best Buy, Staples, Office Depot
Features: Often free for small electronics; can combine with shopping trips

Important: Never Do These

❌ Don't throw in regular trash

❌ Don't burn electronic waste

❌ Don't dump in nature or water

❌ Don't dismantle without proper training

❌ Don't give to uncertified collectors

Before Disposal - Data Security

Back up important data

Factory reset device

Remove SIM cards and memory cards

Log out of all accounts

For computers: wipe hard drive or physically destroy

Recycling Practices

The E-waste Recycling Process

Step 1: Collection and Transportation

E-waste collected and transported to recycling facilities

Step 2: Manual Sorting

Devices categorized by type

Working items identified for reuse

Batteries and hazardous materials removed
Step 3: Mechanical Shredding

Items broken into small pieces

Step 4: Separation

Magnetic separation removes iron and steel

Water separation divides plastic from metal

Screening sorts by size

Step 5: Material Recovery

Precious metals extracted (gold, silver, platinum)

Base metals recovered (copper, aluminum)

Plastics sorted and processed

Step 6: Purification

Metals refined to pure form

Materials prepared for reuse in new products

Benefits of E-waste Recycling Environmental Benefits:
Reduces landfill waste by 80-90%

Prevents soil and water pollution

Conserves natural resources

Reduces greenhouse gas emissions

Economic Benefits:

Creates jobs in recycling industry

Recovers valuable materials worth billions
Supports circular economy

Resource Conservation:

One ton of e-waste contains more gold than 17 tons of gold ore

Recoverable copper, silver, aluminum, and rare earth elements
