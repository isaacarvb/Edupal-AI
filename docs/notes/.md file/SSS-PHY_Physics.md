## Mechanics

### Motion, Scalars and Vectors
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Distinguish between scalar and vector quantities with examples
- Resolve vectors into components and find resultant using graphical and analytical methods
- Define and calculate displacement, velocity, acceleration and speed
- Apply equations of uniformly accelerated motion (SUVAT)
- Sketch and interpret velocity-time and displacement-time graphs
- Solve projectile motion problems

#### 📖 Notes
- Scalar quantities: magnitude only (distance, speed, mass, temperature, energy)
- Vector quantities: magnitude AND direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum)
- Vector addition: head-to-tail (triangle rule) and parallelogram rule
- Resolution of vectors: Fx = F cosθ, Fy = F sinθ
- Resultant vector: R = √(Fx² + Fy²), direction θ = tan⁻¹(Fy/Fx)
- Definitions: distance (scalar), displacement (vector); speed = distance/time, velocity = displacement/time
- Acceleration: a = (v − u)/t; deceleration is negative acceleration
- SUVAT equations: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as; s = (u+v)t/2
- Displacement-time graph: gradient = velocity; flat line = stationary; curve = acceleration
- Velocity-time graph: gradient = acceleration; area under graph = displacement
- Free fall: acceleration due to gravity g = 10 m/s² (9.8 m/s² more precisely)
- Projectile motion: horizontal component constant; vertical component affected by gravity; range R = u²sin2θ/g; max height H = u²sin²θ/2g

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Experimentally determine g using a free-fall apparatus or a pendulum; compare with standard value
2. Draw velocity-time graphs for three motions: uniform velocity, uniform acceleration from rest, and deceleration to rest; calculate total displacement
3. Calculate the range and maximum height of a ball kicked at 20 m/s at 30° and 60° angles; compare results and explain why both give same range

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests SUVAT equations, graph interpretation and projectile motion — identify u, v, a, s, t before selecting an equation
> JAMB tests vector resolution, resultant calculation and graph reading — know that area under v-t graph = displacement
> Trap: confusing speed (scalar) with velocity (vector); using distance in displacement questions — read each question carefully for which quantity is asked

### Newton's Laws of Motion, Momentum and Friction
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- State and apply Newton's three laws of motion
- Calculate momentum and apply the principle of conservation of momentum
- Distinguish between elastic and inelastic collisions
- Define impulse and relate it to change in momentum
- Analyse frictional forces and calculate coefficients of friction

#### 📖 Notes
- Newton's 1st Law (Law of Inertia): a body continues in its state of rest or uniform motion unless acted upon by an external net force
- Newton's 2nd Law: F = ma; net force is proportional to rate of change of momentum
- Newton's 3rd Law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction (action-reaction pairs)
- Momentum: p = mv; unit: kg m/s; vector quantity
- Conservation of momentum: total momentum before = total momentum after (in closed system with no external force)
- Elastic collision: kinetic energy conserved; objects bounce apart
- Inelastic collision: kinetic energy not conserved; objects may stick together (perfectly inelastic)
- Impulse: J = Ft = Δp = mv − mu; unit: N·s = kg·m/s
- Impulse-momentum theorem: applied in crash safety (crumple zones, airbags lengthen time to increase safety)
- Friction: static friction (fs ≤ μsN) resists motion; kinetic friction (fk = μkN) opposes motion
- Coefficient of friction μ: dimensionless; μk < μs always
- Friction applications: braking distance, walking, driving on wet roads

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Set up a trolley experiment on a friction-compensated runway; verify F = ma by varying force and measuring acceleration
2. Solve a collision problem: a 2 kg ball moving at 5 m/s collides with a stationary 3 kg ball; find velocities after elastic collision
3. Measure the coefficient of static friction between a wooden block and different surfaces (smooth wood, sandpaper, carpet)

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests all three Newton's laws, momentum conservation in collision problems, and impulse — draw before-and-after diagrams for collision problems
> JAMB tests F = ma problems, friction calculations, and impulse-momentum — identify the system, direction of forces, and apply conservation correctly
> Trap: forgetting direction in momentum problems (make one direction positive, stick to it) and confusing static with kinetic friction

### Work, Energy and Power
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-001-03`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define work, energy and power with correct SI units
- Calculate work done by a constant force at an angle
- Apply the work-energy theorem
- State and apply the law of conservation of energy
- Distinguish between elastic and inelastic potential energy
- Calculate power and efficiency of machines

#### 📖 Notes
- Work: W = Fs cosθ; unit: joule (J); only the component of force in direction of displacement does work
- Work done against gravity: W = mgh (independent of path)
- Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv²; unit: J
- Gravitational potential energy: GPE = mgh; unit: J
- Elastic potential energy: EPE = ½ke² (spring); k = spring constant (N/m), e = extension
- Work-energy theorem: net work done on a body = change in kinetic energy; Wnet = ΔKE
- Conservation of energy: energy cannot be created or destroyed; only transforms from one form to another
- Conservation in mechanics: KE + GPE = constant (no friction); ½mv² + mgh = constant
- Power: P = W/t = Fv; unit: watt (W) = J/s
- Efficiency: η = (useful output energy / total input energy) × 100%; always ≤ 100%
- Energy resources: renewable (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass) vs. non-renewable (fossil fuels, nuclear)
- Energy transformations: electrical → light (bulb), chemical → kinetic (car engine), solar → electrical (panel)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Calculate the minimum power needed to pump 1000 litres of water to a height of 15 m in 5 minutes
2. Release a ball from different heights on a ramp; measure speed at the bottom; verify KE = GPE using v² = 2gh
3. Analyse a hydroelectric dam system: identify energy transformations from reservoir to electrical output; calculate efficiency if 20% is lost to heat

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests energy conservation, power, efficiency, and W = Fs cosθ for inclined planes — always check if the angle is between force and displacement
> JAMB tests quick calculation of KE, GPE, power and efficiency — memorise KE = ½mv² and P = W/t
> Trap: using W = Fs without checking the angle; efficiency above 100% is impossible — re-check working if you get this

## Waves and Sound

### Wave Motion and Properties
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define and classify waves (transverse, longitudinal, progressive, stationary)
- Identify and measure wave properties (amplitude, frequency, wavelength, period, speed)
- Apply the wave equation v = fλ
- Describe reflection, refraction, diffraction and interference of waves
- Explain resonance and standing waves

#### 📖 Notes
- Wave: a disturbance that transfers energy without transferring matter
- Transverse waves: oscillation perpendicular to wave direction (light, water waves, electromagnetic waves)
- Longitudinal waves: oscillation parallel to wave direction (sound, compression waves)
- Wave properties: amplitude (A) — max displacement; period (T) — time for one complete oscillation; frequency (f) — oscillations per second (Hz); wavelength (λ) — distance between two adjacent crests
- Wave equation: v = fλ = λ/T
- Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection (law of reflection applies to all waves)
- Refraction: wave changes speed and direction when entering a new medium; frequency unchanged
- Diffraction: spreading of waves around obstacles or through gaps; most prominent when gap ≈ wavelength
- Interference: superposition of two waves; constructive (crests meet — amplitude doubles) and destructive (crest meets trough — amplitude cancels)
- Standing (stationary) waves: produced by superposition of two identical waves traveling in opposite directions
- Nodes: points of zero displacement in standing wave; antinodes: points of maximum displacement
- Resonance: when driving frequency = natural frequency, large amplitude vibrations occur

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Use a slinky spring to demonstrate both transverse and longitudinal waves; measure wavelength, count frequency, calculate speed
2. Set up a ripple tank experiment to observe reflection, refraction and diffraction of water waves
3. Demonstrate standing waves on a stretched string: find the fundamental frequency and first three harmonics by adjusting tension

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests wave calculations (v = fλ), properties identification, and descriptions of interference/diffraction — sketch diagrams for full marks
> JAMB tests v = fλ substitutions, transverse vs. longitudinal classification, and standing waves (nodes/antinodes)
> Trap: confusing period (T in seconds) with frequency (f in Hz); remember f = 1/T

### Sound Waves
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-002-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Explain the production and transmission of sound
- Calculate speed of sound in different media and at different temperatures
- Explain echoes, reverberation, loudness and pitch
- Describe resonance in air columns and explain musical instruments
- Explain the Doppler effect and its applications

#### 📖 Notes
- Sound: longitudinal mechanical wave requiring a medium; cannot travel in vacuum
- Speed of sound: ≈340 m/s in air at 0°C; faster in liquids and solids (denser medium → faster travel in elastic media)
- Speed increases with temperature: v ≈ 331 + 0.6T m/s (T in °C)
- Frequency range: infrasound (<20 Hz), audible (20–20,000 Hz), ultrasound (>20,000 Hz)
- Pitch: related to frequency — higher frequency → higher pitch
- Loudness: related to amplitude and intensity; measured in decibels (dB)
- Quality/Timbre: determined by harmonics present — distinguishes instruments playing same note
- Echo: reflection of sound from a hard surface; time delay > 0.1s for separate echo to be perceived
- Reverberation: multiple rapid echoes causing prolonged sound; affects acoustics in buildings
- Resonance in closed pipes: fundamental wavelength λ = 4L; open pipes: λ = 2L
- Harmonics: closed pipe has only odd harmonics; open pipe has all harmonics
- Doppler effect: apparent change in frequency when source or observer moves; ambulance siren pitch rises as it approaches and falls as it passes

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Calculate the depth of a well by shouting and timing the echo; compare with a measured value
2. Investigate resonance in a closed air column (resonance tube): find the resonant length and calculate speed of sound
3. Record the frequency of a tuning fork as it moves toward and away from a microphone to demonstrate the Doppler effect

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests echo calculations (distance = speed × time / 2), resonance in pipes, and speed-temperature relationship
> JAMB tests sound properties, Doppler effect (conceptual), and speed of sound in different media
> Trap: forgetting to halve time in echo problems (sound travels to object and back); confusing closed and open pipe harmonic relationships

## Electricity and Magnetism

### Electric Circuits — Ohm's Law, Resistance and Power
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- State and apply Ohm's Law to simple and complex circuits
- Calculate equivalent resistance for series and parallel circuits
- Apply Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws
- Calculate electrical power and energy
- Describe domestic electrical installations and safety devices

#### 📖 Notes
- Electric current: I = Q/t; unit: ampere (A); flow of charge (electrons in conductors)
- Potential difference (voltage): V = W/Q; unit: volt (V); energy per unit charge
- Ohm's Law: V = IR (for ohmic conductors at constant temperature)
- Resistance R: unit: ohm (Ω); R = ρL/A (resistivity equation)
- Series circuits: I same throughout; V = V₁ + V₂ + …; R_total = R₁ + R₂ + …
- Parallel circuits: V same across each branch; I = I₁ + I₂ + …; 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
- Kirchhoff's Voltage Law: sum of EMF in loop = sum of potential drops (ΣV = 0 around loop)
- Kirchhoff's Current Law: sum of currents entering junction = sum leaving (ΣI_in = ΣI_out)
- Internal resistance: E = I(R + r); terminal voltage = E − Ir
- Electrical power: P = IV = I²R = V²/R; unit: watt (W)
- Electrical energy: E = Pt = IVt; unit: joule (J) or kilowatt-hour (kWh)
- Domestic wiring: live (brown), neutral (blue), earth (green/yellow); fuses, circuit breakers, residual current devices (RCDs)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Build series and parallel circuits using resistors; measure V and I at different points; verify Ohm's law and calculate equivalent resistance
2. Solve a complex circuit with two batteries and three resistors using Kirchhoff's laws
3. Calculate monthly electricity cost for a home with a 100W bulb (8h/day), 2000W AC (4h/day), and 50W fan (12h/day) at ₦50/kWh

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests Ohm's law, series/parallel equivalent resistance, internal resistance and power calculations — draw circuit diagrams before solving
> JAMB tests quick resistance combination, Kirchhoff's laws, and domestic safety — know the 2-resistor parallel formula: R = R₁R₂/(R₁+R₂)
> Trap: in parallel circuits, total resistance is LESS than the smallest individual resistance; if your answer is larger, recheck

### Electromagnetic Induction and Transformers
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-003-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- State Faraday's and Lenz's laws of electromagnetic induction
- Describe how an AC generator and DC generator work
- Explain how a transformer works and calculate turns ratio
- Distinguish between AC and DC and describe AC wave characteristics
- Explain energy losses in transformers and methods to reduce them

#### 📖 Notes
- Electromagnetic induction: EMF is induced when flux through a conductor changes
- Faraday's Law: induced EMF ∝ rate of change of magnetic flux; ε = −dΦ/dt
- Lenz's Law: induced current opposes the change causing it (negative sign in Faraday's law)
- Magnetic flux: Φ = BA cosθ; unit: weber (Wb)
- AC generator: rotating coil in magnetic field; slip rings maintain continuous rotation; EMF = NBAω sin(ωt)
- DC generator: same but uses commutator and brushes to produce unidirectional current
- Transformer: changes AC voltage; uses mutual induction between primary and secondary coils
- Turns ratio: Ns/Np = Vs/Vp = Ip/Is (for ideal transformer)
- Step-up transformer: Ns > Np → Vs > Vp (used in power transmission to reduce current and I²R losses)
- Step-down transformer: Ns < Np → Vs < Vp (used at homes to reduce voltage to safe levels)
- Power loss in transmission: P_loss = I²R; reducing I (by increasing V) reduces losses
- Transformer losses: eddy currents (reduced by laminating core), hysteresis, copper losses (I²R in windings), flux leakage

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Connect a coil to a galvanometer and move a bar magnet in and out; observe deflection and identify Faraday's and Lenz's laws
2. Build a simple transformer with two coils; measure primary and secondary voltages; verify the turns ratio
3. Calculate: a step-up transformer has 500 primary turns and 5000 secondary turns; if primary voltage is 240V and efficiency is 80%, find secondary voltage and power delivered

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests transformer turns ratio, AC generator components (slip rings vs. commutator), Lenz's law direction and energy losses
> JAMB tests transformer calculations (Ns/Np = Vs/Vp), step-up vs. step-down identification, and Faraday's law conceptual questions
> Trap: for ideal transformer only, Vs/Vp = Ns/Np; when efficiency is given, power_out = efficiency × power_in — do not assume ideal unless stated

## Thermal Physics

### Temperature, Heat and Gas Laws
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-004-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Distinguish between temperature and heat
- Describe methods of heat transfer (conduction, convection, radiation)
- Apply specific heat capacity and latent heat in calculations
- State and apply Boyle's, Charles' and the combined gas law
- Explain thermal expansion of solids, liquids and gases

#### 📖 Notes
- Temperature: degree of hotness; measure of average kinetic energy of particles; SI unit: kelvin (K); K = °C + 273
- Heat: thermal energy transferred from hot to cold body; measured in joules
- Specific heat capacity: Q = mcΔT; c = heat required per kg per °C; unit: J/(kg·K)
- Latent heat: Q = mL; energy required to change state at constant temperature
- Latent heat of fusion (Lf): solid → liquid (e.g. ice at 0°C, Lf = 336,000 J/kg)
- Latent heat of vaporisation (Lv): liquid → gas (e.g. water at 100°C, Lv = 2,260,000 J/kg)
- Conduction: heat transfer through direct particle collision; metals are good conductors
- Convection: heat transfer by movement of fluid (liquid or gas) due to density differences
- Radiation: heat transfer by electromagnetic (infrared) waves; no medium needed; dark/matt surfaces absorb/emit best
- Boyle's Law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (at constant temperature); P ∝ 1/V
- Charles' Law: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (at constant pressure); V ∝ T (in Kelvin!)
- Combined Gas Law: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂; Ideal Gas Law: PV = nRT

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Measure the specific heat capacity of water by heating a known mass and recording temperature change; compare with textbook value 4200 J/(kg·K)
2. Demonstrate convection using potassium permanganate crystals in a beaker of water on a tripod: observe coloured water rising then falling
3. Investigate Boyle's Law using a syringe: seal the end, push the plunger in/out, measure volume at different forces, plot P vs. 1/V

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests Q = mcΔT, Q = mL, gas law calculations and heat transfer mode identification
> JAMB tests gas law substitutions, Kelvin conversion, and thermal expansion — ALWAYS convert Celsius to Kelvin before using gas laws
> Trap: using °C instead of K in gas laws gives wrong answers — conversion K = °C + 273 is non-negotiable

## Modern Physics

### Radioactivity and Nuclear Physics
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHY-005-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the structure of the atom and the nucleus
- Identify the three types of nuclear radiation and their properties
- Apply radioactive decay equations and calculate half-life
- Distinguish between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion
- Assess the benefits and hazards of radioactivity

#### 📖 Notes
- Atomic structure: nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electrons in shells
- Proton number (Z): number of protons; determines element
- Nucleon number (A): total protons + neutrons; mass number
- Nuclide notation: ᴬ_Z X
- Isotopes: same proton number, different neutron number (e.g. ¹²C and ¹⁴C)
- Alpha (α) radiation: ⁴He nucleus (2p + 2n); strongly ionising; stopped by paper; short range in air
- Beta (β) radiation: high-speed electron from nucleus (neutron → proton + electron + antineutrino); penetrates paper; stopped by 3mm aluminium
- Gamma (γ) radiation: electromagnetic wave from nucleus; least ionising; stopped by thick lead or concrete
- Radioactive decay equation: conservation of mass number and proton number
- Half-life (t½): time for activity/amount to halve; N = N₀(½)^(t/t½)
- Nuclear fission: heavy nucleus splits into smaller nuclei + neutrons + energy (nuclear power stations; atomic bomb)
- Nuclear fusion: light nuclei combine to form heavier nucleus + energy (sun's energy source; hydrogen bomb)
- Hazards: ionisation damages cells/DNA, causes cancer; safe handling: distance, shielding, time limitation
- Applications: carbon-14 dating, medical imaging (PET, gamma camera), cancer radiotherapy, sterilisation

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Calculate the amount of a 200g radioactive sample remaining after 3 half-lives (answer: 25g); repeat for 4 and 5 half-lives
2. Draw decay series for uranium-238 → lead-206, showing each alpha and beta emission
3. Debate: 'Nigeria should build nuclear power plants to solve its electricity crisis' — students research and present pros and cons

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests half-life calculations, decay equations (balancing A and Z), properties of α/β/γ, and nuclear fission vs. fusion
> JAMB tests half-life, nuclide notation, penetrating ability of radiations, and isotope identification
> Trap: in β decay, A stays the same but Z increases by 1 (neutron becomes proton); in α decay, A decreases by 4 and Z decreases by 2
