# TOPIC 1: The State and Sovereignty
**Topic ID:** SSS-GOV-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of the State

A **state** is a politically organised community occupying a definite territory, with a permanent population, an effective government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states.

---

## 1.2 Elements of a State

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Population** | A definite number of people who inhabit the territory |
| **Territory** | A defined geographical area with land, air, and water boundaries |
| **Government** | A body with authority to make and enforce laws |
| **Sovereignty** | Supreme and independent authority; the highest power |

---

## 1.3 Sovereignty

**Sovereignty** is the supreme, absolute, and inalienable authority of a state to govern itself and conduct its affairs without interference.

### Types of Sovereignty:

| Type | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Internal sovereignty** | Supreme authority within the state's territory (over all persons and groups) |
| **External sovereignty** | Independence from foreign control; equal status with other states in international law |
| **Legal sovereignty** | Formal/constitutional authority held by parliament or the constitution |
| **Political sovereignty** | Actual authority exercised in practice (may be the electorate, a ruling party, or the military) |
| **De jure sovereignty** | Recognised by law |
| **De facto sovereignty** | Exercised in practice (may lack legal recognition, e.g., a coup government) |

---

## 1.4 Government

**Government** is the body or organisation set up to manage the affairs of a state. It operates through three arms:

| Arm | Function | Nigerian equivalent |
|-----|---------|-------------------|
| **Legislature** | Makes laws | National Assembly (Senate + House of Representatives) |
| **Executive** | Implements laws; governs | President, Federal Executive Council |
| **Judiciary** | Interprets laws; resolves disputes | Supreme Court, Federal High Court |

**Separation of powers:** Doctrine that the three arms of government should be independent of one another (Montesquieu).

**Checks and balances:** Each arm limits the powers of the other two to prevent abuse.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Identify and explain the four essential elements of a state.

**Solution:**
1. **Population** — A state must have a definite group of people permanently residing within its territory. There is no minimum number required.
2. **Territory** — A state must have a defined geographical area, including land, internal waters, and airspace, bounded by recognised borders.
3. **Government** — An organised body with authority to make and enforce laws, maintain order, and represent the state internally and externally.
4. **Sovereignty** — The state must possess supreme authority within its territory and independence from foreign control.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests the four elements of a state, types of sovereignty, and the three arms of government.

> **JAMB** tests definitions and distinctions between de jure and de facto sovereignty, and types of government.

> **Common trap:** A government is NOT the same as a state. A government can change; the state continues. Nigeria as a state existed before and after every change of government.

# TOPIC 2: The Nigerian Constitution
**Topic ID:** SSS-GOV-002-01

## 2.1 Definition of a Constitution

A **constitution** is the fundamental law of a state — a body of rules and principles that define the structure and powers of government, the rights and duties of citizens, and the relationship between government and the governed.

---

## 2.2 Types of Constitution

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Written** | Codified in a single document | Nigeria, USA |
| **Unwritten** | No single document; based on customs, conventions, court decisions | UK |
| **Rigid** | Difficult to amend; requires special procedure | USA, Nigeria |
| **Flexible** | Can be amended by ordinary legislative process | UK |
| **Federal** | Power divided between central and state governments | Nigeria, USA |
| **Unitary** | Power concentrated in central government | UK, France |

---

## 2.3 Constitutional Development in Nigeria

| Constitution | Year | Key Features |
|-------------|------|-------------|
| Clifford Constitution | 1922 | First elected legislature in Nigeria; Lagos and Calabar franchise |
| Richards Constitution | 1946 | Regional councils; greater Nigerian participation |
| Macpherson Constitution | 1951 | Wider franchise; regional assemblies |
| Lyttleton Constitution | 1954 | True federalism introduced; three regions |
| Independence Constitution | 1960 | Nigeria independent; Westminster model |
| Republican Constitution | 1963 | Nigeria became a republic; President as head of state |
| 1979 Constitution | 1979 | Presidential system introduced; returned to civilian rule |
| 1999 Constitution | 1999 | Current constitution; Federal Republic with presidential system |

---

## 2.4 Features of the 1999 Nigerian Constitution

1. **Federal system** — Power shared between Federal, State, and Local governments
2. **Presidential system** — President is both Head of State and Head of Government
3. **Separation of powers** — Three arms: Legislature, Executive, Judiciary
4. **Fundamental human rights** (Chapter IV) — Right to life, dignity, liberty, fair hearing, privacy, freedom of expression, etc.
5. **Bicameral legislature** — Senate (109 senators) + House of Representatives (360 members)
6. **Independent judiciary** — Supreme Court at the apex
7. **Exclusive, Concurrent, and Residual legislative lists**

---

## 2.5 Amendment Procedure

The 1999 Nigerian Constitution can be amended by:
- Two-thirds majority in both the Senate and House of Representatives
- Approval by at least two-thirds of the 36 State Houses of Assembly

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** What is a constitution? Distinguish between a rigid and a flexible constitution.

**Solution:**
A **constitution** is the supreme law of a state that defines the structure of government, distributes powers, and guarantees citizens' rights.

A **rigid constitution** requires a special, more difficult procedure to amend than ordinary legislation — for example, a two-thirds supermajority in the legislature and ratification by state assemblies (as in Nigeria and the USA). A **flexible constitution** can be amended by the same process as ordinary laws — a simple majority in parliament (as in the UK). Rigid constitutions offer more stability; flexible constitutions are more adaptable.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests the types of constitution, key features of the 1999 constitution, and constitutional development milestones.

> **Common trap:** Nigeria has a **written and rigid** constitution. The 1999 constitution (as amended) is the current operative one.

# TOPIC 3: Political Parties and Electoral Process
**Topic ID:** SSS-GOV-002-02

## 3.1 Political Parties

A **political party** is an organised group of people with similar political views and goals who seek to win elections and control government in order to implement their policies.

### Functions of Political Parties:
1. Political education of citizens
2. Recruitment and training of political leaders
3. Aggregation and articulation of public interests
4. Forming government (if they win elections)
5. Opposition and accountability (if they lose)
6. Linking citizens to government

---

## 3.2 Types of Party Systems

| System | Description | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **One-party system** | Only one party allowed | China (CCP) |
| **Two-party system** | Two dominant parties | USA (Democrats vs Republicans) |
| **Multi-party system** | Many parties; coalitions common | Nigeria, Germany |

---

## 3.3 Electoral Systems

**Election** is the process by which citizens choose their representatives through voting.

| System | Description | Use in Nigeria |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **First Past the Post (FPTP)** | Candidate with most votes wins | Presidential elections |
| **Proportional representation** | Seats allocated proportionally to votes | Not used in Nigeria |
| **Two-round system** | Second round if no majority in first | Some presidential elections |

**Nigerian electoral requirement (Presidential):** A candidate must:
- Win a majority (more than 50%) AND
- Get at least 25% of votes in two-thirds of states (i.e., 24 states + FCT)

---

## 3.4 INEC and Electoral Administration

**INEC (Independent National Electoral Commission)** is the body responsible for conducting elections in Nigeria.

**Functions of INEC:**
- Registering voters
- Registering political parties
- Conducting elections
- Announcing election results
- Supervising political campaigns

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State FOUR functions of political parties in a democratic state.

**Solution:**
1. **Political recruitment** — Parties identify, train, and present qualified candidates for public office
2. **Political education** — Parties educate citizens about government policies, national issues, and political rights
3. **Interest aggregation** — Parties collect and combine the diverse views of various groups into coherent policy positions
4. **Government formation** — The winning party forms a government and implements its manifesto

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests functions of political parties, types of party systems, and INEC's role.

> **Common trap:** INEC conducts FEDERAL and STATE elections. Local Government elections are conducted by State Independent Electoral Commissions (SIECs), NOT INEC.

# TOPIC 4: Foreign Policy and International Organisations
**Topic ID:** SSS-GOV-003-01

## 4.1 Foreign Policy

**Foreign policy** is the set of principles, objectives, and strategies that guide a state's relations with other states and international organisations.

### Objectives of Nigerian Foreign Policy:
1. Protection of Nigeria's national interest
2. Promotion of African unity and solidarity (Africa as centrepiece of Nigeria's foreign policy)
3. Promotion of world peace and security
4. Promotion of respect for international law
5. Promotion of economic development

**"Africa as centrepiece"** of Nigeria's foreign policy = Nigeria prioritises African issues above all others.

---

## 4.2 The United Nations (UN)

Founded: 1945 (after World War II) | Headquarters: New York, USA
Current members: 193 member states

| Principal Organ | Function |
|----------------|---------|
| **General Assembly** | All member states; debates issues; one state one vote |
| **Security Council** | 15 members (5 permanent, 10 rotating); primary responsibility for peace and security; can authorise force |
| **Secretariat** | Administrative arm; headed by the Secretary-General |
| **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** | Judicial arm; settles disputes between states |
| **Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)** | Promotes economic and social development |
| **Trusteeship Council** | Now largely inactive |

**Permanent 5 (P5) with veto power:** USA, UK, France, Russia, China

---

## 4.3 African Union (AU)

**African Union** replaced the Organisation of African Unity (OAU) in 2002.
Headquarters: Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.

**Objectives:**
- Achieve greater unity among African states
- Promote peace, security, and stability
- Promote economic integration
- Encourage good governance and democracy

---

## 4.4 ECOWAS

**Economic Community of West African States** founded in 1975 (Lagos).
Members: 15 West African states including Nigeria.

**Objectives:** Free trade, common external tariff, free movement of persons, regional peace and security (ECOMOG — military arm).

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State FOUR objectives of Nigerian foreign policy.

**Solution:**
1. Protection of Nigeria's national interest in international affairs
2. Africa as the centrepiece — Nigeria gives priority to promoting African unity and development
3. Promotion of world peace and security through participation in UN peacekeeping missions
4. Non-alignment — Nigeria maintains independence from Cold War power blocs (though this is less relevant post-Cold War)

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests UN structure, ECOWAS objectives, AU functions, and the principles of Nigerian foreign policy.

> **Common trap:** The Security Council has FIVE permanent members (not all 15 are permanent). Only the 5 permanent members have veto power.

# TOPIC 5: Citizenship, Human Rights and Rule of Law
**Topic ID:** SSS-GOV-004-01

## 5.1 Citizenship

**Citizenship** is the legal status of a person as a member of a state, with rights and responsibilities.

### Methods of Acquiring Nigerian Citizenship:

| Method | Provision |
|--------|---------|
| **By birth** | Born in Nigeria to at least one Nigerian parent |
| **By registration** | Married to a Nigerian; apply for registration |
| **By naturalisation** | 15 years of continuous lawful residence; renounce other citizenship |

**Duties of a citizen:**
- Obey the law
- Pay taxes
- Participate in elections
- Defend the state when called upon
- Preserve national resources

---

## 5.2 Fundamental Human Rights (1999 Constitution, Chapter IV)

| Right | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Right to life** | No person shall be deprived of life intentionally (except by court sentence) |
| **Right to dignity** | No torture, slavery, or inhuman treatment |
| **Right to personal liberty** | No unlawful arrest or detention |
| **Right to fair hearing** | Right to a fair and public trial |
| **Right to private and family life** | Privacy of home, correspondence |
| **Freedom of thought, conscience and religion** | Right to hold any belief |
| **Freedom of expression and press** | Right to speak, write, and publish freely |
| **Freedom of peaceful assembly and association** | Right to form and join groups/parties |
| **Freedom of movement** | Right to move freely within Nigeria |
| **Freedom from discrimination** | No discrimination on grounds of sex, religion, ethnicity |
| **Right to property** | No arbitrary seizure of property |

---

## 5.3 Rule of Law

**Rule of law** (A.V. Dicey, 1885) means that the law is supreme — it governs both citizens AND government officials. No one is above the law.

**Three components (Dicey):**
1. **Supremacy of law** — No person or government is above the law; everyone is subject to the ordinary law of the land
2. **Equality before the law** — All persons are equal before the law regardless of status, wealth, or position
3. **Dominance of legal spirit** — The constitution is a product of rights confirmed by courts; rights come from court decisions, not just written text

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Explain what is meant by "rule of law" and state THREE of its features.

**Solution:**
**Rule of law** is the principle that all persons — citizens and government officials alike — are subject to and equal before the law; no one is exempt from it.

Features:
1. **Supremacy of law** — The law is above every individual or institution; the constitution is supreme
2. **Equality before the law** — Rich and poor, powerful and ordinary citizens, are treated equally by courts
3. **Legal protection of individual rights** — Citizens' rights are protected by independent courts; any violation can be challenged in court

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests Dicey's three components of rule of law, the fundamental rights in Chapter IV, and methods of citizenship acquisition.

> **Common trap:** "Rule of law" does NOT mean "rule by law" (where rulers make laws and use them to stay in power). Rule of law means the law itself is supreme and applies to ALL, including those in power.

> In WAEC essays on human rights, cite specific Chapter IV sections and give real examples of violations and how they can be challenged in court.
