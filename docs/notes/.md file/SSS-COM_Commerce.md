## Trade and Commercial Activities

### Trade — Types, Documents and Terms
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-COM-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define trade and distinguish between home and foreign trade
- Identify and explain all trade documents used in buying and selling
- Explain trade terms (FOB, CIF, C&F) and their implications
- Describe the channels of distribution from producer to consumer
- Evaluate the roles of middlemen in trade

#### 📖 Notes
- Trade: buying and selling of goods and services for profit; creates utility (form, place, time, possession)
- Home (domestic) trade: within one country; retail (directly to consumer) and wholesale (to retailers in large quantities)
- Foreign (international) trade: between countries; exports (sell abroad) and imports (buy from abroad)
- Balance of trade: value of exports minus value of imports; favourable (surplus) or unfavourable (deficit)
- Trade documents: enquiry (buyer asks for price information), quotation (seller's reply with prices/terms), order (buyer requests goods), invoice (seller's bill showing quantity, price, total), credit note (refund due to returns), debit note (additional charge), receipt (proof of payment), bill of lading (shipping document), warehouse warrant
- Terms of trade: FOB (Free on Board — seller pays until goods on ship), CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight — seller pays all until destination port), C&F or CFR (Cost and Freight only, no insurance)
- Trade terms for payment: prompt payment, cash on delivery (COD), credit terms (30/60/90 days)
- Channels of distribution: manufacturer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer (traditional); direct selling; online
- Wholesaler functions: buying in bulk, breaking bulk, storage, risk bearing, credit to retailers, market information
- Retailer functions: final link to consumer, display, credit to customers, after-sales service, small quantities
- Types of retail outlets: departmental stores, supermarkets, multiple shops (chain stores), cooperative shops, market stalls, kiosks
- Elimination of middlemen: advantages (lower prices) and disadvantages (manufacturer takes on wholesaler/retailer functions)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Trace the journey of a bag of rice from the Rice Farmers' Association in Benue to a Lagos market woman: identify each document exchanged at each stage
2. Collect and analyse real or sample trade documents (invoice, credit note, delivery note); identify all key information on each
3. Debate: 'Middlemen should be eliminated from the distribution chain in Nigeria' — argue both sides using real market examples

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests document identification, trade terms (FOB vs. CIF), channels of distribution, and functions of wholesalers/retailers
> JAMB tests document purpose matching, balance of trade calculation, and retail outlet identification
> Trap: confusing invoice (unpaid bill from seller) with receipt (proof of payment by buyer) — different documents, different timing

### Aids to Trade — Banking, Insurance, Transport, Warehousing, Communication
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-COM-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify the main aids to trade and explain how each facilitates commerce
- Describe types of banks and banking services
- Explain principles and types of insurance
- Describe types of transport and their suitability for different goods
- Explain the role of warehousing and communication in trade

#### 📖 Notes
- Aids to trade: banking, insurance, transportation, warehousing, communication, advertising — they remove the barriers to trade
- Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN): regulates banking sector; controls money supply; lender of last resort; issues currency; manages foreign exchange
- Commercial banks: accept deposits; grant loans; operate current, savings and fixed deposit accounts; issue cheques
- Services: current account (cheques, no interest), savings account (interest, limited withdrawals), fixed deposit (higher interest, fixed term)
- Insurance: protection against financial loss in exchange for a premium; types: life, fire, marine, motor, employer's liability, accident, burglary
- Principles of insurance: insurable interest, utmost good faith (uberrimae fidei), indemnity, subrogation, contribution, proximate cause, salvage
- Transport: road (flexible, door-to-door, best for perishables), rail (heavy goods, long distance, reliable), air (fastest, expensive, perishables, valuables), sea/water (heavy/bulky goods, cheapest per ton, slow), pipeline (oil and gas only)
- Warehousing: bonded warehouse (imported goods before duty paid), cold storage (perishables), general warehouse, private warehouse
- Functions of warehouse: storage, bulk-breaking, blending, conditioning, financing (as security for loans)
- Communication in trade: GSM, internet (e-commerce), postal services, radio/TV advertising
- E-commerce: buying and selling online; platforms like Jumia, Konga in Nigeria; advantages (24/7, wide reach) and disadvantages (fraud risk, logistics)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Visit (or simulate) a bank: identify services offered on a brochure; complete a sample deposit slip, withdrawal form and cheque
2. Role-play: an importer files a marine insurance claim after goods were damaged at sea — what principles apply and what documents are needed?
3. Compare the cost and time of sending 1 tonne of goods from Lagos to Kano by road, rail and air; recommend a mode for fresh tomatoes vs. iron rods

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests all 6 principles of insurance, types of bank accounts, modes of transport with their suitability, and warehouse types
> JAMB tests CBN functions, insurance principle identification from scenarios, and transport mode matching
> Trap: the principle of INDEMNITY does not apply to life insurance (you cannot be compensated for a life in monetary terms — life policies are valued, not indemnity policies)

## Business Organization and Management

### Forms of Business Organization
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-COM-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify and compare the main forms of business organization
- Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of each form
- Explain the process of forming a company (Memorandum, Articles of Association)
- Distinguish between public and private limited companies
- Explain the role of public enterprises in Nigeria

#### 📖 Notes
- Sole proprietorship: owned and managed by one person; unlimited liability; easy to set up; all profit to owner; limited capital
- Partnership: 2–20 persons (banking: 2–10); governed by Partnership Act; unlimited liability (unless limited partnership); Deed of Partnership
- Limited liability company: separate legal entity; limited liability; can sue and be sued; minimum 2 directors
- Private limited company (Ltd): shares not publicly traded; minimum 1 shareholder; maximum 50 (Companies Act Nigeria)
- Public limited company (Plc): shares on stock exchange; wider ownership; more regulation; prospectus required
- Formation documents: Memorandum of Association (objects, name, capital, liability — external); Articles of Association (internal rules — meetings, directors)
- Cooperative society: owned and managed by members for mutual benefit; UNICOOP; retail, farming, thrift
- Public enterprises (parastatals): owned by government; provide essential services; NNPC, NEPA/PHCN, NIPOST
- Privatisation and commercialisation: government selling/commercialising parastatals for efficiency
- Joint venture: two or more companies combining resources for a specific project
- Franchise: franchise gives a franchisee right to use its brand and system (e.g. MTN franchises)
- Multinational corporations (MNCs): operating in multiple countries; e.g. Shell, Unilever, Dangote

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Using the CAC (Corporate Affairs Commission) website, identify what documents are needed to register a business in Nigeria as a sole trader vs. a limited company
2. Case study: compare Dangote Group (Plc) and a local market trader (sole proprietor) on 5 criteria: capital, liability, control, continuity, regulation
3. Group activity: form a cooperative society in class — draft a simple constitution, elect officers, collect ₦50 dues per member, plan how to use the fund

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests comparing business forms on specific criteria (liability, management, capital), company formation documents, and public enterprise examples in Nigeria
> JAMB tests definition-matching, Memorandum vs. Articles of Association distinction, and advantages/disadvantages identification
> Trap: Memorandum of Association deals with EXTERNAL matters (name, objects, capital); Articles deals with INTERNAL management — students frequently reverse these
