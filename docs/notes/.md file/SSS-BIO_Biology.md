## Cell Biology and Genetics

### Cell Structure, Function and Organization
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-BIO-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the ultrastructure of plant and animal cells as seen under an electron microscope
- State the functions of all major cell organelles
- Distinguish between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
- Explain the levels of organization from cell to organism
- Describe osmosis, diffusion and active transport across cell membranes

#### 📖 Notes
- Cell theory: all organisms are made of cells; cells are the basic unit of life; cells arise from pre-existing cells
- Cell membrane (plasma membrane): phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins; semi-permeable; controls entry/exit
- Nucleus: contains DNA (genetic material); nucleolus produces rRNA; nuclear membrane (double); controls cell activities
- Mitochondria: double-membrane; site of aerobic respiration; produces ATP; called 'powerhouse of the cell'
- Chloroplast: in plant cells only; contains chlorophyll; site of photosynthesis; has grana and stroma
- Endoplasmic reticulum: rough (ribosomes — protein synthesis) and smooth (lipid synthesis, detoxification)
- Golgi apparatus: processes and packages proteins; forms lysosomes; secretion vesicles
- Lysosomes: contain digestive enzymes; autolysis; digest old organelles and foreign particles
- Vacuole: large central vacuole in plants (water storage, turgor pressure); small in animal cells
- Cell wall: in plants only; cellulose; provides rigidity; fully permeable
- Prokaryotic cells: no membrane-bound nucleus; no membrane-bound organelles; naked circular DNA; smaller ribosomes (70S)
- Levels of organization: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism
- Osmosis: movement of water through semi-permeable membrane from high to low water potential (hypotonic → hypertonic)
- Diffusion: net movement of molecules from high to low concentration down concentration gradient; passive
- Active transport: movement AGAINST concentration gradient; requires ATP and carrier proteins

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Prepare, stain and observe cheek cell (animal) and onion epidermis (plant) under microscope; draw and label both
2. Investigate osmosis: place potato cylinders in distilled water, 0.5M and 1M sucrose solutions; measure mass change after 30 minutes
3. Examine and draw electron micrographs of a mitochondrion and chloroplast; identify all labelled structures

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC practical requires drawing and labelling cells from microscope slides — use pencil, clear lines, no colouring, scale bar if asked
> JAMB tests organelle-function matching, prokaryote vs. eukaryote differences, and osmosis/diffusion definitions
> Trap: confusing osmosis (water only, through semi-permeable membrane) with diffusion (any substance, no membrane required)

### Genetics — Inheritance and Variation
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-BIO-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- State Mendel's laws of inheritance
- Solve monohybrid and dihybrid cross problems using Punnett squares
- Explain sex determination and sex-linked inheritance
- Distinguish between genotype and phenotype, dominance, recessiveness and codominance
- Describe mutations and their causes

#### 📖 Notes
- Mendel's Law of Segregation: alleles separate during gamete formation; each gamete receives one allele per gene locus
- Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment: genes on different chromosomes sort independently during gamete formation
- Terminology: gene, allele, locus, dominant, recessive, codominant, homozygous (pure breeding), heterozygous (hybrid)
- Genotype vs. phenotype: genotype = genetic makeup (e.g. Bb); phenotype = observed characteristic (e.g. brown eyes)
- Monohybrid cross: Punnett square for one gene pair; F1 from PP × pp = all Pp; F2 3:1 ratio
- Test cross: unknown phenotype × homozygous recessive; 1:1 ratio = heterozygous; all dominant = homozygous
- Dihybrid cross: two gene pairs; 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio in F2
- Sex determination in humans: XX (female), XY (male); Y chromosome carries SRY gene
- Sex-linked traits: genes on X chromosome (haemophilia, colour blindness); more common in males (only one X)
- Codominance: both alleles expressed simultaneously (e.g. ABO blood groups: IA and IB codominant; IO recessive)
- Blood groups: genotypes IA IA or IA IO = group A; IB IB or IB IO = group B; IA IB = group AB; IO IO = group O
- Mutation: permanent heritable change in DNA; gene mutation (base substitution, insertion, deletion) vs. chromosomal mutation
- Down syndrome: trisomy 21 (chromosomal); sickle cell anaemia (gene mutation, base substitution in β-globin gene)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Solve: a tall (TT) plant is crossed with a dwarf (tt) plant — show F1 and F2 using Punnett squares; find phenotypic ratios
2. Solve a sex-linked problem: a carrier female (XᴴX) marries a normal male (XᴴY) — find probability of haemophiliac son
3. Construct a class blood group survey; calculate allele frequencies and compare with Hardy-Weinberg expectation

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests Punnett squares, genotypic and phenotypic ratios, blood groups and sex-linked inheritance — always define symbols at the start
> JAMB tests rapid recognition of cross ratios, blood group compatibility, and mutation types
> Trap: students confuse genotypic ratio with phenotypic ratio — in a monohybrid F2, genotypic ratio is 1:2:1 but phenotypic ratio is 3:1

## Nutrition and Life Processes

### Nutrition and Digestion in Humans
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-BIO-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify the six classes of food and their specific functions and sources
- Describe the human digestive system and the digestion of each food class
- Explain the role of enzymes in digestion
- Describe absorption and assimilation of digested food
- Identify nutritional deficiency diseases common in Nigeria

#### 📖 Notes
- Six food classes: carbohydrates (energy; e.g. starch, glucose, cellulose), proteins (growth and repair; e.g. meat, beans), fats/lipids (energy store, insulation; oils, butter), vitamins (regulatory; A, B, C, D, E, K), minerals (structural and regulatory; Ca, Fe, I, Na), water (solvent, cooling, transport)
- Carbohydrate digestion: salivary amylase (mouth) → maltose; pancreatic amylase → maltose; maltase (small intestine) → glucose
- Protein digestion: pepsin (stomach, acid pH) → peptides; trypsin/chymotrypsin (pancreas) → peptides; peptidases (small intestine) → amino acids
- Fat digestion: bile emulsifies fat → droplets; pancreatic lipase → fatty acids + glycerol
- Alimentary canal: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → large intestine → rectum → anus
- Digestion in stomach: HCl creates pH 2; pepsin activated; churning (mechanical digestion)
- Digestion in small intestine: bile from liver/gallbladder; pancreatic juice (amylase, lipase, trypsin); intestinal enzymes
- Absorption: villi and microvilli increase surface area of small intestine; glucose and amino acids into blood; fatty acids and glycerol into lacteals (lymph)
- Liver functions: produces bile, deaminates amino acids, stores glycogen, detoxifies, produces blood proteins
- Nutritional deficiency diseases: kwashiorkor (protein), marasmus (protein+calories), scurvy (Vit C), rickets (Vit D/Ca), anaemia (Fe/Vit B12), night blindness (Vit A), goitre (iodine)
- BMI: Body Mass Index = weight(kg) / height²(m²); underweight <18.5, normal 18.5–24.9, overweight 25–29.9, obese ≥30
- Balanced diet: adequate proportions of all food classes; varies with age, sex, activity level

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Perform food tests: Benedict's test for reducing sugars, Biuret test for proteins, iodine test for starch, ethanol emulsion test for lipids
2. Dissect a model of the human digestive system or examine a labelled diagram; trace the path of a bread molecule from mouth to blood
3. Survey classmates' diets for one week; assess for deficiencies; design a balanced Nigerian diet using local foods

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC practical tests food tests and enzyme action experiments — know exact colour changes: Benedict's (blue → brick red), Biuret (blue → purple), iodine (yellow-brown → blue-black)
> JAMB tests enzyme-organ matching, deficiency disease causes, and absorption routes (blood vs. lymph)
> Trap: confusing mechanical digestion (physical breakdown by teeth and churning) with chemical digestion (enzymatic breakdown)

## Ecology and Environment

### Ecology — Ecosystems, Food Webs and Population
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-BIO-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define key ecological terms (habitat, niche, population, community, ecosystem, biome)
- Construct and interpret food chains, food webs and ecological pyramids
- Explain energy flow and nutrient cycling in ecosystems
- Describe factors affecting population size
- Explain ecological balance, biodiversity and conservation

#### 📖 Notes
- Habitat: place where an organism lives; niche: role of organism in ecosystem (what it eats, where it lives, when active)
- Population: all individuals of one species in an area; community: all populations in an area; ecosystem: community + abiotic environment
- Biotic factors: living components (predation, competition, parasitism, mutualism, commensalism)
- Abiotic factors: non-living (temperature, light, humidity, pH, wind, soil type)
- Food chain: unidirectional flow of energy (producer → primary consumer → secondary → tertiary consumer)
- Food web: network of interconnected food chains in a community
- Trophic levels: producers (autotrophs — plants), primary consumers (herbivores), secondary consumers (carnivores/omnivores), decomposers
- Energy flow: 10% rule — only ~10% of energy passed to next trophic level; rest lost as heat, excretion, respiration
- Pyramid of numbers: number of organisms at each trophic level
- Pyramid of energy: energy content at each level — always tapered upward
- Carbon cycle: photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion, sedimentation
- Nitrogen cycle: nitrogen fixation (Rhizobium), nitrification, ammonification, denitrification
- Population growth: biotic potential (r) vs. environmental resistance; logistic (S-curve) vs. exponential (J-curve) growth
- Conservation: biodiversity hotspots in Nigeria (Okomu forest, Cross River NP); CITES, IUCN Red List; deforestation, desertification threats

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Survey the ecosystem of the school compound: identify producers, consumers and decomposers; draw a food web with at least 10 organisms
2. Model population growth with bean seeds as organisms: start with 10, double each 'generation', graph the J-curve, then add a carrying capacity to show S-curve
3. Debate: 'Logging in Cross River National Park should be permitted for economic development' — use ecological arguments

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests food web construction and interpretation, energy flow calculations (10% rule), and nutrient cycle diagrams
> JAMB tests definitions, pyramid types, and population growth curve interpretation
> Trap: pyramid of numbers is NOT always pyramid-shaped (parasites can invert it) — pyramid of ENERGY is always pyramidal
