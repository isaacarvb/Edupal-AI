# TOPIC 1: Introduction to Marketing
**Topic ID:** SSS-MKT-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Marketing

**Marketing** is the business activity that involves identifying, anticipating, and satisfying consumer needs and wants profitably. It covers all activities from product creation to getting goods into the hands of the final consumer.

**Modern marketing concept:** Start with the CUSTOMER (identify needs first), then create products to satisfy those needs — as opposed to the old selling concept where products were made first and customers convinced to buy.

---

## 1.2 The Marketing Mix — The 4 Ps

The **marketing mix** is the combination of strategies a firm uses to achieve its marketing objectives.

| Element | Definition | Key decisions |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| **Product** | The good or service being offered | Design, quality, brand, packaging, warranty |
| **Price** | The amount charged for the product | Pricing strategy (cost-plus, competitive, skimming, penetration) |
| **Place** | How and where the product reaches consumers | Distribution channels, logistics |
| **Promotion** | Communication to persuade consumers to buy | Advertising, sales promotions, PR, personal selling |

---

## 1.3 Types of Markets

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Consumer market** | Individuals/households buying for personal use |
| **Industrial/Producer market** | Businesses buying raw materials or components for production |
| **Reseller market** | Wholesalers and retailers buying to resell |
| **Government market** | Government agencies buying for public use |
| **International market** | Buyers and sellers from different countries |

---

## 1.4 Market Segmentation

**Market segmentation** is the process of dividing a broad market into distinct subgroups (segments) of consumers who have common needs or characteristics.

**Bases for segmentation:**
| Basis | Variables |
|-------|---------|
| **Demographic** | Age, gender, income, education, family size |
| **Geographic** | Region, urban/rural, country, climate |
| **Psychographic** | Lifestyle, values, personality |
| **Behavioural** | Usage rate, brand loyalty, benefits sought |

**Benefits of segmentation:** Targeted marketing, better use of resources, competitive advantage, better customer satisfaction.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Define the marketing mix and explain the role of each element.

**Solution:**
The **marketing mix** is the combination of controllable marketing tools a firm uses to achieve its marketing objectives in a target market.

- **Product:** The actual good or service. Decisions include features, quality, brand name, and packaging to meet consumer needs.
- **Price:** The cost to the consumer. Strategies include penetration pricing (low price to gain market share) or skimming (high price for new/premium products).
- **Place:** Distribution — how the product reaches the consumer efficiently (direct or through intermediaries).
- **Promotion:** Communication activities — advertising, promotions, public relations — that inform and persuade consumers to buy.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests the 4 Ps of marketing, market segmentation bases, and market types.

> **Common trap:** Marketing is NOT the same as selling or advertising. Marketing is broader and starts BEFORE the product is created (identifying needs) and continues AFTER the sale (customer service).

# TOPIC 2: Consumer Behaviour and Market Research
**Topic ID:** SSS-MKT-001-02

## 2.1 Consumer Behaviour

**Consumer behaviour** is the study of how individuals, groups, and organisations select, buy, use, and dispose of goods and services to satisfy their needs.

### Factors Influencing Consumer Behaviour:

| Factor | Variables |
|--------|---------|
| **Cultural** | Culture, subculture, social class |
| **Social** | Reference groups, family, roles and status |
| **Personal** | Age, occupation, income, lifestyle, personality |
| **Psychological** | Motivation, perception, learning, attitudes |

### Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs:
Consumers are motivated by unsatisfied needs. Maslow arranged them in order:
1. **Physiological needs** — food, water, shelter (most basic)
2. **Safety needs** — security, stability
3. **Social needs** — love, belonging, friendship
4. **Esteem needs** — status, recognition, achievement
5. **Self-actualisation** — reaching full potential (highest)

---

## 2.2 Market Research

**Market research** is the systematic collection, analysis, and interpretation of information about a market, consumers, and the business environment.

### Types of Market Research Data:

| Type | Description | Methods |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Primary data** | New data collected for a specific purpose | Surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, experiments |
| **Secondary data** | Data already collected for another purpose | Published reports, government statistics, company records, internet |

### Steps in the Market Research Process:
1. Define the problem/objective
2. Design the research plan (choose methods)
3. Collect data
4. Analyse and interpret data
5. Present findings and recommendations

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Distinguish between primary and secondary data in market research and give TWO examples of each.

**Solution:**
**Primary data** is information collected for the first time specifically for the current research purpose. Examples: (1) Questionnaires administered to shoppers in a market; (2) Focus group discussions with target consumers about a new product.

**Secondary data** is information that already exists, collected originally for a different purpose. Examples: (1) Government census reports; (2) Industry association statistics on consumer spending.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests primary vs secondary data, factors affecting consumer behaviour, and Maslow's hierarchy.

> **Common trap:** Secondary data is not necessarily less valuable — it is cheaper and quicker to obtain. Its main limitation is that it may be outdated or not specifically relevant to the current research question.

# TOPIC 3: Pricing and Product Strategies
**Topic ID:** SSS-MKT-002-01

## 3.1 Pricing Strategies

**Price** is the amount of money customers pay for a product. Pricing decisions directly affect profitability and market positioning.

| Strategy | Description | Best used when |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| **Cost-plus pricing** | Add a mark-up % to the cost of production | Ensuring profits are covered |
| **Penetration pricing** | Set low initial price to gain market share | Entering competitive market |
| **Price skimming** | Set high price initially; lower later | New innovative products |
| **Competitive pricing** | Set price at or near competitors' prices | Competitive market |
| **Psychological pricing** | Set price just below a round number (e.g., ₦999) | Retail; consumer perception |
| **Discount pricing** | Reduce price temporarily | Clearing stock; promotions |
| **Price discrimination** | Charge different prices to different segments | Airlines, cinemas |

---

## 3.2 Product Life Cycle (PLC)

A product goes through four stages from launch to withdrawal:

| Stage | Sales | Profit | Strategy |
|-------|-------|--------|---------|
| **Introduction** | Low | Negative (costs high) | Heavy promotion; build awareness |
| **Growth** | Rising rapidly | Rising | Expand distribution; add features |
| **Maturity** | Peak; slowing | Peak; then declining | Differentiate; retain customers |
| **Decline** | Falling | Falling | Reduce costs; decide to withdraw or rebrand |

---

## 3.3 Branding and Packaging

**Brand:** A name, symbol, or design that identifies and differentiates a product.

**Benefits of branding:**
- Builds consumer loyalty and trust
- Allows premium pricing
- Easier to launch new products under established brand name
- Distinguishes from competitors

**Packaging:** The wrapping/container for a product.
Functions: Protect the product, attract consumers, provide information, facilitate storage and transport.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Explain the product life cycle and discuss the marketing strategies appropriate at each stage.

**Solution:**
The product life cycle describes the stages a product goes through from its launch to withdrawal.

- **Introduction:** Sales are low; heavy investment in advertising to create awareness. Price may be high (skimming) or low (penetration) depending on strategy.
- **Growth:** Sales increase rapidly; word of mouth spreads; profit grows; competitors enter. Strategy: Improve product features, expand distribution.
- **Maturity:** Sales peak and stabilise; intense competition; profits begin to fall. Strategy: Price promotions, product modifications, target new segments.
- **Decline:** Sales and profits fall consistently. Strategy: Reduce costs, withdraw weak products, possibly rebrand or relaunch.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests PLC stages and appropriate strategies, pricing methods, and branding benefits.

> **Common trap:** The PLC describes the product, not the company. A company can have products at different PLC stages simultaneously.

# TOPIC 4: Channels of Distribution and Retail Trade
**Topic ID:** SSS-MKT-003-01

## 4.1 Channels of Distribution

A **channel of distribution** (marketing channel) is the path through which goods and services flow from the producer to the final consumer.

### Types of Channels:

| Channel | Route | Best for |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Direct channel** | Producer → Consumer | Services, perishables, custom goods |
| **Short channel** | Producer → Retailer → Consumer | Large retailers, supermarkets |
| **Traditional channel** | Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer | Most consumer goods |
| **Long channel** | Producer → Agent → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer | International trade, import/export |

### Factors determining channel choice:
- Nature of the product (perishable? bulky? complex?)
- Number of consumers (few or many?)
- Geographic spread of market
- Financial resources of producer
- Degree of control desired over distribution

---

## 4.2 Types of Retailers

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Supermarket** | Self-service; wide product range; large scale | Shoprite, SPAR |
| **Hypermarket** | Very large; food + non-food | Large Shoprite stores |
| **Department store** | Multiple departments under one roof; different goods | Silverbird |
| **Convenience store** | Small; limited range; long opening hours | Neighbourhood shops |
| **Market stall** | Open market trading | Balogun, Ariaria markets |
| **Online retailer** | Sells via internet | Jumia, Konga |
| **Franchise** | Branded outlet operating under licence | Chicken Republic, Cold Stone |

---

## 4.3 Functions of the Retailer

1. **Provides convenience** — locates near consumers; opens at suitable times
2. **Breaks bulk** — buys in large quantities; sells in small units
3. **Provides variety** — stocks products from many manufacturers
4. **Offers credit** — facilitates hire purchase for consumers
5. **After-sales service** — repairs, returns, warranties
6. **Market information** — feeds back consumer preferences to manufacturers

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State FOUR reasons why a producer might prefer to use a short channel of distribution.

**Solution:**
1. **Perishable goods** — Fresh food, flowers, and similar products must reach consumers quickly; every extra link adds delay
2. **Better control** — Bypassing the wholesaler gives the producer more control over pricing and presentation in the retail store
3. **Large retailers** — When selling to major supermarkets that buy in very large quantities, there is no need for a wholesaler
4. **Better information** — A shorter channel provides faster and more direct market feedback to the producer

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests channel types, reasons for channel choice, and retailer functions.

> **Common trap:** A retailer sells ONLY to the final consumer. If an entity sells to other businesses for resale, it is a wholesaler, not a retailer.

# TOPIC 5: Advertising, Sales Promotion and Public Relations
**Topic ID:** SSS-MKT-003-02

## 5.1 Advertising

**Advertising** is a paid, non-personal form of communication through mass media to promote products, services, or ideas to a target audience.

### Types of Advertising:

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Informative** | Educate; tell consumers about new products | New product launch |
| **Persuasive** | Convince consumers to choose a specific brand | Comparative advertising |
| **Reminder** | Keep brand in consumers' minds | Long-established brands |
| **Institutional** | Promote organisation's image, not a product | Corporate social responsibility campaigns |

### Advertising Media:

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| **Television** | Mass reach; visual; sound | Expensive; ad skipping |
| **Radio** | Wide reach; affordable | No visual impact |
| **Newspapers** | Detailed information; targeted regions | Short shelf life |
| **Billboards (outdoor)** | Repeated exposure; 24/7 | Brief message only |
| **Internet/Social media** | Highly targeted; interactive; measurable | Requires internet access |
| **Magazines** | Quality image; specific audience | Less frequent publication |

---

## 5.2 Sales Promotion

**Sales promotion** consists of short-term incentives to encourage the immediate purchase of a product.

| Tool | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Free samples** | Give product to try for free | Toothpaste samples |
| **Coupons** | Discount vouchers | "50% off next purchase" |
| **Price reductions** | Temporary price cuts | "Buy 2, get 1 free" |
| **Competitions** | Prize draws for purchasers | "Win a car" |
| **Loyalty cards/points** | Reward repeat purchases | Shoprite Xtra Savings |
| **Trade discounts** | Discounts to retailers/wholesalers | Bulk purchase discounts |

---

## 5.3 Public Relations (PR)

**Public relations (PR)** is the management of communication between an organisation and its publics to build and maintain a positive image.

**PR tools:**
- Press releases (news to media)
- Press conferences
- Sponsorship (sports events, education)
- Community service
- Crisis management

**PR vs Advertising:**
| Feature | Advertising | PR |
|---------|------------|-----|
| Paid for? | Yes | Usually free (earned media) |
| Control | Full control | Less control (media decides) |
| Credibility | Lower (audience knows it's an ad) | Higher (appears as news) |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State the differences between advertising and sales promotion, and give TWO examples of each.

**Solution:**
**Advertising** is a long-term paid communication strategy to build brand awareness and image. Examples: (1) A TV commercial for a mobile network; (2) A newspaper advertisement for a new car model.

**Sales promotion** is a short-term incentive to stimulate immediate purchase. Examples: (1) "Buy 2 sachets of detergent, get 1 free" in a supermarket; (2) A prize draw for customers who purchase a product within a given week.

**Key difference:** Advertising builds brand over time; sales promotion creates an immediate, temporary boost in sales.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests types of advertising, advertising media, and differences between advertising, sales promotion, and PR.

> **Common trap:** Public relations is NOT advertising. PR is free (earned media); advertising is paid. However, both are forms of promotion within the marketing mix.
