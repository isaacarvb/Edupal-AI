# TOPIC 1: Introduction to Commerce and Trade
**Topic ID:** SSS-COM-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Commerce

**Commerce** is the branch of business activity that is concerned with the exchange of goods and services. It includes all the activities involved in buying and selling, and the services that facilitate this exchange (banking, insurance, transportation, advertising, warehousing, etc.).

**Scope of Commerce:**
- **Trade:** Buying and selling of goods (home trade and foreign trade)
- **Aids to trade:** All activities that support trade (banking, insurance, transport, warehousing, communication, advertising)

---

## 1.2 Types of Trade

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Home (Domestic) Trade** | Trade within a country | Selling goods within Nigeria |
| **Foreign (International) Trade** | Trade between countries | Nigeria exporting crude oil to USA |
| **Import trade** | Buying goods from other countries | Nigeria importing rice from Thailand |
| **Export trade** | Selling goods to other countries | Nigeria exporting cocoa to Europe |
| **Entrepôt (Re-export) trade** | Importing goods and re-exporting them | Singapore re-exporting electronics |

---

## 1.3 Wholesale and Retail Trade

| Feature | Wholesaler | Retailer |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Buys from** | Producer/manufacturer | Wholesaler |
| **Sells to** | Retailers | Final consumer |
| **Quantity** | Large quantities (in bulk) | Small quantities |
| **Storage** | Large warehouses | Small stockroom |
| **Credit** | Gives credit to retailers | Mostly cash sales |
| **Price** | Lower (trade discount) | Higher (retail price) |

**Functions of the wholesaler:**
1. Bulk buying and breaking bulk
2. Warehousing/storage
3. Risk-bearing (holds stock, prices may change)
4. Financing (gives credit to retailers)
5. Provides market information
6. Ensures steady supply

---

## 1.4 Channels of Distribution

The **channel of distribution** is the route goods take from the producer to the final consumer.

| Channel | Route | When used |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Channel 1 (Shortest) | Producer → Consumer | Services; perishables; direct selling |
| Channel 2 | Producer → Retailer → Consumer | Large retailers (supermarkets) |
| Channel 3 (Most common) | Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer | Consumer goods (FMCG) |
| Channel 4 (Longest) | Producer → Agent → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer | International/export trade |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Differentiate between a wholesaler and a retailer, and state TWO functions of each.

**Solution:**
A **wholesaler** buys goods in large quantities from manufacturers and sells in smaller quantities to retailers. A **retailer** buys from wholesalers and sells in very small quantities directly to the final consumer.

Wholesaler functions:
1. Breaking bulk — buys in large amounts and sells in smaller units to retailers
2. Warehousing — stores goods until needed, reducing risk of shortages

Retailer functions:
1. Provides convenience — opens at accessible times/locations for consumers
2. Offers after-sales service — e.g., returns, advice

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests definitions of trade types, channels of distribution, and functions of wholesalers/retailers.

> **Common trap:** The wholesaler does NOT sell directly to the final consumer — that is the retailer's role.

# TOPIC 2: Business Organisations
**Topic ID:** SSS-COM-001-02

## 2.1 Forms of Business Ownership

| Form | Owners | Liability | Capital | Control |
|------|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Sole proprietorship** | 1 person | Unlimited | Personal savings/loans | Owner alone |
| **Partnership** | 2–20 persons | Usually unlimited | Partners' contributions | Shared |
| **Private Limited Company (Ltd)** | 2–50 shareholders | Limited | Sale of shares (not public) | Directors/Board |
| **Public Limited Company (PLC)** | Many shareholders | Limited | Public share issue (Stock Exchange) | Board of Directors |
| **Co-operative society** | Members | Limited | Members' subscriptions | Democratic |
| **Public corporation** | Government | Government | Government budget | Board/Ministry |

**Limited liability:** Shareholders/owners can only lose the money they invested — personal property is protected if the business fails.

---

## 2.2 Advantages and Disadvantages

### Sole Proprietorship:
- ✅ Easy to set up | Complete control | Keeps all profits | Flexible
- ❌ Unlimited liability | Limited capital | No continuity (ends with owner)

### Partnership:
- ✅ More capital | Shared workload | Specialisation
- ❌ Unlimited liability | Risk of disagreements | No continuity if a partner leaves

### Public Limited Company (PLC):
- ✅ Large capital | Limited liability | Continuity | Professional management
- ❌ Complex to set up | Loss of control | Accounts made public | Slow decision-making

---

## 2.3 Public Enterprises

**Public corporations** are owned and operated by the government on behalf of the public.
Examples: Nigerian National Petroleum Company (NNPC), Nigeria Ports Authority (NPA)

**Advantages:** Essential services provided; profits go to public; employment
**Disadvantages:** Political interference; inefficiency; lack of profit motive

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State THREE differences between a public limited company and a private limited company.

**Solution:**

| Feature | Public Limited Company (PLC) | Private Limited Company (Ltd) |
|---------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| Shareholders | Unlimited (can sell shares to public) | 2–50 maximum |
| Shares listed on Stock Exchange? | Yes | No |
| Minimum capital | Higher | Lower |

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests distinctions between forms of business, advantages/disadvantages, and appropriate form for a given scenario.

> **Common trap:** A private limited company is NOT listed on the stock exchange — only public limited companies (PLCs) can sell shares to the general public through the stock exchange.

# TOPIC 3: Banking and Finance
**Topic ID:** SSS-COM-002-01

## 3.1 Definition of a Bank

A **bank** is a financial institution that accepts deposits from customers, makes loans, and provides various financial services.

---

## 3.2 Types of Banks

| Type | Functions |
|------|----------|
| **Central Bank** (CBN in Nigeria) | Issues currency; acts as banker to government; controls monetary policy; regulates commercial banks; lender of last resort |
| **Commercial Bank** | Accepts deposits; gives loans; provides current/savings accounts; money transfer |
| **Merchant Bank** | Finances large businesses; provides investment services; not for small depositors |
| **Mortgage Bank** | Provides long-term loans for property purchase |
| **Development Bank** | Finances large developmental projects |
| **Microfinance Bank** | Provides small loans to low-income earners |

---

## 3.3 Services Provided by Commercial Banks

| Service | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Current account** | No/minimal interest; cheques can be used; overdraft facility |
| **Savings account** | Interest earned; less flexible withdrawal |
| **Fixed deposit account** | Money locked for a fixed period; higher interest |
| **Loans and overdrafts** | Lending to businesses and individuals |
| **Money transfer** | Wire transfers, NEFT, RTGS |
| **Foreign exchange** | Buying and selling foreign currencies |
| **Safe custody** | Keeping valuables in safe deposit boxes |

---

## 3.4 Methods of Payment

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Cash** | Physical money (legal tender) |
| **Cheque** | Written order to bank to pay a specified sum |
| **Bank draft** | Guaranteed cheque issued by a bank |
| **Standing order** | Regular automatic payments from account |
| **Direct debit** | Payee collects variable amounts from payer's account |
| **Credit card** | Buy now, pay later (credit given by card company) |
| **Debit card** | Payment deducted directly from bank account |
| **NEFT/online transfer** | Electronic transfer between accounts |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State FOUR functions of the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN).

**Solution:**
1. **Currency issuance** — The CBN is the sole authority to issue legal tender (naira notes and coins) in Nigeria
2. **Banker to the government** — Manages government accounts and finances government activities
3. **Controller of credit (monetary policy)** — Uses tools like interest rates, cash reserve ratio, and open market operations to control the money supply and inflation
4. **Lender of last resort** — Provides emergency funds to commercial banks facing liquidity problems to prevent banking system collapse

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests functions of central and commercial banks, types of accounts, and methods of payment.

> **Common trap:** A **bank draft** is issued by the bank itself (guaranteed), while a **cheque** is issued by a customer from their account. A bank draft is safer and cannot bounce.

# TOPIC 4: Home Trade, Foreign Trade and Terms of Payment
**Topic ID:** SSS-COM-003-01

## 4.1 Documents Used in Trade

| Document | Issuer | Purpose |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **Enquiry** | Buyer | Initial request for price/info |
| **Quotation** | Seller | Reply stating price and conditions |
| **Order** | Buyer | Formal request to purchase goods |
| **Invoice** | Seller | Itemised bill sent with goods |
| **Receipt** | Seller | Acknowledgment of payment |
| **Credit note** | Seller | Reduction in debt (e.g., for returned goods) |
| **Debit note** | Seller | Increase in amount owed (undercharge correction) |
| **Statement of account** | Seller | Summary of transactions over a period |

---

## 4.2 Documents in Foreign Trade

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Bill of lading** | Receipt issued by shipping company; proof of goods shipped |
| **Certificate of origin** | States the country where goods were produced |
| **Commercial invoice** | Detailed bill for customs purposes |
| **Airway bill** | Air transport equivalent of bill of lading |
| **Bill of exchange** | Written order by seller for buyer to pay |
| **Letter of credit** | Bank guarantees payment to exporter |
| **Insurance certificate** | Proof that goods are insured during transit |

---

## 4.3 Terms of Payment in Trade

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cash on delivery (COD)** | Payment made when goods arrive |
| **Cash before delivery (CBD)** | Full payment before goods are sent |
| **Cash with order (CWO)** | Payment sent with order |
| **Open account** | Buyer pays after a set period (e.g., 30 days) |
| **Bill of exchange** | Formal document requiring payment at a stated future date |
| **Letter of credit** | Bank guarantees payment to exporter once conditions are met |
| **Documentary credit** | Payment against presentation of shipping documents |

---

## 4.4 Balance of Trade vs Balance of Payments

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Balance of trade** | Difference between a country's exports and imports of goods (visible trade) |
| **Favourable balance of trade** | Exports > Imports (trade surplus) |
| **Unfavourable balance of trade** | Imports > Exports (trade deficit) |
| **Balance of payments** | Complete record of all economic transactions between a country and the rest of the world |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Arrange these trade documents in the correct order: Invoice, Receipt, Enquiry, Order, Quotation.

**Solution:**
1. **Enquiry** — Buyer asks for information/prices
2. **Quotation** — Seller responds with prices and terms
3. **Order** — Buyer places a formal order
4. **Invoice** — Seller sends bill with the goods
5. **Receipt** — Issued after payment is made

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests sequencing of trade documents, functions of each document, and foreign trade documents.

> **Common trap:** An **invoice** is sent by the SELLER, not the buyer. A **purchase order** is sent by the buyer.

# TOPIC 5: Consumer Protection and E-Commerce
**Topic ID:** SSS-COM-003-02

## 5.1 Consumer Protection

**Consumer protection** refers to the laws, regulations, and organisations that safeguard buyers from unfair business practices.

**Why consumers need protection:**
- Sellers may provide false or misleading information
- Goods may be substandard or dangerous
- Prices may be inflated through monopoly
- Consumers may not know their rights

---

## 5.2 Consumer Rights

Based on the UN Guidelines for Consumer Protection, consumers have the right to:
1. **Safety** — Protection from harmful products
2. **Information** — Access to accurate product details
3. **Choice** — Access to competitive products
4. **Be heard** — Voice complaints and get redress
5. **Redress** — Compensation for faulty goods
6. **Basic needs** — Access to essential goods/services
7. **Healthy environment** — A safe environment to live in

---

## 5.3 Consumer Protection Agencies in Nigeria

| Organisation | Role |
|-------------|------|
| **Consumer Protection Council (CPC)** | Investigates complaints; ensures product standards |
| **Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON)** | Sets standards for manufactured products; issues SON mark |
| **National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC)** | Regulates food, drugs, cosmetics, water; prevents harmful products |
| **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)** | Regulates business registration; prevents fraudulent companies |

---

## 5.4 E-Commerce

**E-commerce** (Electronic Commerce) is the buying and selling of goods and services over the internet.

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **B2C** (Business to Consumer) | Businesses sell directly to consumers online | Jumia, Konga |
| **B2B** (Business to Business) | Businesses sell to other businesses | Wholesale online suppliers |
| **C2C** (Consumer to Consumer) | Consumers sell to other consumers | Jiji.ng, eBay |
| **M-commerce** | Commerce via mobile devices | Mobile banking, apps |

**Advantages of e-commerce:**
- 24/7 availability
- Wider market reach (global)
- Lower operating costs
- Convenience for consumers
- Easy price comparison

**Disadvantages:**
- Cybercrime/online fraud risk
- Cannot inspect goods before purchase
- Requires internet access
- Delivery delays

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State FOUR ways the government protects consumers in Nigeria.

**Solution:**
1. **Legislation** — Laws like the Consumer Protection Council Act set standards for goods and services
2. **Regulatory agencies** — Bodies like NAFDAC and SON check that products meet safety standards before sale
3. **Price control** — The government may fix prices of essential goods to prevent exploitation
4. **Public education** — Consumer education campaigns inform the public about their rights

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests consumer rights, roles of consumer protection agencies (especially NAFDAC and SON), and advantages/disadvantages of e-commerce.

> **Common trap:** NAFDAC regulates food, drugs, cosmetics, and water — NOT all manufactured goods. SON covers manufactured goods standards more broadly.
