# TOPIC 1: Family, Community and Social Groups
**Topic ID:** JSS-SCI-001-01

## 1.1 The Family

**Definition:** A **family** is the basic social unit in society, consisting of people related by blood, marriage, or adoption who live together and share responsibilities.

---

## 1.2 Types of Family

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Nuclear family** | Parents (husband and wife) and their children only | Father, mother, 3 children |
| **Extended family** | Nuclear family + grandparents, aunts, uncles, cousins | Common in Nigerian culture |
| **Single-parent family** | One parent (father or mother) raising children alone | Widowed or divorced parent |
| **Polygamous family** | One husband with more than one wife and their children | Common in some Nigerian cultures |

---

## 1.3 Functions of the Family

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Reproduction** | Bearing and raising children to continue the human race |
| **Socialisation** | Teaching children norms, values, language, and behaviour |
| **Protection** | Providing physical safety and emotional security |
| **Economic support** | Providing food, clothing, shelter and other needs |
| **Education** | First place of learning for children |
| **Affection and love** | Providing emotional support and belonging |

---

## 1.4 The Community

**Community:** A group of people living in the same geographical area who share resources, interact, and have common interests.

**Types of communities:**
- **Rural community** — Village setting; mainly farming; close-knit
- **Urban community** — City setting; commerce and industry; diverse population
- **Semi-urban** — Transition between rural and urban

---

## 1.5 Social Groups

| Group | Examples | Purpose |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Peer group** | Classmates, friends | Socialisation, support |
| **Religious group** | Church, mosque, prayer group | Worship, moral guidance |
| **Cultural/ethnic group** | Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa community groups | Cultural preservation |
| **Professional group** | NMA (doctors), NBA (lawyers) | Protect professional interests |
| **Political group** | Political parties, pressure groups | Influence governance |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR functions of the family in society.

**Solution:**
1. **Reproduction** — The family is the unit through which children are born and brought into the world to continue the human race.
2. **Socialisation** — Parents and older family members teach children the values, norms, language, and behaviour expected in their society.
3. **Economic provision** — The family provides food, shelter, clothing, healthcare, and other material needs for its members.
4. **Protection** — The family offers physical safety and emotional security, especially for children, the elderly, and vulnerable members.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests types of family, functions of the family, and the difference between rural and urban communities.

> **Common trap:** The nuclear family in Nigeria is NOT the traditional norm — the EXTENDED family is. Many exam questions explore this difference and ask about advantages/disadvantages of each type.

# TOPIC 2: Three Tiers of Government in Nigeria
**Topic ID:** JSS-SCI-002-01

## 2.1 The Federal System

**Nigeria operates a federal system of government**, meaning political power is shared between the central (federal) government and the state and local governments.

---

## 2.2 The Three Tiers

| Tier | Government | Headquarters | Legislative body |
|------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **1st tier** | Federal Government | Abuja (FCT) | National Assembly |
| **2nd tier** | State Government (36 + FCT) | State capitals | State House of Assembly |
| **3rd tier** | Local Government Areas (774 LGAs) | LGA secretariats | LGA Council |

---

## 2.3 Functions of Each Tier

### Federal Government:
- Defence and national security
- Foreign affairs and international relations
- Currency (Central Bank of Nigeria)
- Federal highways and railways
- Education policy and tertiary education
- Immigration and customs

### State Government:
- Primary and secondary education
- State roads and infrastructure
- Agriculture (state level)
- Health (state hospitals)
- State law enforcement (police works with states)

### Local Government:
- Collection of local rates and fees
- Primary school administration
- Local roads and drains
- Market management
- Registration of births, deaths, and marriages
- Naming of streets

---

## 2.4 Revenue Sharing (Fiscal Federalism)

Revenue from oil and other sources is collected by the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) and distributed through the Federation Account Allocation Committee (FAAC):

| Tier | Approximate share |
|------|-----------------|
| Federal Government | ~52% |
| State Governments (36 + FCT) | ~26% |
| Local Government Areas | ~20% |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State THREE functions each of the state government and local government in Nigeria.

**Solution:**
**State Government:**
1. Establishes and manages state primary and secondary schools
2. Constructs and maintains roads within the state
3. Operates state hospitals and health centres

**Local Government:**
1. Manages markets and ensures market traders pay levies
2. Administers primary schools within the local government area
3. Registers births, deaths, and marriages of residents

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the three tiers, their functions, and the distribution of powers under Nigeria's federal system.

> **Common trap:** Local Government Areas (LGAs) are the THIRD tier — they are NOT part of state government even though state governments supervise them. There are 774 LGAs across Nigeria.

# TOPIC 3: Democracy and Elections in Nigeria
**Topic ID:** JSS-SCI-003-01

## 3.1 Definition of Democracy

**Democracy** is a system of government in which political power originates from and is exercised by the people, either directly or through freely elected representatives.

Abraham Lincoln's definition: **"Government of the people, by the people, and for the people."**

---

## 3.2 Types of Democracy

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Direct democracy** | Citizens vote on every decision themselves | Ancient Athens; some Swiss referendums |
| **Representative (indirect) democracy** | Citizens elect representatives to govern on their behalf | Nigeria, USA, UK |
| **Liberal democracy** | Combines elections with protection of individual rights and rule of law | Most modern democracies |

---

## 3.3 Principles of Democracy

| Principle | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Popular sovereignty** | Power belongs to the people |
| **Rule of law** | Everyone, including leaders, is subject to the law |
| **Separation of powers** | Legislature, executive, judiciary are independent |
| **Free and fair elections** | Citizens freely choose their leaders |
| **Fundamental rights** | Citizens' rights are protected |
| **Majority rule with minority rights** | Majority decides but minority is protected |
| **Free press** | Independent media monitors government |

---

## 3.4 Elections in Nigeria

**INEC (Independent National Electoral Commission)** conducts federal and state elections.

**Types of elections:**
| Election | Period | Who is elected |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| Presidential | Every 4 years | President of Nigeria |
| Governorship | Every 4 years | State governor |
| National Assembly | Every 4 years | Senators and House of Representatives members |
| State Assembly | Every 4 years | State legislators |
| Local Government | Conducted by SIECs | LGA chairpersons and councillors |

**Voting process:**
1. Voter registration (obtain Permanent Voter Card — PVC)
2. Accreditation on election day (BVAS — Bimodal Voter Accreditation System)
3. Casting ballot
4. Counting and announcing results

---

## 3.5 Problems of Democracy in Nigeria

- Election rigging and malpractice
- Low voter turnout especially among youth
- Voter apathy and illiteracy
- Violence and thuggery during elections
- Weak institutions and corruption
- Money politics (vote buying)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** What is democracy? State FOUR features of a democratic government.

**Solution:**
**Democracy** is a system of government where political authority comes from the people, who exercise it through regular, free, and fair elections and through the protection of fundamental rights.

Four features:
1. **Regular, free, and fair elections** — Citizens choose their representatives through competitive elections without coercion.
2. **Rule of law** — All citizens and officials are equally subject to the laws of the land.
3. **Fundamental human rights** — Citizens' rights to life, expression, association, and dignity are protected.
4. **Separation of powers** — The legislature, executive, and judiciary are independent of each other to prevent abuse of power.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the definition and features of democracy, INEC's role, the electoral process, and problems of democracy.

> **Common trap:** Local government elections are NOT conducted by INEC — they are conducted by **State Independent Electoral Commissions (SIECs)**, one in each state.

# TOPIC 4: Environmental Challenges and Conservation
**Topic ID:** JSS-SCI-004-01

## 4.1 The Environment

**Environment** is everything that surrounds a living organism, including physical, chemical, and biological factors.

**Two main components:**
- **Physical environment** — air, water, soil, temperature, light
- **Biological environment** — all other living organisms

---

## 4.2 Environmental Challenges in Nigeria

| Challenge | Description | Effects |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Deforestation** | Clearing of forests for farming, building, fuel | Soil erosion, loss of biodiversity, reduced rainfall |
| **Desertification** | Advance of desert (especially in the north) | Loss of farmland, displacement of communities |
| **Oil pollution** | Oil spills in the Niger Delta | Water contamination, loss of aquatic life, health problems |
| **Flooding** | Heavy rains + poor drainage + deforestation | Property loss, disease, displacement |
| **Air pollution** | Vehicle fumes, burning waste, industrial emissions | Respiratory diseases, acid rain, climate change |
| **Water pollution** | Industrial waste, sewage, plastics in water bodies | Death of aquatic life, waterborne diseases |
| **Soil erosion** | Loss of topsoil by wind or water | Reduced agricultural productivity |

---

## 4.3 Causes of Environmental Degradation

- Rapid population growth → increased pressure on land and resources
- Poverty → unsustainable use of natural resources (bush burning, overfarming)
- Industrialisation → pollution and waste
- Poor government policies and enforcement
- Cultural practices (bush burning, open defecation)

---

## 4.4 Conservation and Environmental Protection

**Conservation** is the careful management and protection of natural resources to ensure their availability for future generations.

| Strategy | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Afforestation/Reforestation** | Planting new trees; replacing cut forests |
| **Waste management** | Recycling, proper refuse disposal, waste-to-energy |
| **Pollution control** | Laws limiting emissions; treating waste before discharge |
| **Wildlife protection** | National parks and game reserves (e.g., Yankari, Omo Forest) |
| **Renewable energy** | Using solar, wind instead of fossil fuels |
| **Environmental education** | Raising public awareness about conservation |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Identify THREE environmental challenges in Nigeria and suggest ONE solution for each.

**Solution:**
1. **Deforestation** — Nigeria loses large areas of forest each year to farming, logging, and fuelwood collection.
**Solution:** Government should implement a **national afforestation programme**, encouraging tree planting and declaring moratoriums on illegal logging.

2. **Oil pollution in the Niger Delta** — Oil spills from pipelines contaminate rivers and farmland, destroying livelihoods.
**Solution:** Oil companies should be legally required to **clean up spills immediately** and use modern, non-corrosive pipelines to prevent leaks.

3. **Flooding** — Urban flooding in Lagos and other cities is worsened by blocked drains and removal of vegetation.
**Solution:** Government should invest in proper **drainage infrastructure**, enforce building regulations that preserve green spaces, and prohibit construction on flood plains.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests identification of environmental challenges, their causes and effects, and conservation strategies.

> **Common trap:** Desertification refers specifically to the expansion of DESERT conditions — it mainly affects northern Nigeria (e.g., Borno, Sokoto, Kano). It is different from general deforestation, though deforestation contributes to it.

# TOPIC 5: Human Rights, Responsibilities and Civic Duties
**Topic ID:** JSS-SCI-005-01

## 5.1 Definition of Human Rights

**Human rights** are the basic freedoms and entitlements that every person has simply by virtue of being human. They are universal (apply to everyone), inalienable (cannot be taken away), and indivisible (cannot be separated).

---

## 5.2 Categories of Human Rights

| Category | Rights included |
|----------|----------------|
| **Civil and political rights** | Right to life, liberty, free speech, fair trial, vote |
| **Economic, social and cultural rights** | Right to work, education, healthcare, cultural participation |
| **Group/collective rights** | Right to development, clean environment, peace |

---

## 5.3 Fundamental Rights in Nigeria (1999 Constitution, Chapter IV)

| Right | What it means for students |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Right to life** | No one can take your life unlawfully |
| **Right to dignity** | No one can torture or humiliate you |
| **Right to personal liberty** | You cannot be detained without lawful reason |
| **Right to fair hearing** | You have a right to be heard in any proceeding that affects you |
| **Freedom of expression** | You can speak, write, and share views freely (within the law) |
| **Freedom of thought and religion** | You can hold any belief |
| **Freedom of movement** | You can move freely within Nigeria |
| **Right to education** | Every child has a right to basic education |

---

## 5.4 Civic Responsibilities

Rights come with corresponding **responsibilities** (duties).

| Responsibility | Explanation |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Obey the law** | All citizens must follow the laws of Nigeria |
| **Pay taxes** | Taxes fund schools, hospitals, roads |
| **Vote** | Exercise the right to vote in elections |
| **Respect others' rights** | Your freedom ends where others' rights begin |
| **Protect the environment** | Keep your community clean; conserve resources |
| **Report crime** | Assist law enforcement by reporting criminal activity |
| **National service** | Contribute to nation-building (e.g., NYSC for graduates) |

---

## 5.5 Civic Participation

| Form | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Voting** | Participating in elections |
| **Running for office** | Standing as a candidate for political positions |
| **Community service** | Volunteering in schools, hospitals, cleanups |
| **Advocacy** | Campaigning for rights, justice, or policy change |
| **Paying taxes** | Contributing financially to government services |
| **Petition signing** | Formally requesting government action |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR civic responsibilities of a Nigerian citizen.

**Solution:**
1. **Obedience to the law** — Every citizen is required to obey the laws of Nigeria. Laws are made to protect people and maintain order in society.
2. **Voting in elections** — Citizens aged 18 and above who are registered have the responsibility to participate in elections and choose their leaders.
3. **Payment of taxes** — Citizens earning income are required to pay taxes to fund government services such as schools, roads, and hospitals.
4. **Environmental responsibility** — Citizens must protect the environment by avoiding littering, not burning refuse indiscriminately, and preserving public spaces.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests categories of rights, specific fundamental rights in the Nigerian constitution, civic duties, and forms of civic participation.

> **Common trap:** Rights and responsibilities are two sides of the same coin. You CANNOT only claim rights without accepting responsibilities. BECE often asks you to state the responsibility that corresponds to a given right (e.g., freedom of expression → responsibility to express yourself without inciting violence or spreading false information).
