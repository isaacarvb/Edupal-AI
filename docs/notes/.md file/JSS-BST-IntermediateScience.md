# TOPIC 1: Characteristics and Classification of Living Things
**Topic ID:** JSS-BST-001-01

## 1.1 Definition

**Living things** (organisms) are any entities that exhibit all the characteristics of life. They are distinguished from non-living things by seven key characteristics, often remembered with the acronym **MRS GREN**.

---

## 1.2 Characteristics of Living Things (MRS GREN)

| Letter | Characteristic | Explanation |
|--------|---------------|-------------|
| **M** | **Movement** | Living things can move themselves or parts of themselves |
| **R** | **Respiration** | They release energy from food (aerobic or anaerobic) |
| **S** | **Sensitivity** | They detect and respond to changes in the environment |
| **G** | **Growth** | They increase in size and complexity over time |
| **R** | **Reproduction** | They produce offspring of the same kind |
| **E** | **Excretion** | They remove waste products produced by body chemistry |
| **N** | **Nutrition** | They obtain and use food/energy for growth and survival |

---

## 1.3 Classification of Living Things

Living things are grouped into **5 Kingdoms:**

| Kingdom | Description | Examples |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Monera** | Unicellular; no nucleus; bacteria | Bacteria, blue-green algae |
| **Protista** | Mostly unicellular; have nucleus | Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena |
| **Fungi** | Multicellular (mostly); absorb nutrients from dead material | Mushroom, mould, yeast |
| **Plantae** | Multicellular; photosynthesis; cell wall with cellulose | Mosses, ferns, flowering plants |
| **Animalia** | Multicellular; heterotrophic; no cell wall | Insects, fish, mammals |

---

## 1.4 Further Classification (Taxonomy)

The hierarchy of classification (from broadest to narrowest):
**Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**

Memory aid: **"King Philip Came Over For Good Soup"**

**Species** = the most specific rank; members of the same species can breed and produce fertile offspring.

---

## 1.5 The Animal Kingdom — Major Phyla

| Group | Features | Examples |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Mammals** | Warm-blooded; hair; give birth to live young; suckle young | Humans, dogs, whales |
| **Birds (Aves)** | Warm-blooded; feathers; lay eggs; wings | Eagle, parrot, penguin |
| **Reptiles** | Cold-blooded; scales; lay eggs on land | Lizard, snake, crocodile |
| **Amphibians** | Cold-blooded; live in water AND land; moist skin | Frog, toad, salamander |
| **Fish** | Cold-blooded; live in water; gills; scales | Tilapia, shark, catfish |
| **Insects** | 6 legs; 3 body parts; exoskeleton | Bee, fly, mosquito |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State the SEVEN characteristics of living things.

**Solution (MRS GREN):**
1. **Movement** — Living things can move all or part of their body.
2. **Respiration** — They obtain energy from food through chemical processes.
3. **Sensitivity** — They respond to stimuli in their environment (light, heat, touch).
4. **Growth** — They increase permanently in size and mass.
5. **Reproduction** — They produce offspring to continue their species.
6. **Excretion** — They remove metabolic waste products from their bodies.
7. **Nutrition** — They take in and use nutrients to fuel life processes.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests all seven characteristics of living things, the five kingdoms, and vertebrate/invertebrate classification.

> **Common trap:** Excretion is NOT the same as egestion (defecation). **Excretion** removes waste from METABOLIC REACTIONS inside cells (urine, carbon dioxide, sweat). Egestion removes undigested food from the gut. Both remove waste, but through different processes.

# TOPIC 2: Nutrition and Photosynthesis
**Topic ID:** JSS-BST-001-02

## 2.1 Types of Nutrition

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Autotrophic** | Makes own food using sunlight or chemicals | Plants, algae (photosynthesis) |
| **Heterotrophic** | Cannot make own food; consumes other organisms | Animals, fungi, most bacteria |

---

## 2.2 Food and Nutrients

**Nutrients** are substances in food that provide energy and support body functions.

| Nutrient | Functions | Sources |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Carbohydrates** | Provides energy; stored as glycogen or fat | Rice, yam, bread, cassava |
| **Proteins** | Build and repair tissues; make enzymes | Beans, meat, fish, eggs |
| **Fats/Lipids** | Energy store; insulation; protect organs; fat-soluble vitamins | Palm oil, groundnut, butter |
| **Vitamins** | Regulate body processes; prevent deficiency diseases | Fruits, vegetables, milk |
| **Minerals** | Build bones/teeth; regulate body chemistry | Fish (calcium), meat (iron) |
| **Water** | Transport medium; regulates temperature; dissolves substances | Drinking water, fruits |
| **Fibre (roughage)** | Aids digestion and prevents constipation | Vegetables, bran, legumes |

### Vitamin Deficiency Diseases:
| Vitamin | Deficiency disease |
|---------|------------------|
| Vitamin A | Night blindness |
| Vitamin B₁ (thiamine) | Beri-beri |
| Vitamin C | Scurvy |
| Vitamin D | Rickets (soft/bent bones) |
| Vitamin B₃ (niacin) | Pellagra |

---

## 2.3 Photosynthesis

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to manufacture glucose and release oxygen.

**Word equation:**
> Carbon dioxide + Water → (light energy, chlorophyll) → Glucose + Oxygen

**Chemical equation:**
> 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → (light) → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

**Raw materials needed:**
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — from air, enters through stomata (tiny pores in leaves)
- Water (H₂O) — absorbed from soil through roots
- Light energy — from the sun (absorbed by chlorophyll)
- Chlorophyll — green pigment in chloroplasts; captures light energy

**Products:**
- Glucose — used for energy, growth, or converted to starch
- Oxygen — released through stomata as a by-product

---

## 2.4 Leaf Structure and Function

| Part | Function |
|------|---------|
| **Leaf blade (lamina)** | Large flat surface to absorb maximum sunlight |
| **Chloroplasts** | Contain chlorophyll; site of photosynthesis |
| **Stomata** | Pores on leaf underside for gas exchange (CO₂ in, O₂ out) |
| **Guard cells** | Control opening and closing of stomata |
| **Veins** | Transport water (xylem) and glucose (phloem) |
| **Waxy cuticle** | Reduces water loss |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Write the word equation for photosynthesis and name TWO raw materials required.

**Solution:**
**Word equation:**
Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen
(In the presence of light energy and chlorophyll)

**Two raw materials:**
1. **Carbon dioxide** — taken in from the air through the stomata in the leaf
2. **Water** — absorbed from the soil through the roots and transported to the leaves by the xylem vessels

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the word and chemical equations for photosynthesis, raw materials and products, nutrient classes and their functions, and vitamin deficiency diseases.

> **Common trap:** Oxygen is a BY-PRODUCT of photosynthesis — it is released, not used. Glucose is the PRODUCT. Do not reverse them.

# TOPIC 3: States of Matter
**Topic ID:** JSS-BST-002-01

## 3.1 Definition

**Matter** is anything that has mass and occupies space. All matter is made of tiny particles (atoms and molecules) that are in constant motion.

---

## 3.2 The Three States of Matter

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|----------|-------|--------|-----|
| **Shape** | Fixed/definite | Takes shape of container | Takes shape of container |
| **Volume** | Fixed/definite | Fixed/definite | No fixed volume |
| **Particle arrangement** | Very close; regular order | Close; random | Far apart; random |
| **Particle motion** | Vibrate in fixed positions | Slide past each other | Move freely and quickly |
| **Compressibility** | Cannot be compressed | Barely compressible | Easily compressed |
| **Examples** | Rock, ice, wood | Water, oil, mercury | Steam, air, oxygen |

---

## 3.3 Changes of State

| Change | From → To | Energy change | Example |
|--------|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Melting** | Solid → Liquid | Absorbs heat | Ice → water |
| **Freezing** | Liquid → Solid | Releases heat | Water → ice |
| **Evaporation/Boiling** | Liquid → Gas | Absorbs heat | Water → steam |
| **Condensation** | Gas → Liquid | Releases heat | Steam → water |
| **Sublimation** | Solid → Gas directly | Absorbs heat | Dry ice, iodine crystals |
| **Deposition** | Gas → Solid directly | Releases heat | Frost formation |

---

## 3.4 Melting Point and Boiling Point

**Melting point:** The temperature at which a solid becomes liquid (also the temperature at which a liquid becomes solid).
- Pure water: 0°C

**Boiling point:** The temperature at which a liquid becomes a gas.
- Pure water: 100°C

**Impurities:**
- Dissolving substances in water **lowers** the melting point and **raises** the boiling point (e.g., salt water boils above 100°C and freezes below 0°C).

---

## 3.5 Diffusion and Osmosis

**Diffusion:** Movement of particles from an area of HIGH concentration to LOW concentration — down the concentration gradient. Happens in all states of matter.
Example: Perfume spreading through a room; smell of food.

**Osmosis:** Movement of water molecules across a semi-permeable membrane from a region of LOW solute concentration (dilute solution) to HIGH solute concentration (concentrated solution).

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Explain what happens to the particles of a solid when it is heated until it melts.

**Solution:**
When heat energy is supplied to a solid, the particles absorb the energy and **vibrate more vigorously**. As temperature rises, the vibrations increase until the particles have enough energy to **break free from their fixed positions** and begin to slide past each other. At the **melting point**, the solid becomes liquid — the particles are still close together but no longer in a rigid, ordered arrangement. They can now move freely, giving the liquid its ability to flow and take the shape of its container.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests properties of the three states, changes of state with energy changes, and diffusion vs osmosis.

> **Common trap:** During a change of state (e.g., at the exact melting point), temperature does NOT change even though heat is being added. This heat is called **latent heat** and is used to break the bonds between particles, not to raise temperature.

# TOPIC 4: Energy — Types, Sources and Conversion
**Topic ID:** JSS-BST-002-02

## 4.1 Definition of Energy

**Energy** is the capacity or ability to do work. It exists in many forms and can be transferred from one form to another, but it cannot be created or destroyed — this is the **Law of Conservation of Energy**.

---

## 4.2 Forms (Types) of Energy

| Form | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Kinetic energy** | Energy of motion | Moving car, running water |
| **Potential energy** | Stored energy due to position | Raised object, compressed spring |
| **Thermal (heat) energy** | Energy from temperature | Fire, hot water |
| **Light (radiant) energy** | Energy from electromagnetic radiation | Sunlight, light bulb |
| **Sound energy** | Energy carried by vibrations | Music, thunder |
| **Electrical energy** | Energy from electric charges in motion | Electricity from power sockets |
| **Chemical energy** | Energy stored in chemical bonds | Food, fuel, batteries |
| **Nuclear energy** | Energy from atomic nucleus | Nuclear power stations |

---

## 4.3 Sources of Energy

### Non-Renewable (Fossil Fuels):
- Petroleum (crude oil), natural gas, coal
- Formed over millions of years from dead organisms
- Will eventually run out; produce carbon dioxide when burned

### Renewable (Alternative) Energy Sources:
| Source | How it works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Solar energy** | Sunlight converted to electricity by solar panels |
| **Wind energy** | Wind turns turbines to generate electricity |
| **Hydroelectric** | Flowing water spins turbines in dams |
| **Biomass** | Burning plant material or animal waste |
| **Geothermal** | Heat from inside the Earth |
| **Tidal/Wave** | Energy from ocean movement |

---

## 4.4 Energy Conversion

Energy is constantly being converted from one form to another.

| Conversion | Example |
|------------|---------|
| Chemical → Thermal | Burning wood; food metabolism |
| Chemical → Electrical | Battery |
| Electrical → Light + Thermal | Light bulb |
| Electrical → Kinetic | Electric motor, fan |
| Light → Chemical | Photosynthesis in plants |
| Kinetic → Electrical | Generator, dynamo |
| Potential → Kinetic | Ball falling from a height |

**Energy is never 100% efficient** — some energy is always "lost" as heat in conversions.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Give the energy conversions that occur when a torch (flashlight) is switched on.

**Solution:**
When a torch is switched on:
1. **Chemical energy** (stored in the battery) → **Electrical energy** (as current flows through the circuit)
2. **Electrical energy** → **Light energy** + **Thermal (heat) energy** (in the bulb)

So the overall conversion is: Chemical → Electrical → Light + Heat

Note: The heat produced is "wasted" energy — this is why torches (especially old incandescent ones) get warm with use.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests forms of energy, renewable vs non-renewable sources, and energy conversion chains.

> **Common trap:** Potential energy is stored energy — an object does NOT need to be moving to have energy. A stone at the top of a hill has more gravitational potential energy than one at the bottom, even though neither is moving.

# TOPIC 5: Ecosystems — Habitats, Food Chains and Cycles
**Topic ID:** JSS-BST-003-01

## 5.1 Definition of an Ecosystem

An **ecosystem** is a community of living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms) interacting with each other and with their non-living environment (water, air, soil, temperature, light) as a system.

---

## 5.2 Components of an Ecosystem

| Component | Type | Description |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| **Biotic** | Living | All organisms — plants, animals, fungi, bacteria |
| **Abiotic** | Non-living | Temperature, water, light, soil, air, minerals |
| **Producers** | Biotic | Plants; make food through photosynthesis |
| **Consumers** | Biotic | Animals; eat producers or other consumers |
| **Decomposers** | Biotic | Fungi and bacteria; break down dead organisms |

**Types of consumers:**
- **Herbivores** — eat only plants (primary consumers) e.g., grasshopper, cow
- **Carnivores** — eat only animals (secondary/tertiary consumers) e.g., eagle, lion
- **Omnivores** — eat both plants and animals e.g., human, rat

---

## 5.3 Food Chains and Food Webs

**Food chain:** Shows the flow of energy from one organism to another in a single, linear sequence.

Format: Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary consumer → Tertiary consumer

**Example:**
Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle

**Food web:** A network of many interconnected food chains, giving a more realistic picture of feeding relationships in an ecosystem.

### Trophic Levels:
| Level | Organism | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| 1st (producers) | Plants | Grass, algae |
| 2nd (primary consumers) | Herbivores | Grasshopper |
| 3rd (secondary consumers) | Carnivores | Frog |
| 4th (tertiary consumers) | Top carnivores | Eagle |

---

## 5.4 Energy Flow in Ecosystems

**Only about 10% of energy** is transferred from one trophic level to the next. The rest is lost as heat through respiration, movement, and metabolic processes.

This means:
- Fewer organisms can be supported at higher trophic levels
- An energy pyramid is broad at the base (producers) and narrow at the top (top carnivores)

---

## 5.5 Nutrient Cycles

### The Water (Hydrological) Cycle:
Evaporation (water → water vapour) → Transpiration (from plants) → Condensation (water vapour → clouds) → Precipitation (rain/snow) → Runoff/infiltration → back to oceans/groundwater

### The Carbon Cycle:
- CO₂ in atmosphere → absorbed by plants during **photosynthesis** → stored in plant tissues
- Animals eat plants → carbon in animal bodies
- Respiration by plants and animals releases CO₂ back to atmosphere
- Decomposition of dead organisms → CO₂ released
- Burning fossil fuels → large amounts of CO₂ released (greenhouse effect)

### The Nitrogen Cycle:
- N₂ in atmosphere → **nitrogen fixation** by bacteria → ammonia/nitrates in soil
- Plants absorb nitrates → animals eat plants
- **Decomposition** of dead organisms → ammonia → nitrates
- **Denitrifying bacteria** → N₂ released back to atmosphere

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Construct a food chain of FOUR organisms from a grassland ecosystem and identify the producer and each consumer.

**Solution:**
**Food chain:**
Grass → Grasshopper → Lizard → Hawk

| Organism | Role | Type |
|---------|------|------|
| Grass | Producer | Makes own food by photosynthesis |
| Grasshopper | Primary consumer | Herbivore — eats grass |
| Lizard | Secondary consumer | Carnivore — eats grasshopper |
| Hawk | Tertiary consumer | Carnivore — eats lizard |

The arrows show the direction of **energy flow** (not "what eats what" — the arrow points FROM food TO feeder).

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests constructing food chains/webs, identifying trophic levels, the 10% energy rule, and key stages of the carbon and water cycles.

> **Common trap:** The arrow in a food chain shows the direction of ENERGY FLOW, not "eating direction" per se. Grass → Grasshopper means energy flows FROM grass TO grasshopper (i.e., grasshopper eats grass). Never reverse the arrows.
