## Principles and Concepts of Marketing

### Introduction to Marketing and the Marketing Mix
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MKT-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define marketing and explain its core concepts
- Distinguish between selling and marketing orientations
- Identify and explain the 4Ps of the marketing mix
- Explain market segmentation, targeting and positioning
- Analyse how Nigerian businesses use the marketing mix

#### 📖 Notes
- Marketing: the process of identifying, creating, communicating and delivering value to satisfy customer needs profitably
- Core concepts: needs (basic human requirements), wants (needs shaped by culture/personality), demand (wants backed by purchasing power), product, value, exchange, market
- Production orientation: focuses on making products efficiently; assumes demand exists
- Sales orientation: focuses on aggressive selling to push products already made
- Marketing orientation: focuses on customer needs FIRST, then producing what satisfies them
- Societal marketing orientation: considers long-term societal welfare as well as customer satisfaction and profit
- 4Ps of marketing mix: Product, Price, Place, Promotion
- Product: what is offered (goods, services, ideas); product levels (core benefit, tangible product, augmented product); product life cycle (introduction, growth, maturity, decline)
- Price: amount paid; pricing strategies (penetration, skimming, competitive, cost-plus, psychological pricing)
- Place (distribution): how product reaches customer; channels, logistics, wholesalers, retailers, e-commerce
- Promotion: communicating with target market; advertising, sales promotion, public relations, personal selling, direct marketing
- Market segmentation: dividing market into distinct groups; bases: demographic (age, gender, income), geographic (region), psychographic (lifestyle), behavioural (usage rate)
- Target market: the segment(s) the company decides to serve
- Positioning: how customers perceive the product relative to competitors; positioning statement

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Analyse a popular Nigerian product (e.g. Indomie noodles, Bigi soft drink) using the 4Ps framework; present findings
2. Identify the market segment for three different products: luxury cars, sachet water, and university textbooks
3. Design a simple marketing plan for a new jollof rice restaurant in your school's neighbourhood

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests the 4Ps with specific examples, orientation types (production vs. marketing), and market segmentation bases
> Common question: 'Give two elements of the marketing mix for a beverage company' — know Product, Price, Place and Promotion with Nigerian examples
> Trap: confusing 'selling' (pushing what is made) with 'marketing' (making what customers want) — these represent different business philosophies

### Consumer Behaviour and Market Research
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MKT-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Explain the consumer buying decision process
- Identify factors that influence consumer behaviour
- Define market research and describe its process
- Distinguish between primary and secondary research methods
- Interpret simple market research data

#### 📖 Notes
- Consumer buying decision process: problem recognition → information search → evaluation of alternatives → purchase decision → post-purchase evaluation
- Cultural factors: culture, subculture, social class influence buying behaviour
- Social factors: reference groups (family, friends, celebrities), social roles, status
- Personal factors: age, occupation, income, lifestyle, personality
- Psychological factors: motivation (Maslow's hierarchy of needs), perception, learning, beliefs and attitudes
- Maslow's hierarchy: physiological → safety → social/belonging → esteem → self-actualization
- B2C (business to consumer) vs. B2B (business to business) buying decisions — B2B is more rational, formal, longer process
- Market research: systematic gathering, recording and analysis of data about market, customers, and competition
- Primary research: new data collected for specific purpose; surveys, interviews, observation, experiments, focus groups
- Secondary research: existing data; government statistics, trade journals, company reports, internet databases
- Survey methods: questionnaire (postal, online, personal interview), telephone surveys
- Sampling: population vs. sample; random, stratified, quota, cluster sampling
- Data analysis: frequency distribution, averages, graphs; qualitative vs. quantitative data

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Design a 10-question questionnaire to find out students' preference between three snack brands; conduct the survey and tabulate results
2. Role-play: a Lagos-based cosmetics company wants to launch a new product for young women; design the market research plan (objectives, method, sample, analysis)
3. Analyse a provided market research report; identify three key insights and suggest two marketing actions based on the findings

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests the five-step buying decision process, factors influencing consumer behaviour, and primary vs. secondary research distinction
> Know Maslow's hierarchy by name and order — questions may ask which level is being addressed by a specific marketing appeal
> Trap: 'secondary research' does not mean 'second-best' — it refers to data ALREADY collected; primary is NEW data you collect yourself

## Product, Pricing and Distribution

### Product Development, Branding and Pricing Strategies
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MKT-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the product life cycle and marketing implications at each stage
- Explain branding, packaging and labelling decisions
- Distinguish between pricing strategies and their appropriate contexts
- Explain how pricing objectives influence pricing decisions
- Analyse pricing decisions for a Nigerian product

#### 📖 Notes
- Product: anything offered to the market that might satisfy a want or need; tangible goods, services, ideas, experiences, persons, places
- Product levels: core (fundamental benefit), actual/tangible (physical features, brand, packaging), augmented (after-sales service, warranty, delivery)
- Product life cycle (PLC): Introduction (low sales, high cost, no profit), Growth (rapid sales increase, profits emerge), Maturity (sales peak, competitive pricing), Decline (falling sales, reduce costs or withdraw)
- New product development stages: idea generation, idea screening, concept testing, business analysis, product development, test marketing, commercialisation
- Brand: name, symbol, design identifying a seller's product; brand equity — value of brand recognition
- Branding decisions: individual brand (each product has own name), family brand (all products under one name), co-branding
- Packaging: contains, protects, promotes and provides information; secondary packaging for display
- Labelling: brand name, ingredients, weight, instructions, expiry date, manufacturer's details
- Pricing objectives: profit maximisation, market share growth, survival (in crisis), quality leadership
- Cost-based pricing: cost-plus (add markup to total cost); break-even pricing
- Demand-based pricing: high demand → higher price; psychological pricing (₦999 instead of ₦1,000)
- Competition-based pricing: match, undercut or price above competitors
- Penetration pricing: very low price to gain market share quickly; used for new products in competitive markets
- Price skimming: high initial price, gradually reduced; used for innovative products with few competitors

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Plot the product life cycle of Nokia mobile phones from 1995 to 2020, identifying which stage applies in 2000, 2007, 2012, and 2020
2. Compare the branding strategy of Dangote Group (family brand) with Procter & Gamble Nigeria (individual brands for Ariel, Pampers, etc.)
3. Calculate the break-even price for a student selling zobo drinks: ₦5,000 fixed cost, ₦200 per bottle variable cost, aiming to sell 100 bottles

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests PLC stages with characteristics, pricing strategy identification in scenarios, and branding types
> Know: penetration pricing = low initial price (to grow market share); price skimming = high initial price (for innovation)
> Trap: students confuse stages of PLC — in the DECLINE stage, profits fall and companies must decide to reinvest, reformulate or withdraw
