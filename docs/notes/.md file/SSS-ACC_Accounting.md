## Principles and Concepts of Accounting

### Accounting Concepts, Conventions and Double Entry
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-ACC-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define accounting and state its objectives
- Identify and explain key accounting concepts and conventions
- Apply the double-entry principle to record business transactions
- Distinguish between assets, liabilities, capital, income and expenses
- Prepare journal entries and post to ledger accounts

#### 📖 Notes
- Accounting: systematic recording, classifying, summarising and communicating financial information
- Purpose: decision making (management), taxation, reporting to investors, regulatory compliance
- Accounting concepts: entity (business separate from owner), going concern (business will continue), accrual (record when earned/incurred not when cash received/paid), consistency (same methods year to year), prudence (caution; recognise losses immediately, profits only when certain), materiality (significant items only), matching (expenses matched to revenue they generate)
- Accounting equation: Assets = Capital + Liabilities (or A = C + L)
- Double-entry principle: every transaction has two equal and opposite effects; debit one account, credit another
- Debit: increases assets and expenses; decreases liabilities, capital and income
- Credit: increases liabilities, capital and income; decreases assets and expenses
- DEAD CLIC rule: Debit = Expenses, Assets, Drawings; Credit = Liabilities, Income, Capital
- Types of accounts: real (assets — physical), personal (debtors/creditors — persons), nominal (income/expenses — revenue)
- Journal: book of original entry; records transactions chronologically with narration
- Ledger: book of secondary entry; contains all accounts; grouped in sales, purchase, general ledger
- Posting: transferring journal entries to the appropriate ledger accounts

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Record 10 transactions in a journal including purchases, sales, expenses and drawings; post to T-accounts in the ledger
2. Analyse: 'Owner puts ₦500,000 into business' — identify which accounts are affected; debit and credit sides
3. Class exercise: from a list of 20 accounts, classify each as asset, liability, capital, income or expense

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests journal entries, ledger posting, identification of debit/credit effects, and accounting concepts in scenario questions
> JAMB tests DEAD CLIC rules, accounting concept identification, and the accounting equation
> Trap: students debit and credit in reverse — use the DEAD CLIC rule consistently and practise until it is instinctive

### Books of Original Entry — Subsidiary Books
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-ACC-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify and explain the function of each subsidiary book
- Record credit transactions in the appropriate subsidiary book
- Total and post subsidiary book totals to the ledger
- Prepare a petty cash book using the imprest system
- Balance and interpret a cash book with bank and cash columns

#### 📖 Notes
- Subsidiary books (books of prime entry): Sales Day Book, Purchases Day Book, Sales Returns (Returns Inward) Book, Purchases Returns (Returns Outward) Book, Bills Receivable Book, Bills Payable Book, Journal Proper (for non-routine items), Cash Book
- Sales Day Book: records all credit sales; columns for date, customer, invoice no., amount; total posted to Sales Account (Cr)
- Purchases Day Book: records all credit purchases; total posted to Purchases Account (Dr)
- Returns Inward Book: goods returned by customers; total posted to Returns Inward Account (Dr)
- Returns Outward Book: goods returned to suppliers; total posted to Returns Outward Account (Cr)
- Cash Book: records all receipts and payments in cash and/or bank; acts as both journal AND ledger for cash/bank
- Two-column cash book: cash column and bank column; contra entries (C) when cash withdrawn from bank or deposited
- Three-column cash book: adds discount allowed (Dr) and discount received (Cr) columns
- Petty Cash Book: records small expenses; analysed columns (postage, stationery, sundries, etc.)
- Imprest system: petty cashier given a fixed amount (float); reimbursed by the difference spent at end of period
- Bank reconciliation: explains difference between cash book bank balance and bank statement balance; timing differences (unpresented cheques, uncredited deposits)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Record a week's transactions in the appropriate subsidiary books; total each book and identify the ledger postings
2. Prepare a three-column cash book from a list of transactions and balance it at month-end
3. Given a cash book balance of ₦12,500 and a bank statement balance of ₦15,000 with two unpresented cheques (₦1,000 and ₦1,500) and one uncleared deposit (₦500), prepare a bank reconciliation statement

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests recording in the correct subsidiary book, cash book balancing with contra entries, and bank reconciliation
> JAMB tests book identification from transaction type and imprest petty cash top-up calculation
> Trap: forgetting contra entries in the cash book when cash is deposited to bank or withdrawn from bank — these appear on BOTH sides of the cash book

## Final Accounts

### Trial Balance and Final Accounts of a Sole Trader
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-ACC-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Extract a trial balance from ledger accounts
- Prepare a trading account to calculate gross profit
- Prepare a profit and loss account to calculate net profit
- Prepare a balance sheet to show financial position
- Make adjustments for accruals, prepayments, depreciation and bad debts

#### 📖 Notes
- Trial balance: list of all ledger account balances; debits = credits if correctly posted; detects arithmetic errors
- Errors NOT detected by trial balance: errors of omission, commission, principle, compensating errors, reversal of entries
- Trading Account: Sales − Cost of Goods Sold = Gross Profit; COGS = Opening Stock + Purchases − Returns Out − Closing Stock
- Profit and Loss Account: Gross Profit + Other Income − Expenses = Net Profit (or Net Loss)
- Balance Sheet: not an account; snapshot of financial position at a specific date; Assets = Capital + Liabilities
- Current assets: cash, bank, debtors, stock, prepayments (expected to be converted to cash within 1 year)
- Non-current (fixed) assets: land, buildings, machinery, motor vehicles (used for more than 1 year)
- Current liabilities: creditors, bank overdraft, accruals, short-term loans (due within 1 year)
- Non-current liabilities: long-term loans, mortgage (due after 1 year)
- Capital section: opening capital + net profit − drawings = closing capital
- Accruals: expenses incurred but not yet paid — add to expense in P&L, show as current liability in balance sheet
- Prepayments: expenses paid in advance — deduct from expense in P&L, show as current asset in balance sheet
- Depreciation: systematic reduction in value of fixed assets; straight-line (fixed amount each year), reducing balance (fixed percentage); charged as expense in P&L; reduces fixed asset in balance sheet

#### ✏️ Activities
1. From a given list of account balances, prepare a full set of final accounts (Trading A/c, P&L A/c, Balance Sheet) for a sole trader
2. Make adjustments: given accrued wages of ₦5,000, prepaid rent of ₦2,000, and a machine depreciated at 20% on cost, adjust the final accounts
3. Interpret a balance sheet: calculate current ratio, quick ratio, and working capital; comment on the business's liquidity

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC questions always include adjustments — memorise: accruals ADD to expense (P&L) and appear as creditor (Balance Sheet); prepayments DEDUCT from expense and appear as debtor
> JAMB tests classification of items into correct balance sheet sections, gross profit calculation, and depreciation calculation
> Trap: forgetting to carry closing stock into the balance sheet as a current asset after it has been used in the trading account
