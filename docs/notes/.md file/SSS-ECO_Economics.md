## Introduction to Economics

### Fundamental Economic Concepts and Economic Systems
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-ECO-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define economics and explain the basic economic problem of scarcity and choice
- Distinguish between microeconomics and macroeconomics
- Explain opportunity cost and the production possibility curve
- Compare the three main economic systems (free market, planned, mixed)
- Apply the concept of scale of preference to decision-making

#### 📖 Notes
- Economics: the study of how individuals and societies allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants
- Scarcity: resources (land, labour, capital, enterprise) are limited; wants are unlimited
- The three basic economic questions: What to produce? How to produce? For whom to produce?
- Scale of preference: ranking of wants in order of priority; basis of rational decision-making
- Opportunity cost: the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made; not just money — it is the best alternative given up
- Production possibility curve (PPC/PPF): shows maximum combinations of two goods produceable with given resources; points on curve = efficient; inside = inefficient; outside = currently unattainable
- Microeconomics: study of individual units — households, firms, markets, prices
- Macroeconomics: study of the economy as a whole — GDP, inflation, unemployment, balance of payments
- Free market (capitalist) economy: private ownership; price mechanism allocates resources; Adam Smith's 'invisible hand'; pros: efficiency, choice; cons: inequality, market failures
- Planned (command/socialist) economy: government owns/controls production; central planning; pros: equality; cons: inefficiency, lack of incentives
- Mixed economy: combination of free market and government intervention; Nigeria operates a mixed economy
- Factors of production: Land (natural resources, reward = rent), Labour (human effort, reward = wages), Capital (manufactured resources, reward = interest), Enterprise/Entrepreneurship (organising the other three, reward = profit)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Draw a production possibility curve for Nigeria choosing between oil production and food production; identify the opportunity cost of increasing oil production
2. Class survey: each student lists their scale of preference for spending ₦5,000 pocket money; compare and discuss individual vs. group preferences
3. Debate: 'A free market economy is better for Nigeria than a mixed economy' — use real examples from Nigerian economic policy

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests PPC interpretation, opportunity cost definition and calculation, and comparison of economic systems
> JAMB tests scale of preference, factor of production classification, and free vs. planned economy characteristics
> Trap: opportunity cost is NOT the monetary cost — it is the specific best alternative foregone; always state WHAT was given up, not just 'the cost'

## Demand, Supply and Market Equilibrium

### Demand and Supply Theory
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-ECO-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define demand and supply and state their laws
- Distinguish between change in quantity demanded and change in demand
- Identify and explain the factors that shift demand and supply curves
- Determine market equilibrium price and quantity graphically and algebraically
- Explain and calculate price, income and cross elasticities of demand

#### 📖 Notes
- Demand: quantity of a good consumers are willing and able to buy at a given price in a given time period
- Law of demand: as price rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship); ceteris paribus
- Demand curve: downward sloping; moves along curve (price change); shifts when other factors change
- Determinants of demand (shift factors): income, prices of related goods, tastes, expectations, number of buyers
- Normal goods (income up → demand up); inferior goods (income up → demand down); Giffen goods (price up → demand up — very rare)
- Supply: quantity producers are willing and able to sell at a given price
- Law of supply: as price rises, quantity supplied rises (positive relationship); ceteris paribus
- Supply curve: upward sloping; determinants: cost of production, technology, taxes/subsidies, number of sellers, climate (agricultural goods)
- Market equilibrium: where demand = supply; price clears the market; excess demand (shortage) pushes price up; excess supply (surplus) pushes price down
- Algebraically: set Qd = Qs to find equilibrium P and Q
- Price elasticity of demand (PED): % change in Qd / % change in P; elastic (|PED|>1) vs. inelastic (|PED|<1); unit elastic (|PED|=1)
- Determinants of PED: substitutes available, necessity vs. luxury, proportion of income, time period
- Income elasticity of demand (YED): % ΔQd / % ΔY; positive for normal goods, negative for inferior goods
- Cross elasticity of demand (XED): % ΔQd of A / % ΔP of B; positive = substitutes; negative = complements

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Draw demand and supply curves for tomatoes in Lagos; shift the supply curve leftward (drought); find new equilibrium and explain the price change
2. Given Qd = 100 − 2P and Qs = 20 + 3P, find equilibrium price and quantity algebraically, then graph both curves
3. Calculate PED for a product where price rises from ₦50 to ₦60 and quantity demanded falls from 200 to 160 units; interpret the result for a business

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests demand/supply diagram drawing, shift factor identification, equilibrium calculation, and elasticity classification from PED values
> JAMB tests law of demand/supply, determinant identification, and PED/YED/XED sign interpretation
> Trap: a 'change in demand' (curve shifts) is different from 'change in quantity demanded' (movement along same curve) — price causes movement; other factors cause shifts

## National Income and Macroeconomic Issues

### National Income — GDP, GNP and Measurement
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-ECO-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define national income and related concepts (GDP, GNP, NNP, per capita income)
- Describe the three methods of measuring national income
- Explain the circular flow of income model
- Identify difficulties in measuring national income in Nigeria
- Interpret national income data for comparing living standards

#### 📖 Notes
- National income: total monetary value of all goods and services produced in a country in a year
- GDP (Gross Domestic Product): value of all final goods/services produced within a country's borders in a year
- GNP (Gross National Product): GDP + net factor income from abroad (income of citizens abroad − income of foreigners locally)
- NNP (Net National Product): GNP − depreciation (capital consumption allowance)
- Per capita income: national income / population; used to compare living standards between countries
- Expenditure method: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M) [consumption + investment + government spending + net exports]
- Income method: sum of all factor incomes (wages + rent + interest + profit)
- Output method: sum of value added at each stage of production (to avoid double counting)
- Value added: selling price − cost of inputs at each stage
- Circular flow of income: households supply factors to firms; firms pay factor income; households spend on goods; injections (I, G, X) and withdrawals (S, T, M)
- Limitations of national income measurement in Nigeria: subsistence sector (unrecorded), informal economy, non-monetary transactions, uneven distribution (Gini coefficient), quality of life not captured
- GDP per capita vs. HDI (Human Development Index): HDI also considers education and health, not just income
- Real vs. nominal GDP: nominal measured at current prices; real adjusted for inflation using GDP deflator

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Calculate Nigeria's GDP using expenditure method given: C=₦85tr, I=₦20tr, G=₦18tr, X=₦30tr, M=₦25tr
2. Compare GDP per capita vs. HDI rankings for 5 countries; explain why a high-GDP country may rank lower on HDI
3. Identify 5 economic activities in your local market not captured in official Nigerian GDP figures; explain why

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests GDP/GNP distinction, the expenditure formula C+I+G+(X−M), and limitations of national income measurement in Nigeria
> JAMB tests definition matching, GDP calculation from given data, and circular flow diagram interpretation
> Trap: GDP vs. GNP — GDP is where production occurs (within borders); GNP is who produces (citizens, wherever they are)

### Inflation, Unemployment and Economic Development
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-ECO-003-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define and measure inflation using the Consumer Price Index
- Identify types and causes of inflation
- Distinguish between types of unemployment and their causes
- Describe the characteristics of developing economies like Nigeria
- Evaluate government policies to control inflation and unemployment

#### 📖 Notes
- Inflation: general and sustained rise in the price level; reduces purchasing power of money
- Consumer Price Index (CPI): measures change in price of a basket of goods; inflation rate = (CPI_current − CPI_base)/CPI_base × 100
- Types of inflation: creeping (<3% — mild), walking (3–10%), galloping (10–50%), hyperinflation (>50%)
- Demand-pull inflation: too much money chasing too few goods; AD > AS; common when government prints money
- Cost-push inflation: supply-side shocks (rising wages, raw material cost, oil prices) push prices up
- Stagflation: simultaneous high inflation AND high unemployment (worst scenario for policymakers)
- Effects of inflation: erodes savings, helps debtors, hurts fixed income earners, reduces exports competitiveness
- Unemployment: economically active people who are willing and able to work but cannot find jobs
- Types: frictional (between jobs), structural (mismatch of skills), cyclical (recession), seasonal (agricultural/tourism)
- Unemployment in Nigeria: graduate unemployment, rural-urban migration, skill mismatch, oil dependency
- Government policies: monetary policy (CBN sets interest rates, money supply), fiscal policy (taxation, government spending), supply-side policies (education, infrastructure investment)
- Economic development vs. growth: growth = increase in GDP; development = increase in living standards, education, health and equality
- Characteristics of underdevelopment: low per capita income, high population growth, subsistence agriculture, low industrialisation, high dependence on primary commodities

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Track the Nigerian inflation rate for the past 6 months using NBS data; plot a graph and identify the trend
2. Calculate CPI if a basket costing ₦5,000 in base year now costs ₦6,200; calculate the inflation rate
3. Role-play: the CBN Monetary Policy Committee decides whether to raise or cut interest rates in response to 25% inflation — argue both sides

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests inflation types, CPI calculation, unemployment types, and government anti-inflation policies
> JAMB tests inflation definition, demand-pull vs. cost-push distinction, and unemployment type identification
> Trap: inflation rate measures the RATE OF CHANGE of prices, not the absolute price level — prices rising at 10% means inflation is 10%, not that prices rose by ₦10
