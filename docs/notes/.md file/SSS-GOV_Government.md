## Basic Concepts of Government

### The State, Sovereignty and Government Systems
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-GOV-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define the state, government and politics
- Explain the elements of the state (population, territory, government, sovereignty)
- Distinguish between systems of government (democracy, authoritarianism, military)
- Compare federalism, unitary and confederal systems
- Explain the functions of government and limits on governmental power

#### 📖 Notes
- State: politically organized community occupying a definite territory with a government and sovereignty
- Four elements of the state: population (people), territory (defined area), government (authority to rule), sovereignty (supreme power, internal and external)
- Government: institutions and processes through which the state exercises authority
- Functions of government: making laws, enforcing laws, adjudicating disputes, maintaining security, providing public goods, managing the economy
- Democracy: rule by the people; direct (ancient Athens) and representative/indirect (modern states)
- Principles of democracy: majority rule, minority rights, rule of law, separation of powers, free and fair elections, civilian supremacy
- Authoritarianism: concentrated power; limited political pluralism; Nigeria's military regimes (Yakubu Gowon, Murtala, Buhari/Idiagbon, Babangida, Abacha)
- Federalism: division of powers between central and state governments; written constitution; Nigeria, USA, India are federal
- Unitary system: central government supreme; local governments derive powers from centre; France, UK
- Confederal system: loose alliance of independent states; central authority very weak; e.g. Confederation of 1781 (USA before Constitution)
- Separation of powers: executive, legislature and judiciary exercise different powers; prevents tyranny (Montesquieu)
- Checks and balances: each arm limits the other's power; e.g. legislature approves executive appointments; judiciary can declare legislation unconstitutional

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Create a comparison table for federal, unitary and confederal systems on 5 criteria: location of sovereignty, examples, constitutional arrangement, pros, cons
2. Map all military heads of state in Nigeria from independence to 1999; identify the coup dates and reasons given
3. Debate: 'Nigerian democracy has failed the ordinary citizen since 1999' — argue pro and con using evidence

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests elements of the state, differences between federalism and unitary systems, functions of government, and separation of powers
> JAMB tests definitions, federalism characteristics, and principles of democracy — memorise the four elements of the state
> Trap: sovereignty is the MOST important element of the state — without sovereignty, the state cannot exist (unlike population alone, which could just be a group of people)

## Nigerian Government and Politics

### Nigerian Constitution and the Three Tiers of Government
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-GOV-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the features and significance of the Nigerian Constitution
- Identify and explain the three tiers and three arms of government in Nigeria
- Analyse the distribution of powers under the 1999 Constitution
- Explain the roles of INEC, judiciary and civil service in Nigerian governance
- Evaluate the practice of federalism in Nigeria

#### 📖 Notes
- Constitution: fundamental law of the land; supreme over all other laws in Nigeria; 1999 Constitution (as amended)
- Features of Nigerian constitution: written (codified), rigid (requires special procedure to amend), supreme (other laws are void if inconsistent), federal
- Constitutional supremacy: Section 1 of 1999 Constitution declares it supreme
- Three tiers: Federal (Abuja), State (36 states + FCT), Local Government (774 LGAs)
- Three arms of government: Executive (President/Governor/Chairman — implements laws), Legislature (National Assembly/State House of Assembly/Legislative Council — makes laws), Judiciary (Federal Supreme Court/State High Court — interprets laws)
- Exclusive legislative list: only Federal Government can legislate (defence, immigration, customs, currency, aviation)
- Concurrent list: both Federal and State can legislate; federal law prevails in conflict (education, health, agriculture)
- Residual powers: what is not listed goes to states
- INEC (Independent National Electoral Commission): conducts federal and state elections; registers voters and parties; established by Section 153 of 1999 Constitution
- Revenue allocation: FAAC (Federal Account Allocation Committee); sharing formula: Federal (52.68%), States (26.72%), LGA (20.60%) as of recent revisions
- Challenges of Nigerian federalism: fiscal federalism imbalance, derivation principle debates, state creation, rotational presidency

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Map the exclusive, concurrent and residual legislative lists from the 1999 Constitution; classify 20 functions into the correct list
2. Trace how a bill becomes law in Nigeria: from introduction in the House of Representatives through Senate, President assent, to gazette
3. Case study: evaluate how INEC managed the 2023 General Elections — what worked, what failed, and what reforms are needed

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests exclusive vs. concurrent legislative lists with examples, features of Nigerian constitution, and INEC's roles
> JAMB tests powers of each tier of government, constitutional supremacy, and arm of government functions
> Trap: only FEDERAL government can legislate on the EXCLUSIVE list; both can legislate on the CONCURRENT list — states cannot touch exclusive matters

### Political Parties and Electoral Process in Nigeria
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-GOV-002-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define a political party and explain its functions
- Trace the history of political parties in Nigeria from 1923 to present
- Explain the Nigerian electoral process from registration to declaration of results
- Evaluate the challenges of elections in Nigeria
- Explain the role of pressure groups and civil society

#### 📖 Notes
- Political party: organized group of people with similar political views seeking to win power through elections
- Functions of political parties: political education, recruitment of political leaders, forming government, aggregating interests, political socialization
- History of Nigerian political parties: NNDP (1923 — Macaulay), NYM (1934), NCNC (1944 — Azikiwe), AG (1950 — Awolowo), NPC (1949 — Bello); 2nd Republic: NPN, UPN, NPP, GNPP, PRP; 3rd Republic: NRC, SDP; 4th Republic: PDP (dominant 1999–2015), APC (formed 2013, won 2015), others (LP, NNPP)
- Party system types: one-party (authoritarian), two-party (USA, UK), multi-party (Nigeria, Germany)
- Electoral process: voter registration → party primaries → campaign → polling day → collation → declaration → petition (if any)
- Types of electoral systems: first-past-the-post (FPTP — Nigeria), proportional representation, alternative vote
- Electoral malpractice: ballot stuffing, voter intimidation, underage voting, result manipulation
- Electoral reforms: Uwais Commission (2008), Independent Candidacy, BVAS (Bimodal Voter Accreditation System) introduced 2023
- Pressure groups: organisations that influence government policy without seeking power; NLC, NBA, NMA, NANS
- Civil society: non-governmental organisations (NGOs), media, religious bodies; watchdogs of democracy

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Create a timeline of Nigerian political parties from 1922 to 2023, identifying founding date, leader, region of support and key elections contested
2. Mock election: class forms two parties, campaigns, votes, and conducts a mini-election using INEC procedures; identify lessons learned
3. Analyse: how did the introduction of BVAS technology in 2023 change the Nigerian electoral experience? Were results more credible?

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests party history (founders, years, regions), functions of political parties, and the electoral process step by step
> JAMB tests party founders, definitions, pressure group vs. political party distinction
> Key names to memorise: Macaulay (NNDP), Azikiwe (NCNC), Awolowo (AG/UPN), Ahmadu Bello (NPC), Balewa (PM 1960)

## International Relations and Organizations

### Nigeria's Foreign Policy and International Organizations
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-GOV-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define foreign policy and explain Nigeria's foreign policy objectives
- Describe Nigeria's role in African and world affairs
- Explain the structure and functions of the United Nations
- Describe the African Union and ECOWAS and their roles
- Evaluate Nigeria's contributions to peacekeeping and international development

#### 📖 Notes
- Foreign policy: a country's strategy and actions toward other nations; guides diplomatic, economic and military relations
- Nigeria's foreign policy principles: African-centred policy (Africa as centrepiece), non-alignment (colonial era), non-interference in internal affairs of states, promotion of democracy and human rights, support for UN/AU/ECOWAS
- Nigeria's foreign policy history: Black Africa centrepiece (Balewa, Gowon era), Southward Look (supporting anti-apartheid), Northward Look, Citizen-centred diplomacy (4th Republic)
- United Nations (UN): established 1945 after WWII; 193 member states; headquarters New York; principal organs: General Assembly, Security Council, Secretariat, ICJ, ECOSOC, Trusteeship Council
- UN Security Council: 5 permanent members (USA, UK, France, Russia, China — P5 with veto power) + 10 rotating members; primary responsibility for international peace
- UN Specialized Agencies: UNESCO, WHO, ILO, UNICEF, FAO, UNHCR, World Bank, IMF
- African Union (AU): successor to OAU; established 2002; headquarters Addis Ababa; 55 members; promotes African unity, peace and development; AU Commission, African Court of Human Rights, NEPAD
- ECOWAS (Economic Community of West African States): established 1975, Lagos; 16 West African nations; promotes economic integration, ECOWAS Passport, ECOMOG peacekeeping
- Nigeria's peacekeeping: ECOMOG in Liberia (1990) and Sierra Leone; UN missions worldwide; largest contributor to African Union peacekeeping
- Commonwealth of Nations: 56 countries; former British empire; promotes democracy, development, rule of law

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Map Nigeria's peacekeeping contributions from 1960 to present: list the countries, years and outcomes of each ECOMOG/UN mission
2. Evaluate Nigeria's 'Africa as centrepiece' foreign policy: three successes (e.g. anti-apartheid support, ECOWAS leadership) and three failures (Bakassi Peninsula cession, brain drain)
3. Role-play a UN Security Council debate on a current African conflict; assign P5 roles and veto power to understand UN decision-making

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests Nigeria's foreign policy principles, UN organs and their functions, ECOWAS objectives, and Nigeria's peacekeeping role
> JAMB tests founding of UN/AU/ECOWAS, Security Council membership (P5), and Nigeria's international role
> Trap: OAU (founded 1963) became the AU (2002) — they are not the same organisation; ECOWAS was founded in 1975, NOT 1963
