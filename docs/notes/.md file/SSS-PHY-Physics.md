# TOPIC 1: Motion, Vectors and Scalars
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Motion

**Motion** is the change in position of a body with respect to time and a reference point. All motion is relative — it must be described from a reference frame.

---

## 1.2 Scalars vs Vectors

| Scalar | Vector |
|--------|--------|
| Has magnitude (size) only | Has both magnitude AND direction |
| Added by simple arithmetic | Added by vector rules |
| Distance, speed, time, mass, energy, temperature | Displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, weight |

---

## 1.3 Types of Motion

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Linear (rectilinear)** | Straight-line motion | Car on a straight road |
| **Circular** | Motion in a circle | Satellite, spinning top |
| **Oscillatory** | Back-and-forth motion | Pendulum |
| **Random** | No fixed pattern | Brownian motion of gas molecules |
| **Projectile** | Under gravity only after launch | Thrown ball, bullet |

---

## 1.4 Key Quantities

| Quantity | Symbol | Definition | Unit |
|----------|--------|-----------|------|
| Distance | d | Total path length | m |
| Displacement | s | Straight-line distance with direction | m |
| Speed | v | Distance ÷ time | m/s |
| Velocity | v | Displacement ÷ time (with direction) | m/s |
| Acceleration | a | Rate of change of velocity | m/s² |
| Deceleration | a | Negative acceleration (slowing down) | m/s² |

---

## 1.5 Equations of Motion (Uniform Acceleration)

For a body with initial velocity **u**, final velocity **v**, acceleration **a**, time **t**, and displacement **s**:

| Equation | Variables covered |
|----------|-----------------|
| **v = u + at** | v, u, a, t |
| **s = ut + ½at²** | s, u, a, t |
| **v² = u² + 2as** | v, u, a, s |
| **s = ½(u + v)t** | s, u, v, t |

---

## 1.6 Motion Under Gravity

**Free fall:** Object falls under gravity alone (air resistance neglected)
- Acceleration due to gravity: **g = 10 m/s²** (WAEC standard)
- For a body thrown upward: at maximum height, v = 0
- Time to max height: t = u/g
- Maximum height: H = u²/2g
- Total time of flight (returns to ground): T = 2u/g

---

## 1.7 Projectile Motion

A projectile has two independent components:
- **Horizontal:** Constant velocity (no acceleration)
- **Vertical:** Uniform acceleration under gravity

| Component | Equation |
|-----------|---------|
| Horizontal distance | x = u cos θ × t |
| Vertical height | y = u sin θ × t − ½gt² |
| Time of flight | T = 2u sin θ / g |
| Maximum range | R = u² sin 2θ / g |
| Maximum height | H = u² sin²θ / 2g |

**Range is maximum when θ = 45°**

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 4 m/s² for 5 seconds. Calculate: (a) the final velocity (b) the distance covered.

**Solution:**
u = 0, a = 4 m/s², t = 5 s

(a) v = u + at = 0 + 4 × 5 = **20 m/s**

(b) s = ut + ½at² = 0(5) + ½(4)(5²) = 0 + ½(4)(25) = **50 m**

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** A ball is thrown vertically upward with velocity 30 m/s. Taking g = 10 m/s², find the maximum height.

**Solution:**
At max height, v = 0; u = 30 m/s; a = −10 m/s²

Using v² = u² + 2as:
0 = (30)² + 2(−10)(s)
20s = 900
s = **45 m**

---

**Example 3 (JAMB type):** A projectile is fired at 45° to the horizontal with speed 20 m/s. Calculate its range. (g = 10 m/s²)

**Solution:**
R = u² sin 2θ / g = (20)² × sin 90° / 10 = 400 × 1 / 10 = **40 m**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests the equations of motion heavily. Memorise all four equations and know which to apply.

> **JAMB** favours projectile motion (range and max height) and free-fall calculations.

> **Common trap:** Mixing up distance (scalar) and displacement (vector). Distance is always positive; displacement can be negative (opposite direction).

> **Common trap:** In free fall going UP, acceleration is −10 m/s² (opposing motion). In free fall going DOWN, acceleration is +10 m/s² (in direction of motion).

# TOPIC 2: Newton's Laws of Motion
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-001-02

## 2.1 Definition

**Newton's Laws of Motion** are three fundamental laws formulated by Sir Isaac Newton (1687) that describe the relationship between a body and the forces acting on it, and the body's resulting motion.

---

## 2.2 The Three Laws

| Law | Statement | Mathematical Form |
|-----|-----------|-----------------|
| **First Law (Law of Inertia)** | A body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force | ΣF = 0 → a = 0 |
| **Second Law** | The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and acts in the direction of that force | F = ma |
| **Third Law** | For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction | F₁₂ = −F₂₁ |

---

## 2.3 Key Concepts Related to Newton's Laws

**Inertia:** The resistance of a body to change its state of rest or motion. Mass is the measure of inertia.

**Momentum (p):** p = mv (unit: kg m/s)
- A vector quantity

**Impulse (J):** J = Ft = Δp (change in momentum)
- Impulse = Force × time
- Unit: N·s = kg m/s

**Conservation of Momentum:**
In a closed system, total momentum before = total momentum after
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

---

## 2.4 Types of Collision

| Type | Definition | Kinetic Energy |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| **Elastic** | Bodies separate after collision | KE conserved |
| **Inelastic** | Bodies deform; some KE lost | KE NOT conserved |
| **Perfectly inelastic** | Bodies stick together and move as one | Maximum KE lost |

For **perfectly inelastic:** m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v

---

## 2.5 Weight and Mass

| Property | Mass | Weight |
|----------|------|--------|
| Definition | Amount of matter | Gravitational force on a body |
| Unit | kg | N (Newtons) |
| Formula | — | W = mg |
| Varies with location? | No | Yes (g varies) |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A force of 30 N acts on a 6 kg object. Calculate the acceleration produced.

**Solution:**
F = ma → a = F/m = 30/6 = **5 m/s²**

---

**Example 2 (WAEC type):** A 4 kg ball moving at 6 m/s collides with a stationary 2 kg ball. After collision, the 4 kg ball moves at 2 m/s. Find the velocity of the 2 kg ball after collision.

**Solution:**
Momentum before = Momentum after
(4)(6) + (2)(0) = (4)(2) + (2)v₂
24 = 8 + 2v₂
2v₂ = 16
v₂ = **8 m/s**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests collision problems (both elastic and inelastic), impulse calculations, and application of F = ma.

> **Common trap:** In Newton's Third Law, action and reaction act on DIFFERENT bodies, so they never cancel each other. A book rests on a table — the table pushes up (normal force) but this is NOT Newton's Third Law pair of gravity; the Third Law pair of gravity is the book pulling the Earth upward.

# TOPIC 3: Work, Energy and Power
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-002-01

## 3.1 Work

**Work** is done when a force moves a body through a distance in the direction of the force.

**W = F × d × cos θ**

Where:
- W = work (Joules, J)
- F = force (Newtons, N)
- d = distance (metres, m)
- θ = angle between force and displacement

**Key point:** If force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°), work done = 0 (cos 90° = 0).

---

## 3.2 Energy

**Energy** is the ability to do work. Unit: Joules (J).

### Types of Mechanical Energy:

**Kinetic Energy (KE):** Energy of motion
KE = ½mv²

**Potential Energy (PE):** Stored energy
- Gravitational PE: PE = mgh
- Elastic PE: PE = ½ke² (spring, k = spring constant, e = extension)

### Law of Conservation of Energy:
Energy cannot be created or destroyed — only converted from one form to another.
Total mechanical energy = KE + PE = constant (no friction)

---

## 3.3 Power

**Power** is the rate of doing work.

**P = W/t = Fv**

Where:
- P = power (Watts, W)
- W = work done (J)
- t = time (s)
- v = velocity (m/s)

| Unit | Equivalent |
|------|-----------|
| 1 Watt (W) | 1 J/s |
| 1 kilowatt (kW) | 1000 W |
| 1 horsepower (hp) | 746 W ≈ 750 W |

---

## 3.4 Efficiency

**Efficiency = (Useful output energy / Total input energy) × 100%**

Or: Efficiency = (Power output / Power input) × 100%

A machine is never 100% efficient — some energy is always lost (usually as heat and sound).

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A person lifts a box of mass 10 kg through a vertical height of 2 m in 5 seconds. Calculate: (a) the work done (b) the power developed. (g = 10 m/s²)

**Solution:**
(a) W = F × d = mg × d = 10 × 10 × 2 = **200 J**

(b) P = W/t = 200/5 = **40 W**

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** A car of mass 800 kg moves at 20 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy.

**Solution:**
KE = ½mv² = ½ × 800 × (20)² = ½ × 800 × 400 = **160,000 J = 160 kJ**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests work (with angle), KE, PE, conservation of energy, and efficiency calculations.

> **Common trap:** The angle in W = Fd cos θ is the angle between force and displacement, NOT the angle of incline.

> When a body slides down a frictionless slope: PE lost = KE gained. mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh)

# TOPIC 4: Waves — Properties and Types
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-002-02

## 4.1 Definition of a Wave

A **wave** is a disturbance that transfers energy from one point to another without transferring matter.

---

## 4.2 Types of Waves

### By particle oscillation direction:

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Transverse** | Particle oscillates perpendicular to wave direction | Light, water waves, S-seismic waves |
| **Longitudinal** | Particle oscillates parallel to wave direction | Sound, P-seismic waves |

### By medium requirement:

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Mechanical** | Requires medium to propagate | Sound, water waves |
| **Electromagnetic** | Does NOT need medium (travels in vacuum) | Light, radio, X-rays |

---

## 4.3 Wave Properties and Terms

| Term | Definition | Unit |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Amplitude (A)** | Maximum displacement from equilibrium | m |
| **Wavelength (λ)** | Distance between consecutive identical points (e.g., crest to crest) | m |
| **Frequency (f)** | Number of complete waves per second | Hz |
| **Period (T)** | Time for one complete wave | s |
| **Wave speed (v)** | Speed of wave propagation | m/s |

### Key relationships:
- **v = fλ** (wave speed = frequency × wavelength)
- **T = 1/f** (period = 1/frequency)

---

## 4.4 The Electromagnetic Spectrum

| Type | Wavelength (approximate) | Uses |
|------|------------------------|------|
| Radio waves | 10² to 10⁻¹ m | Broadcasting, communication |
| Microwaves | 10⁻¹ to 10⁻³ m | Radar, microwave ovens |
| Infrared | 10⁻³ to 7×10⁻⁷ m | Thermal imaging, remote controls |
| **Visible light** | 7×10⁻⁷ to 4×10⁻⁷ m | Sight |
| Ultraviolet | 4×10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁸ m | Sterilisation, fluorescence |
| X-rays | 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻¹¹ m | Medical imaging |
| Gamma rays | < 10⁻¹¹ m | Cancer treatment, sterilisation |

**All electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum**

---

## 4.5 Wave Behaviours

| Behaviour | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Reflection** | Wave bounces back from a surface |
| **Refraction** | Wave bends when it changes medium (speed changes) |
| **Diffraction** | Wave spreads when passing through a gap or around an obstacle |
| **Interference** | Superposition of two waves; constructive (crests align) or destructive (crest + trough cancel) |
| **Polarisation** | Vibration restricted to one plane (only transverse waves can be polarised) |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A wave has a frequency of 250 Hz and a wavelength of 1.2 m. Calculate its speed.

**Solution:**
v = fλ = 250 × 1.2 = **300 m/s**

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** Which of the following cannot be polarised?
(a) Light (b) Radio waves (c) Sound (d) Microwaves

**Solution:** (c) **Sound** — Sound is a longitudinal wave; polarisation is only possible for transverse waves.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests wave calculations (v = fλ), EM spectrum ordering, and reflection/refraction laws.

> **JAMB** tests identification of wave types, polarisation, and the EM spectrum.

> **Common trap:** Sound is a longitudinal wave — it CANNOT be polarised.

# TOPIC 5: Sound — Properties and Applications
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-003-01

## 5.1 Nature of Sound

**Sound** is a longitudinal mechanical wave produced by vibrating objects. It requires a material medium (solid, liquid, or gas) — it cannot travel through vacuum.

**Speed of sound:** ~340 m/s in air (at room temperature); faster in solids and liquids.

---

## 5.2 Properties of Sound

| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Pitch** | Highness/lowness of sound; determined by frequency (high frequency = high pitch) |
| **Loudness** | Determined by amplitude of vibration |
| **Timbre (quality)** | Distinctive character of a sound; determined by harmonics/overtones |

---

## 5.3 Echo and Reverberation

**Echo:** A reflected sound that is heard distinctly after the original sound. The ear can distinguish two sounds if they are at least 0.1 s apart.

**Minimum distance for echo:** d = v × t/2 = 340 × 0.1/2 = **17 m**

**Reverberation:** Multiple reflections of sound in a closed space causing sound to persist.

---

## 5.4 Resonance

**Resonance** occurs when a body vibrates at its natural frequency due to receiving energy from another vibrating body at the same frequency.

**Resonance tube (closed pipe):**
- Fundamental frequency: f = v/4L
- Harmonics (overtones): only ODD multiples — 3f, 5f, 7f ...

**Open pipe:**
- Fundamental frequency: f = v/2L
- Harmonics: ALL multiples — 2f, 3f, 4f ...

---

## 5.5 Doppler Effect

The **Doppler Effect** is the apparent change in frequency of a wave due to relative motion between source and observer.

- Source moving TOWARD observer → frequency appears HIGHER (sound becomes shriller)
- Source moving AWAY from observer → frequency appears LOWER (sound becomes deeper)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A man standing 340 m from a cliff fires a gun. How long will he wait before hearing an echo? (speed of sound = 340 m/s)

**Solution:**
Total distance = 340 × 2 = 680 m
Time = distance/speed = 680/340 = **2 s**

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** What is the fundamental frequency of a 0.85 m closed pipe? (v = 340 m/s)

**Solution:**
f = v/4L = 340/(4 × 0.85) = 340/3.4 = **100 Hz**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests echo calculations (distance and time), resonance in pipes, and features of sound.

> **Common trap:** For a closed pipe, only ODD harmonics exist. The second harmonic of a closed pipe is at 3 × f₀ (not 2 × f₀ as in an open pipe).

# TOPIC 6: Electric Circuits — Current, Voltage and Resistance
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-003-02

## 6.1 Basic Electrical Quantities

| Quantity | Symbol | Definition | Unit |
|----------|--------|-----------|------|
| Electric charge | Q | Fundamental property of matter | Coulomb (C) |
| Electric current | I | Rate of flow of charge: I = Q/t | Ampere (A) |
| Voltage (EMF/PD) | V | Energy per unit charge | Volt (V) |
| Resistance | R | Opposition to current flow | Ohm (Ω) |
| Power | P | Rate of electrical energy conversion | Watt (W) |

---

## 6.2 Ohm's Law

**Statement:** The current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it, provided temperature and other physical conditions remain constant.

**V = IR**

The gradient of the V-I graph = resistance R.

---

## 6.3 Resistors in Circuits

### Series:
- Same current through each resistor
- Total resistance: **Rₜ = R₁ + R₂ + R₃**
- Voltage divides: V₁:V₂ = R₁:R₂

### Parallel:
- Same voltage across each resistor
- Total resistance: **1/Rₜ = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃**
- Current divides: I₁:I₂ = R₂:R₁ (inverse ratio)

---

## 6.4 Electrical Power and Energy

| Formula | Variables |
|---------|---------|
| P = IV | P = power; I = current; V = voltage |
| P = I²R | When only current and resistance are known |
| P = V²/R | When only voltage and resistance are known |
| E = Pt = IVt | Electrical energy (J or kWh) |

**1 kWh (kilowatt-hour) = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J**

---

## 6.5 Internal Resistance

Every real cell/battery has **internal resistance (r)**. The terminal voltage is less than the EMF:

**EMF (E) = V + Ir = I(R + r)**

Where R = external resistance, r = internal resistance.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Three resistors of 4 Ω, 6 Ω, and 12 Ω are connected in parallel. Calculate the total resistance.

**Solution:**
1/Rₜ = 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 2/12 + 1/12 = 6/12 = 1/2
Rₜ = **2 Ω**

---

**Example 2 (WAEC type):** A 60 W bulb operates at 240 V. Calculate the current drawn and the resistance of the bulb.

**Solution:**
P = IV → I = P/V = 60/240 = **0.25 A**
R = V/I = 240/0.25 = **960 Ω**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests parallel/series calculations, Ohm's Law, and power calculations extensively.

> **JAMB** favours quick calculations — know P = IV, P = I²R, P = V²/R interchangeably.

> **Common trap:** In a parallel circuit, adding more resistors DECREASES total resistance (more paths for current).

> For two resistors in parallel: Rₜ = (R₁ × R₂)/(R₁ + R₂) — the "product over sum" shortcut.

# TOPIC 7: Electromagnetic Induction
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-004-01

## 7.1 Definition

**Electromagnetic induction** is the production of an electromotive force (EMF) in a conductor when it experiences a changing magnetic flux.

Discovered by Michael Faraday (1831).

---

## 7.2 Faraday's Laws

| Law | Statement |
|-----|-----------|
| **First Law** | When the magnetic flux linking a conductor changes, an EMF is induced in the conductor |
| **Second Law** | The magnitude of the induced EMF is proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage |

**Lenz's Law:** The direction of the induced EMF (and thus current) is such that it opposes the change causing it. (This is consistent with conservation of energy.)

---

## 7.3 Types of Devices

### Transformer:
- Transfers electrical energy between circuits via electromagnetic induction
- Consists of primary and secondary coils wound around a soft iron core

**Transformer equation:**
Vₛ/Vₚ = Nₛ/Nₚ = Iₚ/Iₛ

Where V = voltage, N = number of turns, I = current; p = primary, s = secondary.

| Type | Nₛ vs Nₚ | Effect on voltage |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| Step-up transformer | Nₛ > Nₚ | Voltage increases |
| Step-down transformer | Nₛ < Nₚ | Voltage decreases |

**Transformer efficiency = (Vₛ × Iₛ)/(Vₚ × Iₚ) × 100%**
(Ideal transformer: 100% efficient)

### Generator (Dynamo):
- Converts mechanical energy → electrical energy
- Uses electromagnetic induction (rotating coil in magnetic field)
- AC generator: slip rings | DC generator: commutator

### Electric Motor:
- Converts electrical energy → mechanical energy
- Principle: current-carrying conductor in magnetic field experiences force (F = BIL)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A transformer has 100 turns in the primary coil and 500 turns in the secondary coil. If the primary voltage is 240 V, calculate the secondary voltage.

**Solution:**
Vₛ/Vₚ = Nₛ/Nₚ
Vₛ = Vₚ × (Nₛ/Nₚ) = 240 × (500/100) = 240 × 5 = **1200 V**
This is a step-up transformer.

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** The primary coil of a transformer has 400 turns and is connected to a 200 V supply. If the secondary voltage is 50 V, find the number of secondary turns.

**Solution:**
Nₛ/Nₚ = Vₛ/Vₚ → Nₛ = Nₚ × (Vₛ/Vₚ) = 400 × (50/200) = 400 × 0.25 = **100 turns**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests transformer calculations and Lenz's Law application.

> **JAMB** tests the transformer equation and generator/motor distinction.

> **Common trap:** In an ideal transformer, a step-up in voltage means a step-DOWN in current (energy is conserved: power = VI = constant).

# TOPIC 8: Heat and Gas Laws
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-004-02

## 8.1 Thermal Energy and Temperature

**Temperature** measures the degree of hotness or coldness (related to average kinetic energy of particles).
**Heat** is thermal energy in transit (due to temperature difference).

**Scales:**
- Celsius (°C)
- Kelvin (K): K = °C + 273 (absolute scale; 0 K = absolute zero)

---

## 8.2 The Gas Laws

### Boyle's Law:
At constant temperature, the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its volume.
**P₁V₁ = P₂V₂** (T constant)

### Charles' Law:
At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.
**V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂** (P constant)

### Pressure Law (Gay-Lussac's Law):
At constant volume, the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.
**P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂** (V constant)

### Combined/General Gas Law:
**P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂**

### Ideal Gas Law:
**PV = nRT** (where n = moles, R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

---

## 8.3 Heat Capacity and Latent Heat

**Specific heat capacity (c):** Heat needed to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1°C
**Q = mcΔT**

**Specific latent heat (L):** Heat needed for 1 kg to change state WITHOUT temperature change
**Q = mL**

| Type | Change of state | Typical values |
|------|----------------|---------------|
| Latent heat of fusion (Lf) | Solid ↔ Liquid | 334,000 J/kg (ice) |
| Latent heat of vaporisation (Lv) | Liquid ↔ Gas | 2,260,000 J/kg (water) |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A gas has volume 2 L at 300 K and pressure 100 kPa. Find the volume at 600 K and 200 kPa.

**Solution:**
P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂
(100 × 2)/300 = (200 × V₂)/600
200/300 = 200V₂/600
V₂ = (200/300) × (600/200) = (200 × 600)/(300 × 200) = **2 L**

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** How much heat is required to heat 2 kg of water from 20°C to 70°C? (c_water = 4200 J/kg°C)

**Solution:**
Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4200 × (70 − 20) = 2 × 4200 × 50 = **420,000 J = 420 kJ**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests gas law calculations extensively — note that temperature MUST be in Kelvin.

> **Common trap:** Using °C instead of Kelvin in gas law calculations. Always convert: T(K) = T(°C) + 273.

> Latent heat causes a temperature PLATEAU during a change of state — the graph stays flat while heat is still being added.

# TOPIC 9: Radioactivity and Nuclear Physics
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHY-005-01

## 9.1 The Atom and Nucleus

The atom consists of:
- **Nucleus:** Contains protons (+) and neutrons (0) — very small, dense, positive
- **Electrons:** Orbit the nucleus in shells — negatively charged

| Particle | Symbol | Charge | Mass (a.m.u.) |
|----------|--------|--------|--------------|
| Proton | p | +1 | 1 |
| Neutron | n | 0 | 1 |
| Electron | e | −1 | 0 (negligible) |

**Atomic number (Z):** Number of protons
**Mass number (A):** Protons + Neutrons
**Neutron number (N):** A − Z

---

## 9.2 Radioactive Emissions

**Radioactivity** is the spontaneous emission of particles/radiation from unstable nuclei.

| Radiation | Symbol | Composition | Charge | Ionising power | Penetrating power |
|-----------|--------|-------------|--------|----------------|------------------|
| **Alpha (α)** | ⁴₂He | 2 protons + 2 neutrons | +2 | High | Low (stopped by paper) |
| **Beta (β)** | ⁰₋₁e | High-speed electron | −1 | Medium | Medium (stopped by aluminium) |
| **Gamma (γ)** | γ | EM radiation | 0 | Low | High (stopped by thick lead/concrete) |

---

## 9.3 Radioactive Decay Equations

**Alpha decay:** ᴬ_Z X → ᴬ⁻⁴_(Z−2) Y + ⁴_2 He
Example: ²³⁸_92 U → ²³⁴_90 Th + ⁴_2 He

**Beta decay:** ᴬ_Z X → ᴬ_(Z+1) Y + ⁰_−1 e
Example: ¹⁴_6 C → ¹⁴_7 N + ⁰_−1 e

**Conservation laws in decay:**
- Mass number (A) is conserved
- Atomic number (Z) is conserved

---

## 9.4 Half-Life

**Half-life (t½)** is the time taken for half of the radioactive atoms in a sample to decay.

**Remaining mass after n half-lives:** N = N₀ × (½)ⁿ

where N₀ = initial quantity, n = number of half-lives elapsed

---

## 9.5 Nuclear Fission and Fusion

| Type | Definition | Example | Energy |
|------|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Fission** | Heavy nucleus splits into smaller nuclei | ²³⁵U + n → fragments + energy | Large energy released |
| **Fusion** | Light nuclei combine to form heavier nucleus | H + H → He + energy | Even more energy |

**Binding energy:** Energy needed to break the nucleus apart (E = mc², where c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** A radioactive substance has a half-life of 8 days. If the initial mass is 80 g, what mass remains after 32 days?

**Solution:**
Number of half-lives = 32/8 = 4
Remaining mass = 80 × (½)⁴ = 80 × 1/16 = **5 g**

---

**Example 2 (WAEC type):** Complete the nuclear equation: ²²⁶_88 Ra → ²²²_86 Rn + ?

**Solution:**
Mass number: 226 − 222 = 4; Atomic number: 88 − 86 = 2
Product is **⁴_2 He (alpha particle)**

This is alpha decay.

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests half-life calculations, decay equations, and properties of alpha/beta/gamma radiation.

> **JAMB** tests penetrating power, ionising power, and completing decay equations.

> **Common trap:** In alpha decay, atomic number decreases by 2. In beta decay, atomic number INCREASES by 1 (a neutron becomes a proton).

> **Ionising vs Penetrating power are opposite:** Alpha has highest ionising power but lowest penetrating power; gamma has lowest ionising but highest penetrating power.
