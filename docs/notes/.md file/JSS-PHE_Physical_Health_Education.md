## Games and Sports

### Team Games — Rules, Skills and Tactics
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-PHE-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Demonstrate basic skills in at least two team sports
- Apply rules and regulations of team sports correctly
- Demonstrate fair play, teamwork and sportsmanship
- Identify positions and their roles in team sports
- Apply basic offensive and defensive tactics

#### 📖 Notes
- Football (Soccer): field dimensions (100–110 m × 64–75 m), 11 players per side, 90 minutes, offside rule, fouls and free kicks, goal kick, throw-in, corner kick, penalties; basic skills: dribbling, passing, shooting, heading, trapping
- Basketball: court (28 m × 15 m), 5 players per side, 4 quarters (10 min each), scoring (1, 2, 3 points), violations (travelling, double dribble, shot clock), basic skills: dribbling, passing (chest, bounce, overhead), shooting, rebounding
- Handball: court (40 m × 20 m), 7 players per side, 2 halves (30 min), D-zone, throwing and catching; basic skills: overarm throw, catching, dribbling
- Volleyball: 6 players per side, 5 sets (first to 25, 5th to 15), rotations, libero, skills: forearm pass (dig), set, spike, serve, block
- Athletics track and field: sprints (100m, 200m, 400m), relays (4×100m, 4×400m), throws (shot put, discus, javelin), jumps (long jump, high jump, triple jump)
- Teamwork principles: communication, positioning, covering, interchanging
- Fair play: respecting opponents, officials and rules; acknowledging good performance by opponents
- Sportsmanship: winning and losing with grace; no arguing with officials; helping injured opponents

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Practise the three phases of basketball: dribbling relay, passing drills, free throw shooting competition
2. Play mini-football (5-a-side) with emphasis on one rule: the offside rule — observe and discuss incidents
3. Conduct a round-robin volleyball tournament; one team acts as officials, one plays, one observes and records violations

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests: rules of major sports (number of players, duration, scoring, major violations), basic skills identification, and fair play principles
> Know: football = 11 players, 90 min; basketball = 5 players, 40 min; volleyball = 6 players; handball = 7 players
> Trap: 'offside in football' — the player must be in the opposition's half AND nearer to the goal than the second-to-last defender when the ball is played to them

## Health Education

### Personal Hygiene, Nutrition and First Aid
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-PHE-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Explain personal and environmental hygiene practices
- Identify the nutrients in food and their functions
- Design a balanced diet using local Nigerian foods
- Demonstrate basic first aid procedures for common injuries
- Explain the dangers of drug abuse

#### 📖 Notes
- Personal hygiene: bathing regularly, hand washing (before eating, after toilet), dental care (brushing twice daily), clean clothing, cutting fingernails
- Environmental hygiene: disposal of waste (refuse, sewage), drainage, clean water sources, avoiding open defecation
- Water-borne diseases associated with poor hygiene: cholera, typhoid, dysentery, guinea worm
- Nutrients: carbohydrates (energy — rice, bread, yam, corn), proteins (growth and repair — meat, beans, eggs, fish), fats (energy and insulation — palm oil, groundnut), vitamins (regulatory — fruits, vegetables), minerals (structural — milk, liver, beans), water
- Balanced diet: all nutrients in right proportions; using Nigerian foods: plate model — half vegetables, quarter carbohydrates, quarter protein
- Malnutrition: under-nutrition (kwashiorkor — protein deficiency; marasmus — total calorie deficiency) and over-nutrition (obesity)
- First aid: immediate assistance given to an injured person before professional medical help
- First aid for cuts: clean wound, apply pressure, bandage; for burns: cool with running water for 10 minutes (NOT ice, NOT butter)
- First aid for fractures: immobilise (splint), do not move unnecessarily, seek medical help
- Nosebleed: lean FORWARD (not back), pinch soft part of nose for 10 minutes
- Drug abuse: using drugs for non-medical purposes or in wrong doses; examples: cannabis, tramadol (abuse), alcohol excess, tobacco
- Effects of drug abuse: addiction, mental illness, liver disease, HIV risk (sharing needles), broken families

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Conduct a personal hygiene checklist survey for the class; identify most and least practised habits; design an improvement plan
2. Use the Nigerian food composition chart to plan a one-day balanced diet for a 12-year-old using only local foods within ₦500
3. Demonstrate first aid for a burn injury using the RCOS protocol: Remove from danger, Cool (running water), Cover (clean cloth), Seek medical help

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests personal hygiene practices, food nutrient classification, first aid steps for common injuries, and drug abuse consequences
> Know: for burns — COOL with running water for 10 minutes (NOT butter, NOT ice); for nosebleed — lean FORWARD (NOT backward)
> Trap: 'marasmus' is total calorie (energy) deficiency; 'kwashiorkor' is specifically PROTEIN deficiency — different nutritional disorders

## Body and Movement

### The Skeletal and Muscular Systems
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-PHE-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify the major bones of the human skeleton
- Describe the functions of the skeletal system
- Classify joints and describe their movements
- Identify major muscle groups and their functions in movement
- Explain how muscles and bones work together in physical activity

#### 📖 Notes
- Skeletal system: 206 bones in adult; framework for body; protects organs; supports upright posture; enables movement; produces blood cells (red bone marrow); stores calcium
- Axial skeleton: skull, vertebral column (33 vertebrae), ribcage (12 pairs of ribs + sternum)
- Appendicular skeleton: pectoral girdle + arms (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges); pelvic girdle + legs (femur, tibia, fibula, patella, tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges)
- Joint types: fixed/immovable (skull sutures); slightly movable (vertebrae, pubic symphysis); freely movable/synovial (hip, knee, shoulder, elbow, wrist, ankle)
- Synovial joint structures: articular cartilage, synovial fluid, joint capsule, ligaments
- Types of synovial joints: ball-and-socket (hip, shoulder — widest range of motion), hinge (knee, elbow — flexion/extension only), pivot (neck — rotation), saddle (thumb), gliding/plane (carpals)
- Muscular system: over 600 muscles; types: skeletal (voluntary, striped — movement), smooth (involuntary — organs), cardiac (heart)
- Major muscle groups: quadriceps (front of thigh — extension), hamstrings (back of thigh — flexion), gastrocnemius/calf (plantarflexion), deltoid (shoulder — abduction), biceps (elbow flexion), triceps (elbow extension), core muscles (trunk stability)
- Antagonistic muscle pairs: when agonist (prime mover) contracts, antagonist relaxes; biceps/triceps example
- Muscle contraction: concentric (shortening), eccentric (lengthening under load), isometric (no change in length)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Label a diagram of the human skeleton with 20 named bones; group them into axial and appendicular
2. Identify the type of joint at: shoulder, knee, skull, elbow, hip, and vertebral column; justify each classification
3. Perform bicep curl; identify agonist (biceps), antagonist (triceps), synergist (brachialis), and joint (hinge at elbow)

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests major bone names, joint classification, skeletal functions, and muscle groups involved in common movements
> Know the antagonistic pair for major movements: biceps/triceps (elbow), quadriceps/hamstrings (knee)
> Trap: ligaments connect bone to bone; tendons connect muscle to bone — a common confusion that BECE tests
