# TOPIC 1: Exercise Physiology
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHE-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Exercise Physiology

**Exercise physiology** is the study of the body's responses and adaptations to physical exercise, including short-term (acute) responses during exercise and long-term (chronic) adaptations with training.

---

## 1.2 The Cardiovascular System and Exercise

The cardiovascular system (heart + blood vessels) delivers oxygen to working muscles during exercise.

### Acute (Immediate) Cardiovascular Responses to Exercise:

| Response | Change | Why |
|---------|--------|-----|
| Heart rate | Increases | Pumps more blood to muscles |
| Stroke volume | Increases | Heart contracts more forcefully |
| Cardiac output | Increases | Cardiac output = HR × Stroke volume |
| Blood pressure | Increases | Greater force of blood flow |
| Vasodilation | Occurs in active muscles | More blood directed to muscles |
| Vasoconstriction | In inactive organs | Redirects blood to where needed |

**Cardiac output = Heart rate × Stroke volume**

---

## 1.3 The Respiratory System and Exercise

### Acute Respiratory Responses:

| Response | Change |
|---------|--------|
| Breathing rate | Increases |
| Tidal volume (air per breath) | Increases |
| Minute ventilation | Increases greatly |
| Oxygen uptake | Increases |
| Carbon dioxide removal | Increases |

**VO₂ max (Maximal Oxygen Uptake):** The maximum amount of oxygen the body can use during intense exercise. It is the gold standard measure of cardiorespiratory fitness.

---

## 1.4 Energy Systems

The body uses three energy systems to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate — the body's energy currency):

| System | Oxygen required | Duration | Intensity | Example activity |
|--------|---------------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| **ATP-PC (Phosphocreatine)** | No (anaerobic) | 0–10 seconds | Very high | 100m sprint; explosive lift |
| **Glycolytic (Lactic acid)** | No (anaerobic) | 10 seconds–2 minutes | High | 400m run; 200m swim |
| **Oxidative (Aerobic)** | Yes (aerobic) | >2 minutes | Moderate | Marathon, cycling |

**Oxygen debt (EPOC):** After intense exercise, the body continues consuming extra oxygen to restore normal conditions (remove lactic acid, replenish ATP and phosphocreatine, lower body temperature).

---

## 1.5 Long-Term Adaptations to Training

| System | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Cardiovascular | Lower resting heart rate; larger heart; greater stroke volume; increased blood volume |
| Muscular | Hypertrophy (muscle growth); increased mitochondria; improved oxygen use |
| Skeletal | Increased bone density; stronger ligaments and tendons |
| Respiratory | Improved lung efficiency; greater VO₂ max |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Calculate the cardiac output of an athlete whose resting heart rate is 60 beats/min and stroke volume is 80 mL. Compare with exercise values of 180 bpm and 120 mL.

**Solution:**
Resting cardiac output = 60 × 80 = **4,800 mL/min (4.8 L/min)**
Exercise cardiac output = 180 × 120 = **21,600 mL/min (21.6 L/min)**

This shows that the cardiovascular system can increase output dramatically during exercise to meet the demand of working muscles.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests cardiovascular and respiratory responses to exercise, energy systems, and long-term training adaptations.

> **Common trap:** The ATP-PC system is the FASTEST energy source but depletes within 10 seconds. Aerobic metabolism is slower but sustainable for hours.

# TOPIC 2: Health Promotion and Disease Prevention
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHE-001-02

## 2.1 Definition of Health

The **World Health Organization (WHO)** defines health as "a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity."

---

## 2.2 Dimensions of Health

| Dimension | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Physical health** | Optimal functioning of body systems |
| **Mental/emotional health** | Ability to handle stress; emotional stability |
| **Social health** | Ability to interact positively with others |
| **Spiritual health** | Sense of purpose and meaning |
| **Intellectual health** | Ability to learn, think critically, make decisions |

---

## 2.3 Communicable Diseases

**Communicable (infectious) diseases** are caused by pathogens and can be transmitted from person to person.

| Disease | Causative agent | Mode of transmission | Prevention |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Malaria** | Plasmodium (protozoa) | Anopheles mosquito bite | Mosquito nets, insecticides |
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | Mycobacterium tuberculosis (bacteria) | Airborne droplets (coughing/sneezing) | BCG vaccination, good ventilation |
| **HIV/AIDS** | HIV (virus) | Unprotected sex, blood-to-blood contact, mother to child | Abstinence, condoms, ARV drugs |
| **Cholera** | Vibrio cholerae (bacteria) | Contaminated food/water | Clean water, sanitation |
| **Typhoid** | Salmonella typhi (bacteria) | Faecal-oral route; contaminated water | Vaccination, hand washing |
| **COVID-19** | SARS-CoV-2 (virus) | Respiratory droplets, aerosols | Masking, vaccination, ventilation |

---

## 2.4 Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs)

**NCDs** are chronic diseases not caused by pathogens; cannot be transmitted.

| Disease | Key risk factors |
|---------|----------------|
| **Heart disease** | Obesity, inactivity, hypertension, smoking |
| **Diabetes (Type 2)** | Inactivity, obesity, poor diet |
| **Cancer** | Smoking, radiation, certain chemicals, genetics |
| **Hypertension** | Stress, salt, inactivity, genetics |

**Exercise and NCDs:** Regular physical activity reduces the risk of ALL major NCDs.

---

## 2.5 Health Promotion

**Health promotion** is the process of enabling people to increase control over and improve their health (WHO, Ottawa Charter 1986).

**Health education strategies:**
- Campaigns and mass media
- School health programmes
- Community outreach
- Poster and print media
- Social media campaigns

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** List FOUR ways through which malaria can be prevented.

**Solution:**
1. Sleeping under insecticide-treated mosquito nets (ITNs)
2. Indoor residual spraying (IRS) of insecticides to kill mosquitoes
3. Eliminating stagnant water (breeding sites for Anopheles mosquitoes)
4. Use of repellents and protective clothing at dusk/dawn when mosquitoes are most active

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests modes of transmission of diseases, prevention strategies, and health promotion approaches.

> **Common trap:** HIV is transmitted through specific routes (blood, sexual contact, breastfeeding) — NOT through casual contact like handshakes, sharing food, or mosquito bites.

# TOPIC 3: Sports Administration and Management
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHE-002-01

## 3.1 Definition

**Sports administration** is the organised management of sports activities, organisations, and programmes to achieve set goals efficiently and effectively.

---

## 3.2 Levels of Sports Administration in Nigeria

| Level | Organisation | Function |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Federal** | Sports Ministry / National Sports Commission | Policy-making; funding; national teams |
| **State** | State Sports Council | State-level competitions; talent identification |
| **Local** | LGA Sports Committees | Grassroots development |
| **School** | School Sports Committees | Inter-school competitions |

**Key national sports bodies:**
- **Nigeria Football Federation (NFF)** — Governs football
- **Athletics Federation of Nigeria (AFN)** — Governs track and field
- **Nigeria Olympic Committee (NOC)** — Coordinates Nigeria's participation in Olympic Games

---

## 3.3 Functions of Sports Administration

1. **Planning** — Setting goals and strategies for sports development
2. **Organising** — Arranging resources, personnel, and events
3. **Staffing** — Recruiting and managing coaches, officials, administrators
4. **Directing/Leading** — Guiding sports activities and personnel
5. **Controlling** — Monitoring and evaluating performance against goals
6. **Budgeting** — Allocating financial resources

---

## 3.4 Sports Funding in Nigeria

| Source | Examples |
|--------|---------|
| Government grants | Federal and state budgets |
| Sponsorship | Corporate sponsors (telecoms, banks) |
| Gate receipts | Revenue from fans attending events |
| Lottery/pools | Sports lottery funds |
| International bodies | FIFA, IOC grants to NSCs |
| Membership fees | From clubs and athletes |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State FOUR functions of the state sports council in Nigeria.

**Solution:**
1. Organising state-level sports competitions and championships
2. Identifying and nurturing sporting talent at the grassroots level
3. Constructing, maintaining, and managing sports facilities within the state
4. Selecting and training athletes to represent the state in national competitions

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests the structure of sports administration in Nigeria, funding sources, and management functions.

# TOPIC 4: Sports Medicine and Injury Prevention
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHE-002-02

## 4.1 Definition of Sports Medicine

**Sports medicine** is the branch of medicine that deals with physical fitness and the prevention and treatment of injuries related to sports and exercise.

---

## 4.2 Common Sports Injuries

| Injury | Description | Cause |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Sprain** | Ligament stretch/tear | Sudden twist (e.g., ankle roll) |
| **Strain** | Muscle/tendon stretch/tear | Overexertion, poor warm-up |
| **Fracture** | Bone break | Impact, fall |
| **Dislocation** | Bone pushed out of joint | Severe force to joint |
| **Contusion (bruise)** | Tissue damage from impact | Direct blow |
| **Concussion** | Brain injury from head impact | Head collision |
| **Overuse injury** | Gradual damage from repetitive stress | Running, throwing repetitively |

---

## 4.3 RICE Treatment Protocol

For acute soft tissue injuries:

| Letter | Action | Purpose |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **R** — Rest | Stop activity immediately | Prevent further damage |
| **I** — Ice | Apply ice wrapped in cloth | Reduce swelling and pain |
| **C** — Compression | Apply bandage | Limit swelling |
| **E** — Elevation | Raise injured limb above heart level | Reduce blood pooling/swelling |

**PRICE** adds **P (Protection)** — protect the injury from further trauma.

---

## 4.4 Injury Prevention

| Strategy | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Warm-up** | 10–15 minutes of light activity before intense exercise |
| **Cool-down** | 5–10 minutes of gentle activity after exercise; stretching |
| **Proper technique** | Correct form reduces stress on joints |
| **Appropriate equipment** | Proper footwear, protective gear (helmets, pads) |
| **Progressive overload** | Gradually increase training load; avoid sudden jumps |
| **Adequate rest** | Recovery prevents overuse injuries |
| **Hydration** | Prevents muscle cramps and heat illness |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Describe the RICE treatment for a sprained ankle.

**Solution:**
**RICE** is the standard first aid for a sprained ankle:

**R — Rest:** The athlete should immediately stop activity and avoid putting weight on the injured ankle to prevent further tearing of ligaments.

**I — Ice:** Apply an ice pack wrapped in a cloth or towel to the injured ankle for 15–20 minutes every 2–3 hours during the first 48 hours. Do NOT apply ice directly to skin (ice burn risk). The cold reduces swelling and numbs pain.

**C — Compression:** Wrap the ankle firmly (not tightly) with an elastic bandage to limit swelling and provide gentle support to the joint.

**E — Elevation:** Raise the ankle above the level of the heart (e.g., prop it on pillows while lying down) to use gravity to drain fluid away from the injury, reducing swelling.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests RICE treatment, types of sports injuries, and injury prevention strategies.

> **Common trap:** Ice should NEVER be applied directly to skin — always wrap it in a cloth to prevent frostbite/ice burn.

# TOPIC 5: Physical Fitness, Training Principles and Performance
**Topic ID:** SSS-PHE-003-01

## 5.1 Definition of Physical Fitness

**Physical fitness** is the ability to carry out daily activities with vigour, without undue fatigue, and with enough energy remaining to enjoy leisure activities and respond to emergencies.

---

## 5.2 Components of Physical Fitness

### Health-Related Components:

| Component | Definition | Test example |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Cardiovascular endurance** | Ability to sustain prolonged aerobic activity | Cooper 12-minute run |
| **Muscular strength** | Maximum force a muscle can exert in one effort | 1-rep maximum (1RM) |
| **Muscular endurance** | Ability to repeat muscular contractions over time | Press-ups; sit-ups test |
| **Flexibility** | Range of motion at a joint | Sit-and-reach test |
| **Body composition** | Ratio of fat to lean body mass | BMI; skin-fold measurements |

### Skill-Related Components:

| Component | Definition | Important for |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Speed** | Rate of movement | Sprinting; ball sports |
| **Agility** | Change direction quickly and accurately | Football, basketball |
| **Balance** | Maintain equilibrium | Gymnastics, surfing |
| **Coordination** | Use senses and body parts together smoothly | Throwing, catching |
| **Power** | Strength × Speed (explosive force) | Jumping, throwing |
| **Reaction time** | Speed of response to a stimulus | Sprint start; cricket batting |

---

## 5.3 Principles of Training

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Specificity (SAID)** | Training must be specific to the activity/muscles being developed |
| **Progressive overload** | Gradually increase intensity, duration, or frequency over time |
| **Reversibility** | Fitness gains are lost when training stops ("use it or lose it") |
| **Variance/Variety** | Change training to avoid boredom and overuse injuries |
| **Individuality** | Training must be tailored to the individual's needs and abilities |
| **Rest and recovery** | The body adapts during rest, not just during training |

---

## 5.4 FITT Principle

Used to design training programmes:

| Letter | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **F** — Frequency | How often | 3 times per week |
| **I** — Intensity | How hard | 70% of maximum heart rate |
| **T** — Time | How long | 30–45 minutes |
| **T** — Type | What kind of activity | Running, swimming, weights |

---

## 5.5 Methods of Training

| Method | Description | Best for |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Continuous training** | Sustained moderate-intensity exercise (no rest) | Cardiovascular endurance |
| **Interval training** | Alternating high-intensity bursts with rest | Speed, anaerobic power |
| **Fartlek training** | Varying speed and intensity over natural terrain | Mixed fitness; fun |
| **Circuit training** | Series of stations; different exercises in sequence | General fitness; muscular endurance |
| **Weight training** | Resistance exercises with weights | Muscular strength and power |
| **Flexibility training (stretching)** | Static and dynamic stretches | Flexibility, injury prevention |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** State and explain FOUR principles of training that a coach should follow when designing a fitness programme.

**Solution:**
1. **Specificity:** The training programme must be specific to the demands of the sport and the athlete's role. A sprinter should train differently from a marathon runner — sprint training develops fast-twitch fibres and explosive power, while marathon training develops aerobic endurance.

2. **Progressive overload:** The training load (intensity, duration, or frequency) must be gradually increased over time for continued improvement. If a runner always trains at the same pace and distance, the body adapts and makes no further gains.

3. **Reversibility:** When training stops or reduces significantly, the fitness benefits gained begin to reverse. This is why athletes must maintain their training throughout the season and off-season, though intensity may vary.

4. **Individuality:** Every athlete has different physiological characteristics, fitness levels, and responses to training. A programme designed for one athlete may be too intense or too easy for another — personalisation is key to effective training.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests the FITT principle, training methods, components of fitness (health-related and skill-related), and training principles.

> **Common trap:** Strength is the maximum FORCE in ONE effort. Muscular endurance is sustaining REPEATED effort over time. They are different components requiring different training methods.

> Know which fitness test measures which component: Cooper test → cardiovascular endurance; sit-and-reach → flexibility; 1RM → strength; sit-ups → muscular endurance.
