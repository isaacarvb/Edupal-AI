## Number and Numeration

### Indices and Logarithms
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Apply all laws of indices to simplify algebraic and numerical expressions
- Define logarithm as the inverse of indices and use four-figure log tables
- Use laws of logarithms to evaluate complex expressions
- Solve exponential equations using logarithms
- Apply the change of base formula in problem solving
- Solve real-life problems involving indices and logarithms

#### 📖 Notes
- Laws of indices: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ, aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ, (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
- Zero index: a⁰ = 1 for any non-zero a
- Negative index: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
- Fractional index: aᵐ/ⁿ = ⁿ√(aᵐ)
- Definition of logarithm: logₐN = x means aˣ = N
- Common logarithm (base 10) and natural logarithm (base e ≈ 2.718)
- Laws of logarithms: log(AB) = logA + logB, log(A/B) = logA − logB, log(Aⁿ) = n logA
- Reading and using four-figure logarithm tables: characteristic and mantissa
- Antilogarithm tables and their use in calculations
- Change of base formula: logₐb = log b / log a
- Solving exponential equations: e.g. 3^(2x+1) = 81
- Real-life applications: compound interest A = P(1 + r)ⁿ, population growth, pH scale

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Use four-figure log tables to evaluate (23.5 × 0.042)^(1/3) step by step, showing characteristic and mantissa
2. Solve the equation 2^(x+1) = 5^(x-2) using logarithms and verify the solution
3. Research and present one real-life application of logarithms (e.g. Richter scale, decibels, compound interest)

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC frequently tests simplification of index expressions, evaluation using log tables, and finding antilog — always show table reading steps
> JAMB tests laws of indices (fractional, negative), change of base formula, and solving exponential equations — multiple choice means one wrong step loses the mark
> Common trap: log(A + B) ≠ logA + logB — students must not split sums inside logs

### Number Bases
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Convert numbers from one base to another (binary, octal, denary, hexadecimal)
- Perform addition, subtraction, multiplication and division in different bases
- Solve problems involving number bases in real contexts
- Identify place values in any given base
- Express fractions in different bases

#### 📖 Notes
- Place value in any base: e.g. in base 5, 234₅ = 2×5² + 3×5¹ + 4×5⁰
- Conversion to base 10 (denary): repeated expansion
- Conversion from base 10 to other bases: repeated division method
- Conversion between non-denary bases via base 10
- Binary (base 2): digits 0 and 1; used in computers
- Octal (base 8): digits 0–7
- Hexadecimal (base 16): digits 0–9 and A–F
- Addition in other bases: carry rule (carry when reaching the base value)
- Subtraction in other bases: borrow rule
- Multiplication and division in other bases
- Fractions in other bases: e.g. 0.1 in base 2 = ½ in base 10
- Application of binary numbers in digital systems and computing

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Convert the number 347 (base 10) to base 2, base 5, and base 8, showing all division steps
2. Perform (1101₂ + 1011₂) and (43₅ × 12₅) with full working, checking by converting to base 10
3. Design a table comparing the same number in base 2, 5, 8 and 16 for numbers 1–20

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests conversion in all directions and arithmetic in bases 2, 5, 8 — always show the repeated division or expansion working
> JAMB favours quick conversion and addition/subtraction in base 2 — practise binary arithmetic to speed
> Common error: forgetting carry-over in addition (e.g. 1+1 in base 2 = 10₂, not 2)

### Surds and Rationalization
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-001-03`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define and identify surds, rational and irrational numbers
- Simplify surds by reducing the radicand
- Perform the four operations on surds
- Rationalize the denominator of surds
- Solve equations involving surds

#### 📖 Notes
- Definition: a surd is an irrational root that cannot be simplified to a rational number; e.g. √2, √3, ³√5
- Rational vs. irrational numbers: terminating/recurring decimals vs. non-terminating non-recurring
- Simplifying surds: √12 = √(4×3) = 2√3
- Addition and subtraction of like surds: 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2
- Multiplication of surds: √a × √b = √(ab); (√a)² = a
- Division of surds: √a / √b = √(a/b)
- Rationalizing monomial denominators: multiply numerator and denominator by the surd
- Rationalizing binomial denominators: multiply by conjugate (a + √b) → conjugate is (a − √b)
- Conjugate pairs: (√a + √b)(√a − √b) = a − b
- Square roots of surds: finding √(a + b√c) in the form p + q√c
- Equations involving surds: isolate surd, then square both sides; check for extraneous solutions
- Relationships between surds and indices: √a = a^(1/2)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Simplify (3√5 − 2√3)(3√5 + 2√3) and (1 + √2)³ without a calculator
2. Rationalize 7/(2 + √3) and express in the form a + b√3
3. Investigate: which of √2, √3, √4, √5, √6, √7, √8, √9 are surds? Justify your answer

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC regularly asks for rationalization of denominators with binomial surds — practise conjugate multiplication
> JAMB tests simplification and multiplication of surds quickly; know √2 ≈ 1.414, √3 ≈ 1.732, √5 ≈ 2.236
> Trap: students square before isolating the surd, introducing errors — always isolate first then square both sides

## Algebraic Processes

### Polynomials and the Remainder/Factor Theorem
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define a polynomial and identify its degree and coefficients
- Add, subtract, multiply and divide polynomials
- State and apply the Remainder Theorem to find remainders
- State and apply the Factor Theorem to factorise polynomials
- Solve cubic equations using the Factor Theorem
- Apply polynomial operations to real-world problems

#### 📖 Notes
- Definition of polynomial: P(x) = aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + … + a₁x + a₀; degree, coefficients, constant term
- Operations on polynomials: addition, subtraction, multiplication
- Long division of polynomials: dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder
- Remainder Theorem: when P(x) is divided by (x − a), the remainder = P(a)
- Factor Theorem: (x − a) is a factor of P(x) if and only if P(a) = 0
- Rational Root Theorem: possible rational roots are ±(factors of constant) / (factors of leading coefficient)
- Factorising cubic polynomials: find one root by inspection/trial, then divide, then factorise quadratic
- Solving polynomial equations: equate to zero, factorise, apply zero product property
- Sum and product of roots: for ax² + bx + c = 0, α+β = −b/a, αβ = c/a
- Expanding products of three linear factors
- Partial fractions: decomposing rational expressions into simpler fractions
- Applications: projectile paths, volume and area problems modelled by polynomials

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Find the value of k if (x − 2) is a factor of kx³ − 3x² + 2kx − 4, then fully factorise the polynomial
2. Divide P(x) = 2x³ + 3x² − 8x + 3 by (x + 3) using long division and verify using the Remainder Theorem
3. Solve 2x³ − 3x² − 11x + 6 = 0 completely using the Factor Theorem

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests all four operations, the Remainder Theorem, and full factorisation — show synthetic or long division working
> JAMB tests quick evaluation using Remainder Theorem and identifying factors — P(a) = 0 test is fastest
> Key: recognise that if P(1), P(−1), P(2), P(−2) etc. gives 0 you have found a factor — practise these quick checks

### Quadratic Equations and Graphs
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-002-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Solve quadratic equations by factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula
- Determine the nature of roots using the discriminant
- Sketch the graph of a quadratic function and identify key features
- Form quadratic equations given their roots
- Solve problems involving maximum and minimum values of quadratic functions

#### 📖 Notes
- Standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0)
- Factorisation method: find two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b
- Completing the square: ax² + bx = a(x + b/2a)² − b²/4a
- Quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / 2a
- Discriminant: Δ = b²−4ac; Δ>0 two real distinct roots; Δ=0 equal roots; Δ<0 no real roots
- Sum of roots α+β = −b/a; product of roots αβ = c/a
- Forming quadratic from roots: x² − (α+β)x + αβ = 0
- Vertex of parabola: (−b/2a, f(−b/2a)); axis of symmetry x = −b/2a
- Direction of parabola: opens up if a>0, down if a<0
- Sketching: find x-intercepts, y-intercept, vertex; plot and connect
- Maximum/minimum value: vertex gives max (a<0) or min (a>0) of f(x)
- Application: projectile motion, area problems, profit/revenue maximisation

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0 by all three methods (factorisation, completing the square, quadratic formula) and compare working
2. Sketch the graph of y = −x² + 4x + 5, clearly labelling roots, vertex, axis of symmetry and y-intercept
3. A farmer encloses a rectangular field with 100 m of fencing. Find the maximum area using quadratic methods

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC requires all three solution methods — factorisation scores highest marks and shows understanding; completing the square is often specifically requested
> JAMB tests discriminant conditions, forming equations from roots, and vertex/maximum questions
> Avoid: dividing through by x (loses a root x = 0) and sign errors when applying the quadratic formula

### Simultaneous Equations (Linear and Quadratic)
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-002-03`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Solve two simultaneous linear equations by substitution and elimination
- Solve a linear-quadratic pair of simultaneous equations
- Interpret simultaneous equations graphically as intersection points
- Solve word problems leading to simultaneous equations

#### 📖 Notes
- Simultaneous linear equations: two unknowns, two equations
- Elimination method: multiply equations to make one coefficient equal, then subtract or add
- Substitution method: express one variable in terms of the other
- Graphical method: plot both lines; intersection is the solution
- Three equations, three unknowns: extension using matrices or Gaussian elimination (JAMB)
- Linear-quadratic systems: substitute linear into quadratic to get a quadratic equation
- Geometric interpretation: a line and a parabola can intersect in 0, 1 or 2 points
- Discriminant tells the number of intersections: Δ>0 (2 pts), Δ=0 (tangent), Δ<0 (no intersection)
- Word problems: formulate equations, solve, interpret solution in context
- Age problems, mixture problems, distance-time problems modelled as simultaneous equations
- Currency and trade problems common in WAEC contexts
- Checking solutions: substitute back into BOTH original equations

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Solve the system: y = x² − 3x + 2 and y = 2x − 4, then sketch both curves and mark the intersection(s)
2. A market woman sells rice and beans. Rice costs ₦500/kg, beans ₦300/kg. She sells 20 kg total for ₦8,000. Find the kg of each
3. Two cars leave the same town in opposite directions. One travels 20 km/h faster. After 3 hours they are 300 km apart. Find both speeds

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests linear-linear and linear-quadratic pairs; show substitution steps fully for method marks
> JAMB often disguises simultaneous equations in word problems — identify the two variables and form equations first
> Check: always substitute both values back into original equations to verify — one mark may be awarded just for checking

## Mensuration, Geometry and Trigonometry

### Plane Geometry and Circle Theorems
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- State and apply all standard circle theorems
- Prove selected circle theorems
- Apply circle theorems to solve problems involving angles in circles
- Identify and use properties of tangents to circles
- Solve problems involving two or more circle theorems

#### 📖 Notes
- Circle vocabulary: centre, radius, diameter, chord, arc (major/minor), sector, segment, tangent, secant
- Theorem 1: Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference (same arc)
- Theorem 2: Angles in the same segment are equal
- Theorem 3: Angle in a semicircle = 90°
- Theorem 4: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary (add to 180°)
- Theorem 5: Exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral = interior opposite angle
- Theorem 6: Tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact
- Theorem 7: Two tangents from an external point are equal in length
- Theorem 8: Angle in alternate segment (tangent-chord angle = angle in alternate segment)
- Intersecting chords: PA × PB = PC × PD (chords intersecting inside)
- Intersecting secants: PA × PB = PC × PD (secants from external point)
- Constructing circle theorems problems: identifying the arc being used is key

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Draw a circle, mark a chord, and measure the angle subtended at the centre and at the circumference — verify the 2:1 ratio
2. Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° using the inscribed angle theorem
3. Solve a multi-step problem: given a tangent from external point P touching circle at T, with a chord through the circle, find multiple unknown angles

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC almost always has 2–3 circle theorem questions; identify which theorem applies before calculating — name the theorem in working
> JAMB tests angle calculations from diagrams; the 'angle at centre is twice angle at circumference' theorem is tested most frequently
> Trap: students confuse 'angle in same segment' with 'angle at centre' — always check whether the point is ON the circle or AT the centre

### Trigonometry — Ratios, Graphs and Equations
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-003-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define and use the six trigonometric ratios for acute and obtuse angles
- Apply the SOHCAHTOA rule to right-angled triangles
- Apply the sine rule and cosine rule to non-right triangles
- Calculate areas of triangles using trigonometry
- Sketch and interpret graphs of sin, cos and tan
- Solve simple trigonometric equations in given ranges

#### 📖 Notes
- Trigonometric ratios for right-angled triangles: sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj
- Reciprocal ratios: cosec θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ, cot θ = 1/tan θ
- CAST diagram: quadrant signs — All positive (1st), Sin positive (2nd), Tan positive (3rd), Cos positive (4th)
- Special angles: sin 30°=½, cos 60°=½, tan 45°=1, sin 60°=√3/2, sin 90°=1
- Sine Rule: a/sinA = b/sinB = c/sinC — use when given two angles or angle-side-side
- Cosine Rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cosA — use when given three sides or two sides and included angle
- Area of triangle: Area = ½ab sinC
- Graphs of y = sin x and y = cos x: amplitude 1, period 360°; key points at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, 360°
- Graph of y = tan x: period 180°; asymptotes at 90° and 270°
- Transformations: y = a sin(bx + c) + d — amplitude a, period 360°/b, phase shift, vertical shift
- Solving trigonometric equations: find principal value, use CAST for all solutions in range
- Angles of elevation and depression: bearing and surveying problems

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Measure the height of a building/tree on the school compound using a clinometer and the tangent ratio; compare with actual height
2. Sketch y = 2sin(3x) for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°, identifying amplitude, period, maximum and minimum
3. Solve the triangle ABC where a = 7 cm, b = 10 cm, C = 50°, using both the cosine rule and area formula

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests sine/cosine rule, bearings, angles of elevation/depression, and graphs — show which rule you are using and why
> JAMB tests CAST diagram for signs, solving equations in given ranges (e.g. 0° ≤ θ ≤ 360°), and special angle values
> Trap: using sine rule when cosine rule is needed (ambiguous case) — if given three sides, cosine rule; two sides + included angle, cosine rule

## Statistics and Probability

### Statistics — Data Presentation and Measures
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-004-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Collect, classify and tabulate data in frequency distribution tables
- Draw and interpret bar charts, histograms, frequency polygons and cumulative frequency curves
- Calculate mean, median, mode and range for grouped and ungrouped data
- Calculate variance and standard deviation
- Interpret statistical graphs to draw conclusions

#### 📖 Notes
- Types of data: primary vs. secondary; discrete vs. continuous; quantitative vs. qualitative
- Frequency distribution table: class interval, tally, frequency, cumulative frequency
- Class boundaries, class width, class midpoint
- Bar chart: for discrete data; gaps between bars
- Histogram: for continuous grouped data; no gaps; area represents frequency
- Frequency polygon: join midpoints of tops of histogram bars
- Cumulative frequency curve (ogive): plot cumulative frequencies against upper class boundaries
- Mean for ungrouped data: x̄ = Σx/n; for grouped: x̄ = Σfx/Σf
- Median: middle value (ungrouped); from ogive or L + ((n/2 − F)/f) × h (grouped)
- Mode: most frequent value; modal class for grouped data
- Variance: σ² = Σf(x − x̄)² / Σf; standard deviation σ = √variance
- Quartiles, percentiles and inter-quartile range from ogive

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Collect ages or heights of 30 classmates, group into class intervals, and draw a histogram and cumulative frequency curve; estimate median and IQR
2. Calculate the mean, median, mode and standard deviation for: 12, 15, 18, 22, 14, 18, 20, 12, 25, 18
3. Read a cumulative frequency graph and estimate the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC requires calculation of all four central tendency measures from grouped data — know the median formula for grouped data L + ((n/2−F)/f) × h
> JAMB tests standard deviation, interpretation of histograms, and reading ogives
> Trap: students use midpoints as raw values in ungrouped mean — ensure you use midpoint only for grouped calculations

### Probability
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-004-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define probability and express it as a fraction, decimal or percentage
- Identify sample spaces, events and outcomes
- Apply the addition rule for mutually exclusive events
- Apply the multiplication rule for independent events
- Calculate conditional probability
- Use tree diagrams and Venn diagrams to solve probability problems

#### 📖 Notes
- Definition: probability P(E) = number of favourable outcomes / total number of outcomes, 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
- Complementary events: P(E') = 1 − P(E)
- Sample space S: set of all possible outcomes
- Event: any subset of the sample space
- Mutually exclusive events: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) — cannot occur simultaneously
- Non-mutually exclusive events: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
- Independent events: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) — one does not affect the other
- Dependent events and conditional probability: P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)
- Tree diagrams: map all outcomes and multiply along branches
- Venn diagrams for probability: regions represent sets; intersections represent common outcomes
- Equally likely outcomes: fair coins, fair dice, well-shuffled cards
- Permutations and combinations: nPr = n!/(n−r)!, nCr = n!/(r!(n−r)!)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Draw a sample space for throwing two dice; find P(sum = 7), P(sum > 9) and P(doubles)
2. Use a tree diagram for drawing two balls (without replacement) from a bag of 3 red and 4 blue balls; find P(both same colour)
3. Use a Venn diagram to show that 60 students take Maths, 45 take English, 25 take both; find probability of taking at least one subject

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests all rules — clearly state which rule you are applying; tree diagrams earn structured marks
> JAMB tests combinations, permutations, and the addition/multiplication rules — know nCr formula
> Common error: not distinguishing between 'with replacement' and 'without replacement' in dependent events

## Calculus (Introduction)

### Differentiation
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-005-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Differentiate polynomial, trigonometric and exponential functions from first principles
- Apply the product rule, quotient rule and chain rule
- Find equations of tangents and normals to curves
- Solve maximum and minimum problems using differentiation
- Apply differentiation to kinematics (velocity and acceleration)

#### 📖 Notes
- Concept of a limit: lim(h→0) [f(x+h) − f(x)] / h = f'(x)
- Differentiation from first principles for f(x) = xⁿ, yielding f'(x) = nxⁿ⁻¹
- Standard results: d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹; d/dx(eˣ) = eˣ; d/dx(sin x) = cos x; d/dx(cos x) = −sin x
- Sum/difference rule: d/dx[f(x) ± g(x)] = f'(x) ± g'(x)
- Product rule: d/dx[uv] = u dv/dx + v du/dx
- Quotient rule: d/dx[u/v] = (v du/dx − u dv/dx) / v²
- Chain rule: d/dx[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) × g'(x)
- Second derivative f''(x): rate of change of f'(x); used to classify stationary points
- Stationary points: set f'(x) = 0; maximum if f''(x) < 0, minimum if f''(x) > 0
- Equation of tangent at (x₀, y₀): y − y₀ = f'(x₀)(x − x₀)
- Equation of normal: slope is −1/f'(x₀)
- Kinematics: v = ds/dt, a = dv/dt = d²s/dt²

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Differentiate f(x) = 3x⁴ − 2x³ + 5x − 7 from first principles for the x⁴ term, then use the rule for the rest
2. Find the coordinates of stationary points of y = x³ − 6x² + 9x + 2 and classify them; sketch the curve
3. A particle moves so that s = 2t³ − 9t² + 12t. Find when the particle is momentarily at rest and its acceleration at those times

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests product/quotient/chain rules, stationary point classification, tangent/normal equations — always classify max/min using f''(x)
> JAMB tests speed/velocity/acceleration problems and polynomial differentiation — know that velocity = ds/dt
> Trap: students find stationary points but forget to classify them as max or min — WAEC often deducts marks for incomplete classification

### Integration
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-MTH-005-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Integrate polynomial functions using the reverse power rule
- Apply integration to find areas under curves
- Evaluate definite integrals
- Apply integration to displacement, velocity and acceleration problems
- Use integration to compute volumes of revolution (extension)

#### 📖 Notes
- Integration as the reverse of differentiation: antiderivative
- Indefinite integral: ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C (n ≠ −1)
- Constant of integration C: determined from initial/boundary conditions
- Standard integrals: ∫eˣ dx = eˣ + C; ∫sin x dx = −cos x + C; ∫cos x dx = sin x + C
- Integration of polynomials: integrate term by term
- Definite integral: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = [F(x)]ₐᵇ = F(b) − F(a); no constant of integration
- Area under a curve: A = ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx (when curve is above x-axis)
- Area between two curves: A = ∫ₐᵇ [f(x) − g(x)] dx
- Negative area: when curve dips below x-axis, use |∫| or split the integral
- Kinematics integration: s = ∫v dt, v = ∫a dt
- Substitution method: let u = g(x), convert integral in terms of u
- Volumes of revolution: V = π∫ₐᵇ y² dx (rotation about x-axis)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Find ∫(3x² − 4x + 5) dx and use the result to compute the definite integral from x = 1 to x = 3
2. Find the area enclosed between y = x² and y = 4 − x², sketching the region first
3. A particle starts from rest with acceleration a = 6t − 4. Find the velocity and displacement after 3 seconds

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests indefinite integrals with constants, definite integrals, and area under curves — always include +C for indefinite integrals
> JAMB tests kinematics integration and polynomial integration — practise integrating velocity to get displacement
> Trap: forgetting to add +C in indefinite integrals loses marks; confusing order of limits in definite integrals gives wrong sign
