## Number and Numeration

### Whole Numbers, Fractions and Decimals
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-MTH-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Read, write and compare whole numbers up to one billion
- Perform the four operations on whole numbers, fractions and decimals
- Convert between fractions, decimals and percentages
- Apply BODMAS/PEMDAS rule to simplify expressions
- Solve word problems involving numbers in everyday contexts

#### 📖 Notes
- Place value: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, millions, billions
- Roman numerals: I=1, V=5, X=10, L=50, C=100, D=500, M=1000
- Types of fractions: proper (n<d), improper (n≥d), mixed numbers
- Operations on fractions: addition/subtraction (common denominator), multiplication (straight across), division (multiply by reciprocal)
- Decimals: place value system extended; adding zeros for alignment
- Converting fractions to decimals: divide numerator by denominator
- Converting decimals to fractions: place value denominator, simplify
- Percentages: means 'out of 100'; x% = x/100
- Percentage of a quantity: % × amount; percentage increase/decrease
- BODMAS: Brackets, Order (powers), Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction
- LCM (Lowest Common Multiple) and HCF (Highest Common Factor): prime factorisation method
- Word problems: key words (total, difference, product, quotient, per, of)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Use fraction strips to visually add 1/3 + 1/4; verify algebraically
2. Convert the class's test scores to percentages, fractions and decimals; rank in order
3. Apply BODMAS to 5 multi-operation expressions and compare answers with classmates

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests all four operations with fractions and decimals, percentage calculations, and BODMAS — show all working
> Know the key percentage relationships: 25% = 1/4, 50% = 1/2, 75% = 3/4, 10% = 1/10, 20% = 1/5
> Trap: in BODMAS, Division and Multiplication have equal priority (left to right); same for Addition and Subtraction — do NOT always divide before multiplying

### Integers, Directed Numbers and Number Lines
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-MTH-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define integers and distinguish positive, negative and zero
- Perform operations on directed (positive and negative) numbers
- Use a number line to represent and compare integers
- Apply directed numbers to real-life situations (temperature, profit/loss, sea level)

#### 📖 Notes
- Integers: whole numbers; positive (1, 2, 3...), zero (0), negative (...−3, −2, −1)
- Number line: negative numbers to the left of zero; positive to the right
- Comparing integers: further left = smaller; −7 < −2
- Adding integers: same sign → add, keep sign; different signs → subtract smaller from larger, keep sign of larger
- Subtracting integers: change sign of subtracted number and add; e.g. 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8
- Multiplying/dividing integers: same signs → positive product; different signs → negative product
- Real-life applications: temperature (above/below zero °C), altitude (above/below sea level), profit/loss, deposits/withdrawals
- Absolute value: |n| = distance from zero; always non-negative; |−5| = 5
- Order of operations still applies with negative numbers

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Use a vertical number line to model changes in temperature: starts at 5°C, drops 8°C overnight, rises 3°C by noon — what is the final temperature?
2. Simulate a bank account: ₦500 balance, deposit ₦300, withdraw ₦700, deposit ₦200 — track the balance using directed numbers
3. Play directed number bingo: teacher calls operations, students compute and mark their boards

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests operations with negative numbers and real-life applications — draw a number line if it helps
> Remember: negative × negative = positive; negative × positive = negative
> Trap: −2² = −4 (only the 2 is squared, not the negative sign), but (−2)² = +4 (the negative IS squared)

## Algebra

### Algebraic Expressions and Simple Equations
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-MTH-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Write and simplify algebraic expressions
- Expand and factorise simple algebraic expressions
- Solve simple linear equations in one variable
- Set up and solve equations from word problems
- Evaluate algebraic expressions by substitution

#### 📖 Notes
- Algebraic expression: combination of variables, coefficients and constants; e.g. 3x + 2y − 5
- Term, coefficient, variable, constant: in 5x², '5' is coefficient, 'x' is variable, '²' is power
- Like terms: same variable(s) with same power; can be combined; 3x + 5x = 8x
- Expanding brackets: multiply each term inside by the factor outside; 3(2x + 4) = 6x + 12
- Factorisation: reverse of expansion; find common factor; 6x + 12 = 6(x + 2)
- Substitution: replace variable with given number; if a = 3, b = 2: find 2a + b = 2(3) + 2 = 8
- Linear equation: highest power of variable is 1; one solution
- Solving linear equations: balance principle — whatever you do to one side, do to the other
- Steps: expand brackets → collect like terms → isolate variable → divide by coefficient
- Word problems: 'a number added to 5 equals 13' → n + 5 = 13; solve for n
- Inequalities: < less than, > greater than, ≤ less than or equal, ≥ greater than or equal
- Representing inequalities on a number line

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Simplify: 3x + 2y − x + 4y − 2; then substitute x = 2, y = 1 to verify
2. Solve: 2(x + 3) = 14 showing each step; check by substituting back
3. Write and solve: 'Amaka is 3 years older than Chidi. The sum of their ages is 29. Find each person's age'

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests simplification, expansion, factorisation and solving equations from word problems
> Always CHECK your answer by substituting back into the original equation
> Trap: when solving 3x = 12, divide BOTH sides by 3 (not subtract) — inverse operation of multiplication is division

## Geometry

### Shapes, Angles and Properties of 2D Figures
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-MTH-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify and classify angles (acute, right, obtuse, reflex, straight)
- Apply angle properties (angles on a line, at a point, in a triangle, in a polygon)
- Identify and state properties of triangles and quadrilaterals
- Calculate the interior and exterior angle sum of any polygon
- Solve geometrical problems involving parallel lines and transversals

#### 📖 Notes
- Types of angles: acute (0°–90°), right (90°), obtuse (90°–180°), straight (180°), reflex (180°–360°), revolution (360°)
- Angle relationships: angles on a straight line sum to 180°; angles at a point sum to 360°; vertically opposite angles are equal
- Types of triangles: by sides — equilateral (3 equal), isosceles (2 equal), scalene (none equal); by angles — acute, right, obtuse
- Triangle angle sum: always 180°
- Properties of quadrilaterals: square (4 equal sides, 4 right angles), rectangle (opposite sides equal, 4 right angles), parallelogram (opposite sides parallel and equal), rhombus (4 equal sides), trapezium (1 pair parallel sides), kite (2 pairs adjacent equal sides)
- Polygon angle sums: interior angle sum = (n−2) × 180°; exterior angle sum always = 360°
- Regular polygon: each interior angle = (n−2) × 180° / n
- Parallel lines and transversals: corresponding angles equal (F-shape), alternate angles equal (Z-shape), co-interior (same-side interior) angles supplementary (C-shape)
- Construction tools: ruler, compass, protractor; constructing 60°, 90°, bisecting angles and lines

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Measure all interior angles of 5 different triangles cut from paper; verify they each sum to 180°
2. Construct an equilateral triangle using ruler and compass (60° method); then bisect one angle to create 30°
3. Find all unknown angles in a diagram with two parallel lines cut by a transversal, justifying each step with the correct property name

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests angle calculations, quadrilateral properties, parallel line angle relationships, and polygon interior angle sums
> Always state the reason for each angle found: 'alternate angles are equal' not just writing the number
> Trap: co-interior angles are SUPPLEMENTARY (sum to 180°), not equal — unlike alternate and corresponding angles which ARE equal

## Mensuration

### Perimeter, Area and Volume
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-MTH-004-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Calculate perimeter and area of rectangles, triangles, circles and compound shapes
- Calculate surface area and volume of cuboids, cylinders and triangular prisms
- Solve problems involving area and volume in everyday contexts
- Convert between units of length, area and volume

#### 📖 Notes
- Perimeter: total distance around a shape; add all sides
- Area of rectangle: l × b; square: l²
- Area of triangle: ½ × base × height
- Area of parallelogram: base × perpendicular height
- Area of trapezium: ½(a + b) × h
- Circumference of circle: 2πr or πd; π ≈ 22/7 or 3.142
- Area of circle: πr²
- Area of sector: (θ/360) × πr²
- Arc length: (θ/360) × 2πr
- Volume of cuboid: l × b × h
- Volume of cylinder: πr²h
- Volume of triangular prism: ½ × base × height × length
- Surface area: total area of all faces; for cuboid = 2(lb + lh + bh)
- Unit conversion: 1 m = 100 cm; 1 km = 1000 m; 1 m² = 10,000 cm²; 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Measure the classroom floor; calculate its area and perimeter; estimate how many tiles (30cm × 30cm) are needed
2. Find the volume of a cylindrical well of radius 0.5 m and depth 10 m; calculate how many litres of water it holds (1 m³ = 1,000 L)
3. Calculate the area of a compound shape: a rectangle with a semicircle on one end

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests area, perimeter, and volume using standard formulas — always write down the formula before substituting
> Use π = 22/7 when radius is a multiple of 7; use π = 3.14 or 3.142 otherwise — check what the question specifies
> Trap: area is in square units (cm², m²) and volume in cubic units (cm³, m³) — losing units loses marks
