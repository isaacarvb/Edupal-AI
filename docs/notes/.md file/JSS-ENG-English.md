# TOPIC 1: Speech Sounds — Vowels, Consonants and Phonics
**Topic ID:** JSS-ENG-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Speech Sounds (Phonology)

**Phonology** is the study of the sound system of a language. In English, sounds are divided into **vowels** and **consonants**. A **phoneme** is the smallest unit of sound that distinguishes one word from another (e.g., the difference between "cat" and "bat" is the phoneme /k/ vs /b/).

---

## 1.2 Vowel Sounds

Vowels are sounds produced with an open vocal tract — air flows freely without obstruction.

### Short Vowels:
| Symbol | Example words |
|--------|--------------|
| /æ/ | cat, bat, man |
| /ɛ/ | bed, red, hen |
| /ɪ/ | sit, big, milk |
| /ɒ/ | hot, dog, top |
| /ʌ/ | cup, sun, run |
| /ʊ/ | book, look, cook |

### Long Vowels:
| Symbol | Example words |
|--------|--------------|
| /iː/ | see, feet, beat |
| /ɑː/ | car, far, hard |
| /ɔː/ | call, door, more |
| /uː/ | food, moon, pool |
| /ɜː/ | bird, turn, learn |

### Diphthongs (two vowel sounds glided together):
| Symbol | Example words |
|--------|--------------|
| /eɪ/ | say, day, name |
| /aɪ/ | my, sky, high |
| /ɔɪ/ | boy, coin, joy |
| /aʊ/ | cow, now, loud |
| /əʊ/ | go, snow, road |

---

## 1.3 Consonant Sounds

Consonants are produced when airflow is partially or fully blocked in the mouth.

| Consonant type | Sounds | Example |
|---------------|--------|---------|
| **Plosives** (stopped) | /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /g/ | pen, big, top, dog |
| **Fricatives** (friction) | /f/, /v/, /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/ | fan, van, sit, zoo, shoe |
| **Nasals** | /m/, /n/, /ŋ/ | man, no, ring |
| **Approximants** | /l/, /r/, /j/, /w/ | leg, run, yes, wet |
| **Affricates** | /tʃ/, /dʒ/ | church, judge |

---

## 1.4 Stress and Rhythm

**Word stress** is emphasis placed on a syllable in a word.
- 'PREsent (noun) vs pre'SENT (verb)
- 'REcord (noun) vs re'CORD (verb)

**Sentence stress:** Content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) receive stress; function words (articles, prepositions) usually do not.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Identify the sound that is different from the others in each set:
a) cat, bat, call, sat
b) sea, lean, bread, dream

**Solution:**
a) "call" — the others have the short /æ/ vowel sound (cat /kæt/, bat /bæt/, sat /sæt/), while "call" has the /ɔː/ sound.
b) "bread" — the others have the long /iː/ sound (sea /siː/, lean /liːn/, dream /driːm/), while "bread" has the short /ɛ/ sound.

---

**Example 2 (BECE type):** Which word has the same vowel sound as "food"?
a) good  b) flood  c) moon  d) book

**Solution:** c) **moon** — /uː/ sound, same as food /fuːd/. "Good" and "book" have /ʊ/; "flood" has /ʌ/.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests: identifying vowel sounds, matching words with similar sounds, and word stress patterns.

> **Common trap:** Spelling ≠ sound. "Bread" and "bead" look similar but have different vowel sounds. Always think about the SOUND, not the letter.

# TOPIC 2: Reading Skills — Comprehension and Inference
**Topic ID:** JSS-ENG-002-01

## 2.1 Definition of Reading Comprehension

**Reading comprehension** is the ability to read a text, understand its meaning, and draw conclusions from it. It involves both **literal understanding** (what the text directly says) and **inferential understanding** (what the text implies).

---

## 2.2 Types of Reading

| Type | Purpose | When to use |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Skimming** | Get a general overview quickly | First look at a text; checking if relevant |
| **Scanning** | Find specific information | Looking for names, dates, figures |
| **Intensive reading** | Deep understanding of every detail | Comprehension exercises; studying |
| **Extensive reading** | Reading for general understanding/pleasure | Novels; newspapers |

---

## 2.3 Comprehension Question Types

| Type | What it asks | Example stem |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Literal** | Information directly in the text | "What did...?" "When did...?" |
| **Inferential** | Implied meaning; read between the lines | "What does the writer imply...?" |
| **Vocabulary in context** | Meaning of a word/phrase as used | "What does the word 'X' mean in line 4?" |
| **Summary** | Condense main points | "In 3 sentences, summarise..." |
| **Writer's purpose/attitude** | Why wrote this; how does writer feel | "What is the writer's attitude toward...?" |

---

## 2.4 Summary Writing Technique

Steps for writing a summary:
1. Read the passage carefully
2. Identify the main idea of EACH paragraph
3. Note the key supporting points
4. Write in YOUR OWN WORDS — paraphrase, do not copy verbatim
5. Use the number of points or sentences specified
6. Use clear, direct language

**Number of points:** In BECE, you are often asked for 5–6 points. Write one clearly expressed point per sentence.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Read the following and answer the questions:

*"The harmattan is a dry, dusty wind that blows from the Sahara Desert across West Africa, typically between November and March. It brings cold nights and warm days, and causes the air to become very dry. People often develop cracked lips, dry skin, and respiratory problems during this season. However, farmers welcome the harmattan because it dries harvested crops quickly."*

**Questions:**
a) When does the harmattan blow?
b) What problems does it cause?
c) In your own words, explain why farmers welcome the harmattan.

**Solutions:**
a) The harmattan blows **between November and March**.
b) It causes **cracked lips, dry skin, and respiratory problems**.
c) Farmers welcome the harmattan because the dry, warm air helps to **dry their harvested crops quickly**, making it easier to store and sell the crops without them rotting.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** comprehension tests literal questions (direct answers from text), vocabulary meaning from context, and a summary question.

> **Common trap:** For vocabulary questions, the answer is the meaning in CONTEXT, not the general dictionary definition. A word like "sharp" means different things in different sentences — always read the line before and after.

# TOPIC 3: Writing — Paragraphing, Letters and Essays
**Topic ID:** JSS-ENG-003-01

## 3.1 Paragraphing

A **paragraph** is a group of sentences that develops one main idea. Every paragraph has:
- **Topic sentence** — States the main idea (usually first)
- **Supporting sentences** — Give details, examples, explanations
- **Concluding sentence** — Summarises or links to the next point

**Properties of a good paragraph:**
| Property | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **Unity** | All sentences relate to ONE main idea |
| **Coherence** | Sentences are logically connected; ideas flow |
| **Completeness** | The main idea is fully developed |

**Linking words (cohesive devices):**
- Addition: furthermore, in addition, also, moreover
- Contrast: however, on the other hand, nevertheless, although
- Result: therefore, consequently, as a result, thus
- Sequence: firstly, secondly, finally, then, subsequently

---

## 3.2 Formal Letter Writing

**Formal letters** are written to official bodies, institutions, or people you do not know personally.

**Format of a Formal Letter:**
```
                    [Your address — top right]
                    [Town, State]
                    [Date]

[Recipient's name/title]
[Their address — left side]

Dear Sir/Madam (or Dear Mr/Mrs [Surname]),

[Body — paragraphs]

Yours faithfully, (if you don't know their name)
OR
Yours sincerely, (if you used their name in the greeting)

[Your signature]
[Your full name — printed]
```

**Types of formal letters:**
- Letter of complaint
- Application letter (for job or admission)
- Letter to a newspaper
- Letter to a government official

---

## 3.3 Informal Letter Writing

**Informal letters** are written to friends and family.

**Format:**
```
                    [Your address — top right]
                    [Date]

Dear [First name],

[Body — conversational tone]

Your friend/Yours truly/With love,
[Your name]
```

---

## 3.4 Essay Types

| Type | Purpose | Key features |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Narrative** | Tell a story | Uses past tense; chronological events; characters |
| **Descriptive** | Describe a person/place/event | Vivid language; sensory details; adjectives |
| **Expository** | Explain or inform | Clear facts; logical order; neutral tone |
| **Argumentative/Discursive** | Present a case; argue for or against | Clear position; evidence; counterarguments |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Write a formal letter to the principal of your school requesting permission to form a reading club.

**Model Answer:**
```
                    14 Broad Street,
                    Surulere,
                    Lagos.
                    21 October, 2026.

The Principal,
Government Secondary School,
Lagos.

Dear Sir/Madam,

REQUEST FOR PERMISSION TO FORM A READING CLUB

I write on behalf of a group of students in JSS 3 who are keen readers 
and wish to establish a Reading Club in our school. We believe this 
initiative will help students improve their reading skills, broaden their 
vocabulary, and develop a lifelong love of learning.

We propose to meet every Friday during the lunch break in the school 
library, under the supervision of our English teacher. We humbly request 
your approval and any support you are able to provide.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Yours faithfully,

[Signature]
Isaac Areo
```

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests formal and informal letter formats (headings, salutation, complimentary close), essay writing (narrative, descriptive, argumentative), and paragraph organisation.

> **Common trap:** "Yours faithfully" is ONLY used when you do NOT know the recipient's name (Dear Sir/Madam). If you used their name (Dear Mr Johnson), close with "Yours sincerely."

# TOPIC 4: Parts of Speech — Identification and Usage
**Topic ID:** JSS-ENG-004-01

## 4.1 The Eight Parts of Speech

| Part of speech | Definition | Examples |
|---------------|-----------|---------|
| **Noun** | Name of a person, place, thing, or idea | teacher, Lagos, book, love |
| **Pronoun** | Replaces a noun | he, she, it, they, we, I |
| **Verb** | Shows action or state of being | run, is, seems, became |
| **Adjective** | Describes a noun | tall, happy, three, red |
| **Adverb** | Modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb | quickly, very, well, always |
| **Preposition** | Shows relationship between noun and another word | in, on, at, by, for, with |
| **Conjunction** | Joins words or clauses | and, but, or, because, although |
| **Interjection** | Expresses sudden emotion | Oh! Wow! Ouch! Hurray! |

---

## 4.2 Types of Nouns

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Common** | General names | boy, city, animal |
| **Proper** | Specific names (capitalised) | Emeka, Lagos, WAEC |
| **Collective** | Group of persons/things | a flock of birds, a pride of lions |
| **Abstract** | Ideas; cannot be touched | love, freedom, anger |
| **Concrete** | Physical; can be touched | table, rain, food |
| **Countable** | Can be counted | book/books, chair/chairs |
| **Uncountable** | Cannot be counted | water, sand, advice |

---

## 4.3 Types of Verbs

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Action** | Shows what subject does | run, eat, speak |
| **Linking** | Connects subject to its description | is, are, seem, become |
| **Auxiliary (helping)** | Assists main verb | have, do, will, can, should |
| **Transitive** | Requires an object | She **kicked** the ball |
| **Intransitive** | Does not require an object | He **slept** |

### Verb Tenses:
| Tense | Form | Example |
|-------|------|---------|
| Simple present | Base form (+s/es for 3rd person) | She reads every day |
| Simple past | -ed or irregular | He went home |
| Simple future | will + base | They will come |
| Present continuous | is/am/are + -ing | I am eating |
| Past continuous | was/were + -ing | We were playing |

---

## 4.4 Types of Pronouns

| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| **Personal** | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| **Possessive** | mine, yours, his, hers, ours |
| **Reflexive** | myself, yourself, himself, herself |
| **Relative** | who, whom, which, that |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those |
| **Interrogative** | who, what, which, whom |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Identify the part of speech of the underlined word:
a) **Quickly** she ran to the door.
b) The **beautiful** dress was expensive.
c) He plays football **with** his friends.

**Solutions:**
a) **Quickly** — **adverb** (modifies the verb "ran")
b) **Beautiful** — **adjective** (modifies the noun "dress")
c) **With** — **preposition** (shows the relationship between "football" and "his friends")

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests identification and usage of all eight parts of speech, noun types, verb tenses, and pronoun types.

> **Common trap:** Some words can be different parts of speech depending on how they are used. "Fast" can be an adjective ("a fast car") or adverb ("she ran fast"). Always check the word's function in the sentence, not just its form.

# TOPIC 5: Comprehension and Summary Skills
**Topic ID:** JSS-ENG-005-01

## 5.1 Advanced Comprehension Techniques

### Identifying the Main Idea:
The **main idea** is the most important point the writer is making. It is often found:
- In the first sentence of a paragraph (topic sentence)
- In the title or heading
- Repeated or reinforced throughout the passage

### Identifying Tone and Mood:
| Tone | Key words/signals |
|------|-----------------|
| **Informative** | Factual language; statistics; "it is established that..." |
| **Critical** | Negative evaluations; "fails to," "unfortunately," "regrettably" |
| **Sympathetic** | Emotional language; expresses concern or understanding |
| **Humorous** | Irony, exaggeration, light-hearted language |
| **Persuasive** | Imperative verbs; rhetorical questions; "we must..." |

---

## 5.2 Vocabulary in Context

Words often have multiple meanings. To find the meaning **in context**:
1. Read the sentence containing the word
2. Read the sentence(s) before and after
3. Ask: "What makes sense here?"
4. Substitute your answer back and check it works

**Example:** "The new policy has been **suspended** pending review."
Here, "suspended" means **postponed/halted temporarily** — not "hanging from something."

---

## 5.3 Summary Writing — Step-by-Step

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1. Pre-read | Skim the passage for topic and structure |
| 2. Identify | Find the main idea and key supporting points |
| 3. Note | Jot down the points briefly in your own words |
| 4. Draft | Write the summary — usually 5–6 points each in ONE sentence |
| 5. Check | Did you use your own words? Did you include all key points? |

**Number your points** clearly as 1, 2, 3... when asked for "points."

**Words NOT to copy:** Avoid lifting whole sentences from the passage. You will lose marks.

---

## 5.4 Cloze Tests

A **cloze test** is a passage with blanks to fill in. Strategies:
- Read the whole passage first before attempting any blank
- Look at the words before and after the blank
- Consider the part of speech needed (noun? verb? adjective?)
- Check for grammar agreement (singular/plural, verb tense)
- If unsure, choose the option that sounds most natural

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Read the extract and answer:

*"The school library is one of the most valuable resources available to students. Yet many students rarely visit it, preferring instead to rely on their textbooks alone. A library offers a wealth of books, reference materials, and increasingly, digital resources. Students who develop the habit of reading widely will find that their vocabulary, general knowledge, and exam performance all improve significantly."*

In not more than **four points**, summarise what the writer says about the school library and its benefits.

**Solution:**
1. The school library is a very valuable resource for students.
2. It contains books, reference materials, and digital resources beyond what textbooks provide.
3. Many students unfortunately do not make use of the library.
4. Students who read widely using library resources improve their vocabulary, general knowledge, and performance in examinations.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** summary questions require your own words, clear point-by-point presentation, and proper sentence structure.

> **Common trap:** Copying sentences directly from the passage earns ZERO marks for those points, even if they are correct. Always paraphrase.

> A summary should contain ONLY the requested number of points. Do not pad your answer — extra irrelevant points do not earn marks and waste time.
