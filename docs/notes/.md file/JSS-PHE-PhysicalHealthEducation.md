# TOPIC 1: Team Games — Rules, Skills and Benefits
**Topic ID:** JSS-PHE-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Team Games

**Team games** are organised competitive physical activities in which individuals work together as a unit to achieve a common goal, usually scoring more points than the opposing team within a set of agreed rules.

---

## 1.2 Popular Team Games in Nigerian Schools

| Game | Players per side | Main skills | Key rules |
|------|-----------------|-------------|----------|
| **Football (Soccer)** | 11 | Dribbling, passing, shooting, heading | No handling by outfield players; offside rule; 90 minutes |
| **Basketball** | 5 | Dribbling, passing, shooting, rebounding | No walking with ball; 4 quarters × 10 minutes |
| **Volleyball** | 6 | Serving, setting, spiking, blocking | 3 touches per side; rally scoring; 5 sets |
| **Handball** | 7 | Throwing, catching, jumping | 3 steps with ball; goalie area restricted |
| **Cricket** | 11 | Batting, bowling, fielding | Two innings; batter is "out" by several methods |
| **Table Tennis** | 1 or 2 (singles/doubles) | Serving, attacking, defending | First to 11 points; 2-clear-point margin |

---

## 1.3 Benefits of Team Games

| Benefit | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Physical fitness** | Improves cardiovascular endurance, strength, speed, and coordination |
| **Social skills** | Teaches cooperation, communication, and respect for teammates and opponents |
| **Leadership** | Players develop decision-making, responsibility, and leadership qualities |
| **Discipline** | Rules teach adherence to regulations and respect for authority |
| **Sportsmanship** | Win or lose gracefully; fair play values |
| **Stress relief** | Physical activity reduces stress and improves mental well-being |

---

## 1.4 Officials in Team Games

| Game | Officials |
|------|----------|
| Football | Referee, 2 Assistant Referees (linesmen), 4th Official |
| Basketball | 2 Referees, Timekeeper, Scorer |
| Volleyball | 1st Referee, 2nd Referee, Linesmen, Scorer |

**General duties of officials:**
- Apply and enforce the rules
- Control the game fairly
- Handle player disputes
- Keep time

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR benefits of participating in team games.

**Solution:**
1. **Physical fitness** — Team games involve running, jumping, and other vigorous activities that improve the cardiovascular system and muscular strength.
2. **Social development** — Players learn to cooperate with others, communicate effectively, and develop respect for teammates and opponents.
3. **Discipline and rule-following** — The rules of each game teach players to respect authority and follow established regulations.
4. **Sportsmanship** — Team games foster values of fairness, respect, and graciousness in both victory and defeat.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the rules and skills of major team games, benefits of team sports, and the duties of game officials.

> **Common trap:** Volleyball uses **rally scoring** — every rally (play) produces a point, regardless of which side served. This is different from traditional side-out scoring. A set is won at 11 points (or 15 in the deciding set) with a 2-point margin.

# TOPIC 2: Personal Hygiene and Communicable Diseases
**Topic ID:** JSS-PHE-002-01

## 2.1 Definition of Personal Hygiene

**Personal hygiene** is the practice of keeping the body clean and healthy to prevent disease and maintain a presentable appearance. It involves daily care of the body to remove dirt, bacteria, and other microorganisms.

---

## 2.2 Practices of Personal Hygiene

| Body area | Good hygiene practice |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **Body/skin** | Bathe daily; use soap to remove sweat and bacteria |
| **Hair** | Wash regularly; brush/comb to remove dirt and lice |
| **Teeth** | Brush at least twice daily; floss; avoid excess sugar |
| **Hands** | Wash with soap and water before eating, after toilet |
| **Feet** | Wash between toes; dry well; trim toenails |
| **Clothes** | Wash regularly; wear clean underwear daily |
| **Ears** | Clean gently; do not insert objects |

---

## 2.3 Communicable Diseases — Classification and Prevention

**Communicable diseases** are illnesses caused by microorganisms (pathogens) that can be passed from one person to another.

### Types of Pathogens:
| Pathogen | Examples of diseases |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Bacteria** | Cholera, typhoid, tuberculosis (TB) |
| **Viruses** | Malaria (no—protozoa), influenza, HIV/AIDS, measles |
| **Protozoa** | Malaria, amoebic dysentery |
| **Fungi** | Ringworm, athlete's foot |
| **Worms (helminths)** | Roundworm, tapeworm |

### Common Diseases at JSS Level:

| Disease | Cause | Mode of Spread | Prevention |
|---------|-------|---------------|-----------|
| **Malaria** | Plasmodium protozoa | Bite of female Anopheles mosquito | Mosquito nets, clear stagnant water |
| **Typhoid** | Salmonella typhi bacteria | Contaminated food and water | Vaccination, hand washing, safe water |
| **Cholera** | Vibrio cholerae bacteria | Contaminated water/food | Safe water, ORS, sanitation |
| **Ringworm** | Fungal infection | Contact with infected person/items | Avoid sharing towels/combs; antifungal cream |
| **Malaria (prevention)** | — | — | Use ITNs; eliminate stagnant water; repellents |
| **Scabies** | Sarcoptes mite | Skin-to-skin contact | Avoid close contact; permethrin cream |

---

## 2.4 Modes of Disease Transmission

| Mode | Description | Example diseases |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Airborne** | Pathogens in air droplets (coughing, sneezing) | TB, measles, influenza |
| **Water/food-borne** | Contaminated water or food | Cholera, typhoid |
| **Vector-borne** | Carried by insects | Malaria (mosquito), sleeping sickness (tsetse fly) |
| **Direct contact** | Touching infected person or objects | Ringworm, scabies |
| **Sexual contact** | During sexual intercourse | HIV/AIDS, gonorrhoea |
| **Blood** | Sharing needles; transfusion | HIV/AIDS, hepatitis B |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR personal hygiene practices that can prevent the spread of disease in school.

**Solution:**
1. **Regular handwashing** with soap and water before eating and after using the toilet kills bacteria and viruses that cause illnesses like cholera and typhoid.
2. **Wearing clean clothes** — changing and washing school uniforms regularly reduces the transfer of bacteria and fungi that cause skin infections.
3. **Covering the mouth and nose** when coughing or sneezing prevents airborne pathogens (bacteria, viruses) from spreading to classmates.
4. **Avoiding sharing personal items** such as combs, towels, and eating utensils prevents the transmission of infections like ringworm and oral diseases.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests personal hygiene practices, types of communicable diseases, modes of transmission, and prevention strategies.

> **Common trap:** Malaria is caused by a PROTOZOAN (Plasmodium), NOT a bacterium or virus. It is transmitted by the FEMALE Anopheles mosquito — not any mosquito.

# TOPIC 3: The Skeletal and Muscular Systems
**Topic ID:** JSS-PHE-003-01

## 3.1 The Skeletal System

**Definition:** The skeletal system is the framework of bones and cartilage that gives the body its shape, protects internal organs, and enables movement.

**The human skeleton has approximately 206 bones.**

### Functions of the Skeleton:
| Function | Explanation |
|----------|-------------|
| **Support** | Provides the framework that holds the body upright |
| **Protection** | Skull protects brain; ribcage protects heart and lungs |
| **Movement** | Bones act as levers; muscles pull on bones to cause movement |
| **Blood cell production** | Red bone marrow produces red and white blood cells |
| **Mineral storage** | Stores calcium and phosphorus |

---

## 3.2 Types of Bones

| Type | Shape | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Long bones** | Length > width | Femur, humerus, tibia |
| **Short bones** | Similar length and width | Carpals (wrist), tarsals (ankle) |
| **Flat bones** | Broad, flat surfaces | Skull, shoulder blade (scapula), sternum |
| **Irregular bones** | Irregular shape | Vertebrae, facial bones |
| **Sesamoid bones** | Embedded in tendons | Patella (kneecap) |

---

## 3.3 Types of Joints

A **joint** is where two or more bones meet.

| Type of joint | Movement | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| **Ball and socket** | Movement in all directions | Hip, shoulder |
| **Hinge** | Bending and straightening (one plane) | Knee, elbow |
| **Pivot** | Rotation | Neck (atlas and axis) |
| **Gliding/Plane** | Sliding movement | Wrist, ankle |
| **Fixed (immovable)** | No movement | Skull sutures |

---

## 3.4 The Muscular System

**Definition:** The muscular system consists of all the muscles in the body that produce movement, maintain posture, and generate heat.

### Types of Muscles:
| Type | Control | Location | Example |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Skeletal (voluntary)** | Conscious control | Attached to bones | Biceps, quadriceps |
| **Smooth (involuntary)** | Automatic | Internal organs | Stomach, intestines |
| **Cardiac (involuntary)** | Automatic | Heart | Heart muscle |

### How Muscles Work:
Muscles work in **antagonistic pairs** — when one contracts, the other relaxes.

**Example — Arm curl:**
- **Biceps** (front of upper arm) contracts → arm bends (flexion)
- **Triceps** (back of upper arm) relaxes
- To straighten the arm: Triceps contracts, Biceps relaxes (extension)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR functions of the skeleton.

**Solution:**
1. **Support** — The skeleton forms the body's structural framework, holding the body upright and supporting soft tissues.
2. **Protection** — The skull protects the brain; the ribcage shields the heart and lungs from injury.
3. **Movement** — Bones act as levers; muscles attached to bones contract to produce movement at joints.
4. **Blood cell production** — Red bone marrow in long bones produces red blood cells (for oxygen transport) and white blood cells (for immunity).

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests functions of the skeleton and muscles, types of joints, and how antagonistic muscle pairs work.

> **Common trap:** The knee is a HINGE joint (bends in one direction), NOT a ball-and-socket joint. The hip and shoulder are ball-and-socket joints because they move in all directions.

# TOPIC 4: Physical Fitness Components
**Topic ID:** JSS-PHE-004-01

## 4.1 Definition of Physical Fitness

**Physical fitness** is the body's ability to carry out daily tasks efficiently without experiencing undue fatigue, with enough energy left for leisure activities and to respond to emergencies.

---

## 4.2 Health-Related Components of Fitness

These directly relate to good health and disease prevention:

| Component | Definition | How to develop it |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Cardiovascular endurance** | The ability of the heart and lungs to supply oxygen to working muscles over a long period | Running, swimming, cycling |
| **Muscular strength** | The maximum force a muscle can exert in a single effort | Weight training, press-ups |
| **Muscular endurance** | The ability of a muscle to exert force repeatedly over time | Press-ups, sit-ups, cycling |
| **Flexibility** | The range of motion at a joint | Stretching exercises, yoga |
| **Body composition** | The ratio of fat to lean body mass (muscles, bones, organs) | Diet + exercise |

---

## 4.3 Skill-Related Components of Fitness

These relate to performance in sport and physical activities:

| Component | Definition | Important for |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Speed** | How quickly you can move your body or a body part | Sprinting, football |
| **Agility** | Ability to change direction quickly and accurately | Basketball, tennis |
| **Balance** | Ability to maintain body position and equilibrium | Gymnastics, martial arts |
| **Coordination** | Using different body parts together smoothly | Throwing, catching, dribbling |
| **Power** | Combination of strength and speed (explosive force) | Jumping, shot put |
| **Reaction time** | Time taken to respond to a stimulus | Sprint start, goalkeeper |

---

## 4.4 Benefits of Physical Fitness

| Benefit | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Better health** | Reduces risk of obesity, heart disease, diabetes |
| **Improved performance** | Better at sports and physical tasks |
| **Mental well-being** | Exercise reduces stress, anxiety, and depression |
| **Social confidence** | Physically fit people often feel more confident |
| **Healthy weight** | Exercise burns calories and maintains healthy body weight |
| **Stronger bones** | Weight-bearing exercise increases bone density |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Distinguish between muscular strength and muscular endurance, and give ONE activity that develops each.

**Solution:**
**Muscular strength** is the maximum force a muscle group can produce in a single exertion. It is about how MUCH force you can generate once. Activity: Weightlifting — specifically attempting to lift the heaviest possible weight once (1-rep maximum).

**Muscular endurance** is the ability of a muscle group to produce force repeatedly over a period of time without fatigue. It is about how LONG you can keep going. Activity: Press-ups — performing as many press-ups as possible without stopping tests and develops muscular endurance.

**Key difference:** Strength = one maximum effort; Endurance = many repeated efforts.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the five health-related components and six skill-related components — their definitions, differences, and example activities for developing each.

> **Common trap:** Power is NOT the same as strength. **Power = Strength × Speed** — it is explosive force, like in jumping or throwing. A very strong person is not necessarily powerful if they are slow.

# TOPIC 5: Safety Education and Accident Prevention
**Topic ID:** JSS-PHE-005-01

## 5.1 Definition of Safety Education

**Safety education** is the process of teaching individuals how to identify potential hazards, take precautions to prevent accidents, and respond appropriately when accidents occur.

---

## 5.2 Types of Accidents and Their Causes

### At School:
| Accident | Common causes |
|---------|--------------|
| Falls | Running in corridors; wet floors; uneven surfaces |
| Cuts/lacerations | Broken glass; sharp equipment |
| Burns/scalds | Laboratory/cooking accidents; hot liquids |
| Eye injuries | Flying objects; chemical splashes |
| Sports injuries | Improper warm-up; bad technique; no protective gear |

### On the Road:
| Accident | Common causes |
|---------|--------------|
| Pedestrian knock-down | Crossing without looking; no pedestrian bridge; speeding vehicles |
| Road traffic crashes | Speeding, drunk driving, bad roads, overloading |

---

## 5.3 Accident Prevention Strategies

| Setting | Prevention strategies |
|---------|----------------------|
| **At school** | Walk (don't run) in corridors; clear spillages immediately; use equipment properly; wear protective gear in lab/sports |
| **In the home** | Store chemicals safely; install smoke detectors; cover electrical outlets; never overload sockets |
| **On the road** | Use pedestrian crossings; look both ways; wear reflective clothing at night; never run across the road |
| **During sports** | Warm up properly; use correct technique; wear appropriate protective gear; follow rules |

---

## 5.4 First Aid

**First aid** is the immediate assistance given to an injured or ill person before professional medical help arrives.

**Aims of first aid (The 3 Ps):**
1. **Preserve life** — prevent the situation from becoming fatal
2. **Prevent worsening** — stop the condition from getting worse
3. **Promote recovery** — help the casualty toward recovery

### Basic First Aid for Common Accidents:

| Incident | First aid response |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Bleeding wound** | Apply direct pressure with clean cloth; elevate; bandage |
| **Burns** | Cool with running cold water for 10–20 min; do NOT use ice, butter, or toothpaste |
| **Fracture (suspected)** | Immobilise; do NOT attempt to straighten; call for help |
| **Choking** | Back blows (5 sharp blows between shoulder blades); Heimlich manoeuvre |
| **Fainting** | Lay flat; elevate legs; loosen tight clothing; ensure fresh air |
| **Nosebleed** | Sit upright; lean slightly forward; pinch soft part of nose for 10 minutes; do NOT tilt head back |

---

## 5.5 Road Safety

**Key road safety rules for students:**
- Always use pedestrian crossings (zebra crossings, footbridges)
- Look RIGHT–LEFT–RIGHT before crossing
- Walk facing oncoming traffic when there is no pavement
- Do NOT use mobile phones while crossing the road
- Wear seat belts in vehicles

**Key organisations:**
- **FRSC (Federal Road Safety Corps)** — Enforces road safety regulations in Nigeria
- **VIO (Vehicle Inspection Office)** — Inspects vehicles for roadworthiness

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** What should you do if a classmate fainted during morning assembly?

**Solution:**
1. **Call for help** — Alert a teacher or adult immediately.
2. **Lower the person to the ground** carefully to prevent injury from falling.
3. **Lay them flat on their back** and **elevate their legs** slightly (about 30 cm) to improve blood flow to the brain.
4. **Loosen any tight clothing** around the neck, chest, or waist to ease breathing.
5. **Ensure fresh air** — clear the crowd away; open windows if indoors; fan gently.
6. **Monitor breathing** — if they do not regain consciousness within a minute, seek emergency medical help.
7. **Do NOT give water** or any substance orally to an unconscious person — they may choke.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests types of accidents, prevention strategies, first aid procedures for specific incidents, and road safety rules.

> **Common trap:** For burns, NEVER apply butter, oil, toothpaste, or ice. ONLY cool, running water (for 10–20 minutes) is correct. Ice can damage burned tissue; butter traps heat and causes infection.
