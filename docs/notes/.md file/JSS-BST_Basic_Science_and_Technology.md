## Living Things

### Characteristics, Classification and Cell Structure
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BST-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- State the seven characteristics of living things
- Classify organisms into the five kingdoms
- Distinguish between plant and animal cells using diagrams
- Describe the functions of major cell organelles
- Explain the importance of microorganisms to humans and the environment

#### 📖 Notes
- Characteristics of living things: MRS GREN — Movement, Respiration, Sensitivity, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion, Nutrition
- Five kingdoms: Monera (bacteria — no nucleus), Protista (amoeba, euglena — single-celled eukaryotes), Fungi (mushrooms, moulds — decomposers), Plantae (plants — photosynthesis), Animalia (animals — heterotrophs)
- Viruses: not classified in any kingdom; non-living outside host; consist of genetic material (DNA or RNA) + protein coat
- Cell theory: all organisms are made of cells; cell is basic unit of life; cells arise from existing cells
- Animal cell structures: cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, ribosomes, Golgi body, lysosomes
- Plant cell additional structures: cell wall (cellulose), chloroplast (photosynthesis), large permanent vacuole
- Differences: plant cells have cell wall, chloroplasts and permanent vacuole; animal cells do not
- Microscope: light microscope in JSS labs; parts — eyepiece, objective lens, stage, mirror/light, coarse and fine focus
- Magnification: total magnification = eyepiece lens power × objective lens power
- Beneficial microorganisms: decomposition (recycle nutrients), nitrogen fixation (Rhizobium), fermentation (yeast for bread/beer), antibiotics (Penicillium mould)
- Harmful microorganisms: disease-causing bacteria (cholera, tuberculosis), viruses (malaria — wrong, it's protozoa; HIV/AIDS)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Prepare and observe onion epidermis and cheek cells under the microscope; draw and label both at ×100 and ×400 magnification
2. Create a classification key for 10 organisms: is it living? Does it have cells? Does it have a nucleus? Does it photosynthesise?
3. Investigate fermentation: mix yeast, warm water and sugar in a bottle with a balloon on top; observe CO₂ production over 30 minutes

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests MRS GREN (memorise all 7), plant vs. animal cell differences, kingdom names with examples, and microscope parts
> Know: chloroplasts and cell wall are in PLANT cells only; lysosomes are mainly in ANIMAL cells
> Trap: malaria is caused by Plasmodium (a PROTOZOAN — kingdom Protista), NOT a bacterium; HIV/AIDS is caused by a VIRUS

### Nutrition, Photosynthesis and Digestive System
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BST-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Explain the process of photosynthesis and identify its raw materials and products
- Describe the human digestive system and identify organs and their functions
- Identify the six food classes, their functions and Nigerian food sources
- Identify common nutritional deficiency diseases in Nigeria
- Perform food tests to identify nutrients

#### 📖 Notes
- Photosynthesis equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (in chloroplasts)
- Raw materials: carbon dioxide (from air through stomata), water (from soil through roots), light energy (from sun)
- Products: glucose (stored as starch or used for energy) and oxygen (released as by-product)
- Leaf adaptations for photosynthesis: broad flat shape (surface area), chlorophyll (absorbs light), stomata (gas exchange), transparent epidermis (light penetration)
- Food classes: carbohydrates (energy: rice, yam, bread), proteins (growth: beans, meat, fish, eggs), fats (energy storage: palm oil, avocado), vitamins (regulatory: fruits, leafy vegetables), minerals (structural: milk, beans), water (solvent)
- Food tests: iodine → blue-black (starch present); Benedict's → brick red (reducing sugar); Biuret → purple (protein); brown paper → translucent mark (fat)
- Human digestive system: mouth (chewing, salivary amylase) → oesophagus (peristalsis) → stomach (HCl, pepsin) → small intestine (absorption, bile, pancreatic juice) → large intestine (water absorption) → rectum → anus
- Villi: finger-like projections in small intestine; increase surface area; microvilli on villi; glucose and amino acids into blood; fatty acids into lacteal
- Nutritional deficiency diseases: kwashiorkor (protein → swollen belly, discoloured hair), marasmus (total calorie → wasting), scurvy (Vitamin C → bleeding gums), rickets (Vitamin D/Ca → bow legs), night blindness (Vitamin A → poor night vision), anaemia (iron → fatigue, pale skin), goitre (iodine → swollen neck)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Test three food samples (bread, milk, vegetable oil) for starch, protein and fat; record observations and conclusions in a results table
2. Demonstrate the conditions needed for photosynthesis: use a destarched plant, cover one leaf with foil; after 6 hours in light, test both for starch
3. Match deficiency diseases to their missing nutrient: make a matching game; learn by repetition

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE consistently tests food tests (know ALL four), deficiency diseases with their nutrients, and photosynthesis equation
> Memorise ALL 7 deficiency diseases with their cause, symptom, and food source
> Trap: photosynthesis uses CO₂ and produces O₂; respiration uses O₂ and produces CO₂ — they are OPPOSITE processes but both happen in plants

## Matter and Energy

### States of Matter, Separation and Changes
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BST-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the three states of matter and their properties in terms of particle arrangement
- Explain changes of state using particle theory
- Distinguish between physical and chemical changes
- Describe and explain methods of separating mixtures
- Distinguish between elements, compounds and mixtures

#### 📖 Notes
- States of matter: solid (fixed shape, fixed volume, particles close together, vibrate in fixed positions), liquid (fixed volume, no fixed shape, particles close but free to move), gas (no fixed shape or volume, particles far apart, move rapidly in all directions)
- Changes of state: melting (solid → liquid, absorbs heat), freezing (liquid → solid, releases heat), evaporation (liquid → gas surface, absorbs heat), boiling (liquid → gas throughout, at boiling point), condensation (gas → liquid, releases heat), sublimation (solid → gas directly: e.g. iodine, dry ice)
- Physical change: no new substance formed; reversible; change in state, shape or size (e.g. melting ice, dissolving salt)
- Chemical change: new substance(s) formed; usually irreversible; change in chemical composition (e.g. burning wood, cooking food, rusting)
- Pure substance vs. mixture: pure substance (one type of particle — element or compound); mixture (two or more substances combined, not chemically combined)
- Separation of mixtures: filtration (insoluble solid from liquid), evaporation (soluble solid from solution — crystallisation), distillation (liquids with different boiling points), chromatography (coloured substances — different solubility), magnetism (magnetic from non-magnetic), decantation (liquid from settled solid), sieving (different particle sizes)
- Elements: substances that cannot be broken down into simpler substances; 118 known elements; arranged in periodic table
- Compounds: substances formed when two or more elements combine chemically; fixed ratio; different properties from constituent elements (e.g. H₂O is neither hydrogen nor oxygen)
- Mixtures: two or more substances physically combined; variable ratio; each retains its own properties (e.g. saltwater)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Set up a filtration experiment to separate sand from salt water; then evaporate the filtrate to recover salt crystals
2. Investigate paper chromatography using black marker pen ink and water; observe how many colours separate and explain why
3. Classify 10 household changes as physical or chemical: ironing a shirt, burning wood, mixing salt in water, cooking egg, melting butter — justify each

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests separation method for given mixture type, physical vs. chemical change identification, and states of matter properties
> Know WHICH separation method for WHICH mixture type: filtration = insoluble solid + liquid; evaporation = dissolved solid + liquid; distillation = two miscible liquids
> Trap: dissolving is a PHYSICAL change (you can evaporate the water to get the salt back); burning is CHEMICAL (you cannot un-burn wood)

### Energy — Forms, Transfer and Electricity
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BST-002-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify and give examples of the different forms of energy
- Explain energy conversion with practical examples
- State the law of conservation of energy
- Describe the structure of a simple electric circuit
- Explain the difference between conductors and insulators

#### 📖 Notes
- Forms of energy: kinetic (moving objects), potential (stored in position — gravitational, elastic), heat/thermal, light, sound, chemical (food, fuel, batteries), electrical, nuclear
- Law of conservation of energy: energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only change from one form to another
- Energy conversions: solar panel (light → electrical), electric motor (electrical → kinetic), generator (kinetic → electrical), battery (chemical → electrical), bulb (electrical → light + heat), car engine (chemical → kinetic + heat)
- Electric circuit: closed path through which electricity flows; components: battery/cell (source of EMF), switch (open/close circuit), bulb/resistor (load), wires (conductor)
- Series circuit: components connected end to end; same current through all; one break stops the whole circuit
- Parallel circuit: components on separate branches; each has same voltage; one branch can be broken without affecting others
- Electrical symbols: cell (─┤├─), battery (─┤├┤├─), bulb (─⊗─), switch (─/─), resistor (─▭─), ammeter (─A─), voltmeter (─V─)
- Conductors: allow electricity to flow; metals (copper, aluminium, silver, gold); carbon (graphite)
- Insulators: do not allow electricity to flow; rubber, plastic, glass, wood, ceramic, dry air
- Domestic electrical safety: fuses, circuit breakers, earthing, not overloading sockets, not using damaged wires
- Renewable energy in Nigeria: solar power (rural electrification), small hydro, wind; importance for reducing dependence on fossil fuels

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Build a simple series circuit with a battery, two bulbs, and a switch; observe what happens when one bulb is removed; rebuild as parallel
2. Investigate conductors and insulators: use a circuit with a battery, bulb and two wires; test various objects (coin, rubber, pencil, wood, nail, plastic) to see which complete the circuit
3. Energy conversion poster: draw a chain of energy conversions in a hydroelectric dam from water in reservoir to light in a home

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests energy form identification, energy conversion examples, circuit diagrams (series vs. parallel), and conductor/insulator classification
> Know all circuit symbols by drawing — BECE may ask you to draw a circuit
> Trap: in a SERIES circuit, removing ONE bulb breaks the whole circuit; in PARALLEL, the other bulbs continue — this is a common BECE question

## Environment and Ecology

### Ecosystems, Pollution and Conservation
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BST-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define ecosystem and describe its components
- Construct food chains and food webs from given data
- Explain the effects of pollution on the environment
- Describe conservation measures in Nigeria
- Explain the causes and consequences of climate change

#### 📖 Notes
- Ecosystem: a community of living organisms (biotic) interacting with each other and their non-living environment (abiotic) in a specific area
- Biotic components: producers (green plants), consumers (animals), decomposers (bacteria and fungi)
- Abiotic components: temperature, light, water, soil, wind, minerals
- Food chain: shows feeding relationships; arrows show direction of energy flow; e.g. grass → grasshopper → lizard → eagle
- Food web: multiple interconnected food chains; more realistic representation of ecosystem feeding
- Trophic levels: producers (level 1), primary consumers (level 2), secondary consumers (level 3), tertiary consumers (level 4)
- Energy loss: only 10% of energy passed to next level; rest lost as heat, excretion, movement
- Pollution: introduction of harmful substances into the environment; types — air (vehicle exhaust, factory smoke), water (oil spill, sewage, chemicals), land/soil (refuse dumps, chemicals, erosion), noise (generators, traffic, construction)
- Effects of pollution in Nigeria: oil spills (Niger Delta — Ogoni land devastation), deforestation (loss of biodiversity), flooding (blocked drainage + building on flood plains)
- Conservation: protection and wise use of natural resources; National Parks in Nigeria: Yankari (Bauchi), Cross River, Okomu (Edo), Kamuku
- Climate change: global warming due to greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide) trapping heat; effects — rising sea levels, erratic rainfall, flooding, drought
- Individual actions: reduce, reuse, recycle; planting trees; using energy-saving bulbs; reducing waste

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Draw a food web for a savanna ecosystem: grass, acacia tree, zebra, impala, lion, hyena, vulture, termite, fungi; identify the producers and top predator
2. Investigate pollution in the local environment: survey the school's waste disposal methods; identify pollutants and suggest improvements
3. Calculate: if grass contains 10,000 J of energy, how much reaches a hawk (4th trophic level) using the 10% rule?

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests food chain construction, energy flow (10% rule), pollution types and their effects, and conservation area examples in Nigeria
> Know 3 National Parks in Nigeria by name and state: Yankari (Bauchi), Cross River NP, Kamuku (Kaduna)
> Trap: arrows in food chains point FROM the organism eaten TO the organism eating — they show ENERGY FLOW, not 'who eats who'
