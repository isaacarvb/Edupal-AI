## Introduction to Business

### Business, Commerce and Entrepreneurship
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BUS-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define business and explain why people engage in business
- Distinguish between industry, commerce and services
- Identify entrepreneurial characteristics and opportunities
- Explain the importance of small businesses to the Nigerian economy
- Identify career opportunities in business

#### 📖 Notes
- Business: any activity carried out to produce goods or services to satisfy human needs and make profit
- Types of business activities: production/industry (extractive — mining, farming; manufacturing — turning raw materials into goods; construction — building), commerce (trade + aids to trade), services (direct services — doctors, teachers, lawyers; commercial services — transport, banking, insurance)
- The business cycle: production → distribution → consumption
- Needs vs. wants: needs are essential (food, shelter, clothing); wants are desirable (TV, car, luxury goods)
- Entrepreneur: person who identifies a business opportunity, takes risks to organise resources and manage a business for profit
- Characteristics of an entrepreneur: initiative, risk-taking, creativity, determination, leadership, problem-solving, self-confidence
- Entrepreneurship opportunities in Nigeria: agribusiness, ICT services, fashion/tailoring, food processing, transport, e-commerce
- SMEs (Small and Medium Enterprises): backbone of Nigerian economy; major employer; SMEDAN, BOI (Bank of Industry) support SMEs
- Business ownership types at JSS level: sole proprietorship, partnership, cooperative
- Importance of business to national development: employment creation, tax revenue, innovation, import substitution

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Identify a business problem in your school: e.g. lack of affordable stationery; design a simple business idea to solve it; present to class
2. Interview a local small business owner (market trader, artisan, etc.): find out how they started, their challenges, and their advice
3. Class entrepreneurship fair: each student brings one product made from local materials; display and present their 'business'

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests industry classification (extractive, manufacturing, construction), distinction between industry and commerce, and entrepreneur characteristics
> Know examples of each industry type: extractive (coal mining, cassava farming), manufacturing (Nasco biscuits, Dangote cement), construction (Julius Berger roads)
> Trap: banks, insurance and transport are AIDS TO TRADE (part of commerce) — not industries — though transport companies may engage in industry

## Office Practice and Keyboarding

### Office Practice and Communication Skills
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BUS-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the functions and organisation of a business office
- Identify office equipment and their uses
- Apply business communication skills (memos, letters, reports, emails)
- Demonstrate basic keyboarding techniques using the home row method
- Explain filing and record management systems

#### 📖 Notes
- Office: the nerve centre of a business; where information is collected, processed, stored and distributed
- Functions of an office: receiving information, recording information, organising information, distributing information, safeguarding assets (filing and custody)
- Types of offices: open plan (no partitions — good for teamwork), private (individual cubicles — good for confidential work), virtual (remote, no physical location)
- Office equipment: typewriter/computer, photocopier, printer, scanner, shredder, telephone, filing cabinet, fax machine
- Business communication types: internal (within organisation — memo, circular, notice board, email) and external (outside — business letter, report, fax, email)
- Memo (memorandum): internal document; TO, FROM, DATE, SUBJECT headings; formal but internal
- Business letter: external; sender's address, date, recipient's address, subject, salutation, body, complimentary close, signature
- Report: structured document presenting findings; headings — Title, Introduction, Findings, Recommendations, Conclusion
- Keyboarding: QWERTY keyboard; home row keys (ASDF JKL;); correct finger placement; posture (straight back, feet flat); aim for speed and accuracy
- Touch typing: typing without looking at keyboard; increases speed and efficiency
- Filing systems: alphabetical (by name), numerical (by number), geographical (by location), chronological (by date), subject (by topic)
- Record management: creating, maintaining, protecting, storing and disposing of records systematically

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Write a memo from the school principal to all teachers about a change in assembly time; follow correct memo format
2. Practise home row keyboarding: ASDF JKL; for 10 minutes daily; track words per minute over a week
3. Set up a simple filing system for the class register using alphabetical order; practice retrieving a file by name

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests office functions, internal vs. external communication types, memo vs. business letter format, and filing system names
> Memo format: TO: / FROM: / DATE: / SUBJECT: — no 'Dear' or 'Yours faithfully'
> Trap: a memo is INTERNAL (within the organisation); a business letter is EXTERNAL (to outside parties) — they have different formats

## Basic Bookkeeping

### Introduction to Bookkeeping and Basic Accounts
**Class:** JSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-BUS-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define bookkeeping and distinguish it from accounting
- Apply the double-entry principle to basic transactions
- Record transactions in a simple cash book
- Prepare a simple income and expenditure statement
- Understand the importance of record-keeping for small businesses

#### 📖 Notes
- Bookkeeping: systematic recording of financial transactions of a business; quantitative, routine task
- Accounting: broader concept; includes bookkeeping + interpretation + analysis + reporting
- Why records matter: know profit/loss, track debtors/creditors, meet tax obligations, plan the business, access loans
- Source documents: receipt (proof of payment), invoice (bill for goods), delivery note, bank statement
- Accounting equation: Assets = Capital + Liabilities; business is separate from owner (entity concept)
- Assets: things owned by the business (cash, stock, equipment, building, debtors)
- Liabilities: amounts owed by the business (creditors, loans, bank overdraft)
- Capital: amount invested by owner; owner's equity
- Double-entry: every transaction has two effects — one account debited, another credited; total debits = total credits
- Simple cash book: records all cash received (receipts — left/debit side) and cash paid out (payments — right/credit side); balance = closing cash
- Simple income statement: Income − Expenses = Profit (or Loss); used for small businesses and petty traders
- Ledger accounts (T-accounts): Dr side (debit) on left; Cr side (credit) on right; balanced at end of period

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Record a week of a small suya seller's transactions in a simple cash book; balance the book; identify closing cash
2. Given: Cash ₦50,000; Debtors ₦20,000; Goods ₦30,000; Loan ₦40,000; calculate Capital using the accounting equation
3. Categorise 15 items as assets, liabilities or capital: motor vehicle, bank loan, cash in till, merchandise, creditors, building, overdraft, equipment

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests accounting equation, asset/liability/capital classification, simple cash book balancing, and bookkeeping vs. accounting distinction
> The accounting equation is the foundation: A = C + L; if two values are given, you can always find the third
> Trap: capital is NOT the same as profit — capital is what the owner put in; profit is what was earned from trading
