# TOPIC 1: Business and Commerce — Introduction
**Topic ID:** JSS-BUS-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Business

**Business** is any activity carried out by individuals or organisations to produce goods or provide services in order to earn profit or achieve other objectives.

**Commerce** is the branch of business concerned with the exchange of goods and services. It includes trade and all the activities that assist trade (banking, insurance, transport, warehousing, advertising).

---

## 1.2 Types of Business Activities

| Activity | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Production** | Creating goods from raw materials | Farming, manufacturing |
| **Commerce** | Buying and selling goods; assistance to trade | Trading, banking, insurance |
| **Services** | Providing non-tangible benefits | Teaching, medicine, repairs |

---

## 1.3 Types of Business Organisation

| Type | Description | Key features |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Sole proprietorship** | One person owns and runs the business | Simple; unlimited liability; all profit to owner |
| **Partnership** | 2–20 persons own and run together | Shared capital and profit; governed by Partnership Act |
| **Private limited company (Ltd)** | Shareholders; shares NOT on stock exchange | Limited liability; "Ltd" in name |
| **Public limited company (Plc)** | Shares sold to the public on stock exchange | Limited liability; "Plc" in name; more capital |
| **Cooperative** | Members-owned; democratic | Surplus shared equally; common in farming/marketing |
| **Public enterprise** | Government-owned | Provide essential services; profit not primary goal |

---

## 1.4 Aids to Trade

These are activities that make trade possible:

| Aid | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| **Banking** | Provides funds; safe money storage; payment services |
| **Insurance** | Protects against risks and losses |
| **Transport** | Moves goods from producers to consumers |
| **Warehousing** | Stores goods until needed; bridges time gap |
| **Advertising** | Informs consumers about products |
| **Communication** | Telephone, internet — connects buyers and sellers |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR differences between a sole proprietorship and a private limited company.

**Solution:**

| Feature | Sole Proprietorship | Private Limited Company |
|---------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Ownership** | Single person | 2–50 shareholders |
| **Liability** | Unlimited (personal assets at risk) | Limited (liability = value of shares only) |
| **Capital** | Limited to owner's resources | Can raise more by selling shares |
| **Decision-making** | Owner decides alone | Board of directors decides |

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests types of business organisations, their features, and the aids to trade.

> **Common trap:** **Limited liability** means shareholders can only lose what they invested (the value of their shares). If the company goes bankrupt, personal assets are NOT at risk. In a sole proprietorship, the owner has UNLIMITED liability — personal property can be seized to pay business debts.

# TOPIC 2: Office Practice and Communication
**Topic ID:** JSS-BUS-002-01

## 2.1 The Office

**Definition:** An **office** is the administrative centre of a business organisation where information is received, processed, stored, and communicated to help the organisation achieve its objectives.

---

## 2.2 Functions of an Office

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Receiving information** | Mail, telephone calls, emails, reports |
| **Recording information** | Filing, data entry, maintaining records |
| **Processing information** | Preparing reports, invoices, correspondence |
| **Distributing information** | Sending memos, letters, circulars to appropriate persons |
| **Storing information** | Maintaining files; database management |
| **Coordinating activities** | Organising meetings, schedules, and tasks |

---

## 2.3 Office Equipment

| Equipment | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| **Computer/Laptop** | Word processing, spreadsheets, email, databases |
| **Printer** | Producing hard copies of documents |
| **Photocopier** | Reproducing documents |
| **Telephone/Intercom** | Internal and external communication |
| **Filing cabinet** | Storing physical files and documents |
| **Fax machine** | Sending document images via telephone |
| **Shredder** | Destroying confidential documents |

---

## 2.4 Business Communication

**Communication** is the process of conveying information, ideas, or instructions from one person or organisation to another.

### Types of Business Communication:
| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| **Written** | Letters, memos, emails, reports, notices |
| **Verbal/Oral** | Telephone calls, meetings, interviews, presentations |
| **Visual** | Charts, diagrams, graphs, signboards |
| **Electronic** | Email, text messages, social media, video calls |

### Internal vs External Communication:
| Type | Direction | Examples |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Internal** | Within the organisation | Memo, circular, notice board |
| **External** | Outside the organisation | Letter, email to a client |

---

## 2.5 Business Documents

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Purchase order** | Buyer requests goods from supplier |
| **Invoice** | Seller requests payment for goods/services supplied |
| **Receipt** | Proof that payment has been made |
| **Delivery note** | Confirms goods have been delivered |
| **Credit note** | Reduces buyer's debt (for returns or overcharging) |
| **Statement of account** | Summary of all transactions between buyer and seller |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR functions of a business office.

**Solution:**
1. **Receiving information** — The office receives and sorts incoming mail, telephone calls, and reports from external and internal sources.
2. **Processing information** — Raw data is processed into useful documents such as reports, invoices, and financial statements.
3. **Storing information** — The office maintains an organised system of filing physical and digital records for future reference.
4. **Communicating information** — The office distributes information through memos, letters, circulars, and emails to the relevant departments and persons.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests functions of the office, types of business communication, and major business documents.

> **Common trap:** A **memo** (memorandum) is internal — it goes within an organisation. A **letter** is external — it goes outside the organisation. They have different formats and are NOT interchangeable.

# TOPIC 3: Bookkeeping — Recording Business Transactions
**Topic ID:** JSS-BUS-003-01

## 3.1 Definition of Bookkeeping

**Bookkeeping** is the systematic process of recording all financial transactions of a business in an organised manner. It forms the foundation of accounting.

**Purpose:**
- Know whether the business is making profit or loss
- Keep track of money owed and money owing
- Provide records for tax purposes
- Enable management decisions

---

## 3.2 The Double Entry Principle

Every financial transaction affects at least TWO accounts. For every debit entry, there is an equal credit entry.

**Golden rules:**
| Account type | Debit (Dr) | Credit (Cr) |
|-------------|-----------|------------|
| Asset account | Increases | Decreases |
| Liability account | Decreases | Increases |
| Capital account | Decreases | Increases |
| Revenue/Income | Decreases | Increases |
| Expense account | Increases | Decreases |

---

## 3.3 Books of Original Entry

**Source documents → Books of original entry → Ledger**

| Book | Transactions recorded |
|------|--------------------|
| **Sales day book** | Credit sales |
| **Purchases day book** | Credit purchases |
| **Cash book** | Cash and bank receipts and payments |
| **Returns inward book** | Goods returned TO the business by customers |
| **Returns outward book** | Goods returned BY the business to suppliers |
| **Journal** | All other transactions not in other books |

---

## 3.4 The Ledger and Trial Balance

**Ledger:** The main book of accounts; each account has its own page (T-account).

**T-account format:**
```
     Name of Account
  Dr (Debit)   |   Cr (Credit)
  Date | Item | ₦  |  Date | Item | ₦
```

**Trial Balance:** A list of all ledger account balances, arranged in debit and credit columns. If correctly done, totals MUST be equal.

---

## 3.5 Simple Profit and Loss

**Gross profit = Sales − Cost of goods sold**
**Net profit = Gross profit − Expenses**

**Example:**
- Sales = ₦50,000
- Cost of goods sold = ₦30,000
- Gross profit = ₦20,000
- Expenses (rent + wages) = ₦8,000
- **Net profit = ₦12,000**

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** A small shop had sales of ₦75,000 and the cost of goods sold was ₦45,000. Expenses for the month were ₦15,000. Calculate the (a) gross profit and (b) net profit.

**Solution:**
a) Gross profit = Sales − Cost of goods sold
= ₦75,000 − ₦45,000 = **₦30,000**

b) Net profit = Gross profit − Expenses
= ₦30,000 − ₦15,000 = **₦15,000**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests books of original entry, the purpose of bookkeeping, and simple profit/loss calculations.

> **Common trap:** Gross profit is NOT the final profit. It does not yet account for expenses (rent, salaries, electricity). Net profit deducts those expenses and gives the true profit figure. Always read what the question is asking — gross OR net.

# TOPIC 4: Marketing, Distribution and Retailing
**Topic ID:** JSS-BUS-004-01

## 4.1 Marketing at JSS Level

**Marketing** is all the activities involved in getting goods and services from the producer to the final consumer. It includes identifying what consumers want, producing it, pricing it, promoting it, and distributing it.

### The 4 Ps of Marketing (simplified):
| P | What it means | Example |
|---|--------------|---------|
| **Product** | The goods or service being sold | Bread, mobile phone |
| **Price** | What the consumer pays | ₦200 per loaf |
| **Place** | Where and how the product is available | Local shop, online |
| **Promotion** | How the product is advertised and sold | Radio ad, poster |

---

## 4.2 Channels of Distribution

The path goods take from producer to consumer.

| Channel | Route | Example |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Direct** | Producer → Consumer | Farmer selling at farm gate |
| **Short** | Producer → Retailer → Consumer | Manufacturer selling to Shoprite |
| **Long (traditional)** | Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer | Most consumer goods |

---

## 4.3 Wholesalers

**Wholesaler:** A trader who buys goods in LARGE quantities from manufacturers and sells in SMALLER quantities to retailers.

**Functions of the wholesaler:**
- Buying in bulk (breaks bulk for retailers)
- Storing goods in warehouses
- Financing (extending credit to retailers)
- Risk-bearing (price changes, spoilage)
- Transport (delivers to retailers)
- Provides market information to manufacturers

---

## 4.4 Retailers

**Retailer:** A trader who buys from wholesalers (or manufacturers) and sells in SMALL quantities to the final consumer.

**Types of retailers:**
| Type | Description | Nigerian example |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Supermarket** | Self-service; many products | Shoprite, SPAR |
| **Market trader** | Sells in open markets | Alaba, Balogun market |
| **Hawker** | Sells from place to place | Street food vendors |
| **Online store** | Sells via internet | Jumia, Konga |
| **Kiosk/Corner shop** | Small neighbourhood stores | Local provision store |

**Functions of the retailer:**
- Provides convenience — close to consumers
- Breaks bulk — sells small quantities
- Offers variety — products from many manufacturers
- Provides credit to known customers
- Gives after-sales services
- Feeds back market information

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR functions of a wholesaler.

**Solution:**
1. **Bulk buying** — Wholesalers purchase large quantities from manufacturers, allowing manufacturers to sell in volume without dealing with thousands of small orders.
2. **Storage** — Wholesalers maintain large warehouses, storing goods until retailers need them, thus bridging the time gap between production and consumption.
3. **Breaking bulk** — Wholesalers buy in bulk but sell smaller quantities to retailers who cannot afford or store very large amounts.
4. **Financing/Credit** — Wholesalers often allow retailers to buy on credit, helping retailers who may not have immediate cash to purchase stock.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the functions of wholesalers and retailers, channels of distribution, and the 4 Ps.

> **Common trap:** A retailer sells to the FINAL CONSUMER (person who uses the goods). If a trader sells to another trader for resale, they are a WHOLESALER, not a retailer — it's about WHO you sell to, not how big your shop is.

# TOPIC 5: Consumer Rights, Business Ethics and Economics
**Topic ID:** JSS-BUS-005-01

## 5.1 Consumer Rights

**Consumer** = a person who buys goods and services for personal use.

**Consumer rights (based on UN Guidelines):**

| Right | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Right to safety** | Products should not be harmful |
| **Right to information** | Truthful information about products (labels, ingredients, expiry dates) |
| **Right to choose** | Access to a variety of products at competitive prices |
| **Right to be heard** | Consumer complaints should be addressed |
| **Right to redress** | Refund or replacement for defective goods |
| **Right to education** | Learn about being a wise consumer |
| **Right to a healthy environment** | Clean, safe surroundings |

---

## 5.2 Consumer Protection in Nigeria

**Key bodies:**
| Body | Function |
|------|---------|
| **Consumer Protection Council (CPC)** | Investigates complaints; protects consumer rights in Nigeria |
| **NAFDAC** | Regulates food, drugs, cosmetics — ensures they are safe |
| **SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria)** | Sets and enforces quality standards for products |
| **NESREA** | Protects the environment |

---

## 5.3 Business Ethics

**Business ethics** is the application of moral principles and standards to business activities.

**Ethical business practices:**
- Honest advertising (no misleading claims)
- Fair pricing (no price gouging)
- Fair treatment of employees (fair wages, safe conditions)
- Environmental responsibility (no illegal dumping)
- Transparency and accountability in financial matters

**Unethical practices (to avoid):**
- Selling expired or fake products
- Bribery and corruption
- Exploitation of workers
- False advertising
- Tax evasion

---

## 5.4 Basic Economic Concepts

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Needs** | Things essential for survival (food, water, shelter, clothing) |
| **Wants** | Things desirable but not essential (TV, phone, car) |
| **Scarcity** | Resources are limited; not all wants can be satisfied |
| **Opportunity cost** | The next best alternative given up when making a choice |
| **Production** | Creating goods and services to satisfy wants |
| **Consumption** | Using goods and services to satisfy wants |

**Factors of production:**
- **Land** — Natural resources (soil, water, minerals)
- **Labour** — Human effort (physical and mental)
- **Capital** — Man-made resources used in production (machines, buildings)
- **Entrepreneurship** — Risk-taking; organising the other factors

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State FOUR rights of a consumer and mention TWO government agencies in Nigeria that protect consumer rights.

**Solution:**
**Four consumer rights:**
1. **Right to safety** — Consumers have the right to be protected from products that are dangerous to health or life.
2. **Right to information** — Consumers must be given honest, accurate information about products (price, ingredients, expiry date, instructions).
3. **Right to choose** — Consumers should have access to a range of products and not be forced to buy from one supplier.
4. **Right to redress** — Consumers have the right to receive compensation, replacement, or refund when they purchase defective or substandard goods.

**Two government agencies:**
1. **Consumer Protection Council (CPC)** — Investigates consumer complaints and takes action against businesses that violate consumer rights.
2. **NAFDAC** — Ensures that foods, drugs, and cosmetics on the Nigerian market meet safety standards and are not fake or expired.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests consumer rights, government bodies protecting consumers, business ethics, and basic economic concepts (needs vs wants, opportunity cost, factors of production).

> **Common trap:** NAFDAC's role is specifically food, drugs, cosmetics, and chemicals — NOT general consumer goods. SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria) handles general product quality standards. Know which agency handles what.
