## The Individual and Society

### Family, Community and Social Values
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-SCI-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe types and functions of the family
- Explain the importance of community and community development
- Demonstrate core social values in daily interactions
- Analyse the impact of social values on national development
- Evaluate the role of the school in promoting social values

#### 📖 Notes
- Definition of family: the smallest social unit; the basic building block of society
- Types of family: nuclear (father, mother, children — most common in cities), extended (includes grandparents, aunts, uncles, cousins — more common in rural Nigeria), single-parent, blended (step-family), foster family
- Functions of the family: biological (reproduction), economic (provision of needs), socialisation (teaching values and norms), protection (safety, care), psychological (love, belonging)
- Community: a group of people living in the same area and sharing common interests or characteristics
- Types of community: rural (farming, close-knit, traditional), urban (diverse, industrial, fast-paced), sub-urban (between rural and urban)
- Community development: process of improving living conditions through collective action; local government involvement, CBOs (Community-Based Organisations)
- Social values: shared beliefs and standards that guide behaviour in society
- Core social values in Nigeria: honesty (telling the truth), respect (honouring elders and authority), responsibility (being accountable), tolerance (accepting differences), compassion (caring for others), hard work, patriotism
- Cultural values vs. universal values: traditional practices may vary; some values transcend culture (human dignity, justice)
- The school as a social institution: transmits values, develops skills, prepares citizens
- Agents of socialisation: family (first and most powerful), school, peer group, mass media, religious institutions

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Draw a genogram (family tree) showing at least three generations; identify nuclear and extended family members
2. Class survey: identify one community problem in your neighbourhood (e.g. poor roads, waste disposal) and design a simple community action plan
3. Values auction: each student receives 10 value tokens; they bid for values listed on cards; at the end, discuss why they chose those values

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests types of family with examples, functions of family, types of community, and list of social values
> Know all 5+ functions of the family: biological, economic, socialisation, protection, psychological (emotional)
> Trap: a nuclear family is NOT the same as a small family — it specifically refers to ONLY parents and their unmarried children, regardless of size

## Nigerian Government and Citizenship

### Three Tiers and Three Arms of Government
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-SCI-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the three tiers of government in Nigeria
- Explain the roles of the executive, legislative and judicial arms
- Identify Nigerian citizens' rights and responsibilities
- Explain the role of INEC in democracy
- Demonstrate civic responsibilities in school and community

#### 📖 Notes
- Three tiers of government in Nigeria: Federal (President + National Assembly + Judiciary — Abuja), State (Governor + State House of Assembly — each state capital), Local Government (Chairman + Legislative Council — 774 LGAs)
- Federal capital: Abuja; 36 states + FCT; 774 LGAs
- Three arms of government: Executive (implements/executes laws), Legislature (makes laws), Judiciary (interprets laws and settles disputes)
- Federal Executive: President (Commander-in-Chief + Head of State + Head of Government), Vice President, Ministers, security agencies
- National Assembly: Senate (109 senators — 3 per state + 1 FCT) + House of Representatives (360 members); passes bills, approves budget, confirms ministerial appointments
- Judiciary: Supreme Court (highest; final appeals), Court of Appeal, Federal High Court, State High Courts; independent of other arms
- Separation of powers and checks and balances: prevents any one arm from becoming too powerful
- Fundamental Human Rights (Chapter IV, 1999 Constitution): right to life, dignity, freedom from slavery, fair hearing, freedom of thought/expression/religion, right to education
- Citizens' responsibilities: obey the law, pay taxes, vote, defend the country, respect others' rights, protect the environment
- INEC: conducts elections; registers voters and parties; declares results; maintains election register
- Democracy: government of the people, by the people, for the people (Abraham Lincoln)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Create a government structure chart: three arms × three tiers = nine-box grid; fill in the name of the institution in each box
2. Mock parliament: class creates a bill ('All students must wear uniforms'); debate in two houses; vote; the 'President' assents or withholds
3. Rights and responsibilities card sort: given 20 cards (half rights, half responsibilities), sort them into two groups; then match each right to a corresponding responsibility

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests the three tiers and arms, functions of each arm, fundamental human rights, and INEC's role
> Know the numbers: 109 senators, 360 House reps, 36 states, 774 LGAs
> Trap: the JUDICIARY does not make laws (that is the Legislature) — it INTERPRETS and APPLIES the law

## Democracy, Human Rights and Peace

### Democracy, Electoral Process and Conflict Resolution
**Class:** JSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-SCI-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Explain the principles and features of democracy
- Describe the electoral process in Nigeria step by step
- Apply conflict resolution strategies in school and community
- Explain the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC)
- Evaluate Nigeria's democratic journey from independence to present

#### 📖 Notes
- Democracy: government based on free and fair elections, majority rule with minority rights, rule of law, separation of powers, freedom of press
- Democratic principles: sovereignty of the people, political equality, majority rule, protection of minority rights, civil liberties, accountability of leaders
- Nigeria's democratic history: Independence 1960 (parliamentary), 1st Republic 1963 (republican), military (1966), 2nd Republic (1979–1983), military (1983–1999), 4th Republic (1999–present)
- Electoral process in Nigeria: voter registration → party primaries → campaign period → polling day (accreditation → voting → counting) → collation → declaration → possible tribunal petition
- INEC responsibilities: register voters, register parties, conduct elections, train ad hoc officials, announce results, handle electoral offences
- Types of electoral malpractice: underage voting, ballot box snatching, vote buying, result manipulation, voter intimidation
- Conflict: a disagreement or clash between opposing groups; types — intrapersonal (within oneself), interpersonal (between individuals), intergroup (between groups), international
- Causes of conflict: injustice, inequality, intolerance, resource competition, miscommunication, tribalism, religious differences
- Conflict resolution methods: negotiation (direct discussion), mediation (neutral third party), arbitration (binding decision from arbitrator), litigation (courts), compromise
- UNCRC (1989): 54 articles protecting children's rights: survival (life, food, water), development (education, play), protection (from abuse, exploitation), participation (express views)
- Child rights in Nigeria: Child Rights Act 2003; 28 states have domesticated it; challenges of implementation

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Simulate a community conflict: two village groups want the only borehole for different purposes; apply mediation steps to reach a compromise
2. Research: which year was Nigeria under civilian government vs. military rule? Draw a governance timeline from 1960 to 2023
3. Write a letter to the INEC Chairman suggesting two reforms to improve Nigeria's electoral process, using evidence from recent elections

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests principles of democracy, electoral process steps, INEC's role, conflict resolution methods, and child rights
> Know the 4 categories of UNCRC rights: survival, development, protection, participation
> Trap: negotiation is DIRECT (no third party); mediation has a NEUTRAL third party who facilitates; arbitration has a third party whose decision is BINDING

## Environment and Sustainable Development

### Environmental Challenges and SDGs
**Class:** JSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-SCI-004-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify major environmental challenges facing Nigeria
- Explain the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
- Suggest practical solutions to environmental problems at community level
- Evaluate individual and collective responsibility for the environment
- Explain the relationship between environment and national development

#### 📖 Notes
- Environmental challenges in Nigeria: deforestation (logging, farming, fuelwood), desertification (northward advance of Sahara Desert), flooding (Lagos, Rivers State), erosion (Anambra, Enugu, Cross River), oil pollution (Niger Delta), solid waste disposal, water pollution
- Causes: overpopulation, poverty, poor governance, industrial activities, climate change, poor land use practices
- Effects of deforestation: loss of biodiversity, increased soil erosion, reduced rainfall, climate change contribution
- Effects of oil pollution (Niger Delta): destruction of fishing grounds, contamination of drinking water, health problems, displacement of communities
- UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): 17 goals adopted by 193 UN member states in 2015; achieve by 2030
- Key SDGs relevant to Nigeria: SDG 1 (No poverty), SDG 2 (Zero hunger), SDG 3 (Good health), SDG 4 (Quality education), SDG 6 (Clean water and sanitation), SDG 7 (Affordable clean energy), SDG 11 (Sustainable cities), SDG 13 (Climate action), SDG 15 (Life on land), SDG 16 (Peace and justice)
- Nigeria's SDG progress: mixed performance; education improvements (but still high out-of-school children), health challenges (malaria, maternal mortality), economic inequality
- Individual actions: recycling, saving water and energy, planting trees, reducing plastic use, proper waste disposal
- Collective actions: community clean-up, reforestation programmes, advocacy, policy reform campaigns

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Create a visual poster on one SDG that Nigeria is struggling with; include statistics, causes, and 3 achievable solutions
2. Conduct a school environmental audit: assess waste disposal, water use, energy use; present findings to school management
3. Debate: 'Economic development in Nigeria is incompatible with environmental protection' — support or oppose with evidence

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests major environmental problems in Nigeria, their causes and effects, and at least 5 SDGs by number and name
> Know which states/regions are associated with each problem: Niger Delta (oil pollution), Anambra/Enugu (erosion), Northern states (desertification), Lagos (flooding, waste)
> Trap: SDGs were adopted in 2015 (NOT 2000 — that was MDGs, the Millennium Development Goals) with a target of 2030
