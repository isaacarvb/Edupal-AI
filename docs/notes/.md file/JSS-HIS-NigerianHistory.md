# TOPIC 1: Pre-Colonial Nigeria — Kingdoms and Societies
**Topic ID:** JSS-HIS-001-01

## 1.1 Definition

**Pre-colonial Nigeria** refers to the period before British conquest and formal colonisation (i.e., before approximately 1900). During this period, various highly organised kingdoms, empires, and societies existed across the territory that would later become Nigeria.

---

## 1.2 Major Pre-Colonial Kingdoms and Empires

### The Benin Kingdom:
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Location** | Edo State (South-South Nigeria) |
| **Period** | Approximately 13th–19th centuries |
| **Ruler** | Oba of Benin |
| **Key achievement** | Renowned for **Benin Bronzes** — highly sophisticated brass and ivory artworks |
| **Significance** | Powerful centralised monarchy; engaged in trade with Europeans from the 15th century |

### The Oyo Empire:
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Location** | Southwestern Nigeria (Yoruba) |
| **Peak period** | 17th–18th centuries |
| **Ruler** | Alaafin of Oyo |
| **Key feature** | Powerful cavalry (horses); one of the largest empires in West Africa |
| **Decline** | Internal conflicts; Fulani jihad pressure in 19th century |

### The Sokoto Caliphate:
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Location** | Northern Nigeria |
| **Founded** | 1804 by Usman dan Fodio |
| **Ruler** | Sultan of Sokoto |
| **Key feature** | Founded on Islamic principles after the Fulani jihad; largest empire in sub-Saharan Africa at its peak |

### The Kanem-Bornu Empire:
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Location** | Lake Chad region (northeastern Nigeria and beyond) |
| **Period** | 9th–19th centuries |
| **Ruler** | Mai (later Sheikh) |
| **Key feature** | Trans-Saharan trade; Islam adopted early; one of the longest-surviving states in African history |

### The Igbo Societies:
- Largely **stateless/acephalous** — no centralised monarchy
- Governed by councils of elders, age grades, and title holders
- **Nri Kingdom** was a notable exception — spiritual authority over much of Igbo land
- **Arochukwu** — influential oracle and trade network

---

## 1.3 Social Organisation

| Society | System |
|---------|--------|
| Yoruba | Age grades; kingship; Ogboni society |
| Igbo | Elders' council; age grades; Okonko society |
| Hausa-Fulani | Emirates; Islamic scholars; hierarchical |
| Efik (Calabar) | Ekpe society; important in trade |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Identify TWO pre-colonial kingdoms in Nigeria and state ONE achievement of each.

**Solution:**
1. **The Benin Kingdom** — Achieved mastery of bronze and brass casting, producing world-famous Benin Bronzes that depicted the Oba, warriors, and court life. These artworks demonstrated an advanced level of artistic and technological skill.

2. **The Oyo Empire** — Built a powerful cavalry-based military force that enabled it to control a large area of southwestern Nigeria and exact tribute from neighbouring states. At its peak it was one of the most powerful states in West Africa.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the major pre-colonial kingdoms — their location, rulers, key features, and achievements.

> **Common trap:** The Sokoto Caliphate was founded in **1804** — it was founded by **Usman dan Fodio** (not by the British). It preceded British colonisation by nearly a century.

# TOPIC 2: British Colonialism in Nigeria
**Topic ID:** JSS-HIS-002-01

## 2.1 Definition

**Colonialism** is the policy and practice of a powerful country acquiring and maintaining control over another territory — exploiting its resources and governing its people.

**British colonialism in Nigeria** spanned from the mid-19th century to 1960 (independence).

---

## 2.2 How the British Came to Nigeria

| Stage | Period | Event |
|-------|--------|-------|
| **Trade and missionaries** | 1840s–1860s | British merchants and Christian missionaries arrived; palm oil trade flourished |
| **Consulate of Benin Coast** | 1849 | British established a consulate at Fernando Po (Bioko) to monitor trade |
| **Annexation of Lagos** | **1861** | British forcibly annexed Lagos under King Docemo |
| **Royal Niger Company** | 1886 | British government chartered the Royal Niger Company to control trade along the Niger |
| **Protectorates declared** | 1900 | British government took over from the Royal Niger Company; declared Protectorate of Northern Nigeria and Protectorate of Southern Nigeria |
| **Amalgamation** | **1914** | Sir Frederick Lugard amalgamated the Northern and Southern Protectorates to form the **Colony and Protectorate of Nigeria** |

---

## 2.3 Methods of Colonial Rule

### Indirect Rule:
- **Introduced by Lord Lugard** (from North)
- Governed through **existing traditional rulers** (emirs, chiefs)
- Cheaper; less European personnel needed
- Worked well in the North (where emirates were well organised)
- Less effective in Igbo areas (no traditional rulers with wide authority)

### Direct Rule:
- Used mainly in the South (Lagos, where Western education spread)
- British officials governed directly

---

## 2.4 Effects of British Colonialism

| Positive effects | Negative effects |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Introduction of Western education | Disruption of traditional political systems |
| Modern healthcare and hospitals | Economic exploitation (cash crops; cheap raw materials exported) |
| Roads, railways, ports | Arbitrary borders dividing ethnic groups |
| Unified legal and administrative system | Cultural suppression; missionary opposition to African traditions |
| Introduction of currency | Land alienation; forced labour in some areas |

---

## 2.5 Key Figures

| Person | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Lord Frederick Lugard** | Governor-General; amalgamated Nigeria in 1914 |
| **Lady Flora Shaw (Lugard)** | Proposed the name "Nigeria" (1897) |
| **King Jaja of Opobo** | Resisted British trade monopoly; deported 1887 |
| **Nana of Itsekiri** | Resisted British encroachment; deported 1894 |
| **Oba Ovonramwen** | Last free Oba of Benin; British invaded in 1897 |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** What is indirect rule? State TWO reasons why it was more successful in Northern Nigeria than in Eastern Nigeria.

**Solution:**
**Indirect rule** was a system of colonial administration in which the British governed through existing traditional rulers, using them as intermediaries to collect taxes, maintain order, and implement British policies.

**Reasons it was more successful in the North:**
1. **Well-established traditional rulers** — Northern Nigeria had powerful, hierarchical Emirates under Emirs with wide authority and subjects who were accustomed to obeying them. The British could simply work through these existing power structures.
2. **Centralised political system** — The hierarchical emirate system in the North provided ready-made administrative machinery. In the East, Igbo society was largely acephalous (no single paramount rulers), so the British had to create artificial "warrant chiefs" who had no traditional legitimacy, causing resistance.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests the annexation of Lagos (1861), amalgamation (1914), indirect rule (Lugard), and the effects of colonialism.

> **Common trap:** The name "Nigeria" was proposed by **Lady Flora Shaw** (later Lady Lugard) in 1897 — NOT by Lord Lugard. Amalgamation was done by Lugard in **1914**, not 1900. These dates are frequently tested.

# TOPIC 3: Post-Independence Nigeria
**Topic ID:** JSS-HIS-003-01

## 3.1 The Road to Independence

| Event | Date |
|-------|------|
| Richards Constitution | 1946 |
| Macpherson Constitution (wider participation) | 1951 |
| Lyttleton Constitution (full federalism) | 1954 |
| Self-government in the regions | 1957–1959 |
| **Independence** | **1 October 1960** |
| Nigeria becomes a Republic | **1 October 1963** |

**Key independence figures:**
| Person | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Nnamdi Azikiwe (Zik)** | First President of Nigeria; leader of NCNC |
| **Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa** | First Prime Minister of Nigeria |
| **Obafemi Awolowo** | Premier of Western Region; leader of AG |
| **Ahmadu Bello** | Sardauna of Sokoto; Premier of Northern Region; leader of NPC |

---

## 3.2 Nigerian Political History at a Glance

| Period | Type of government |
|--------|------------------|
| 1960–1966 | First Republic (parliamentary) |
| 1966 | Military coup (January — Ironsi; July — Gowon) |
| 1967–1970 | Civil War (Biafra War) |
| 1975–1979 | Military rule (Murtala Mohammed; Obasanjo) |
| 1979–1983 | Second Republic (presidential — Shagari) |
| 1983–1985 | Military coup (Buhari/Idiagbon) |
| 1985–1993 | Military rule (Babangida) |
| 1993 | June 12 annulment; Shonekan/Abacha |
| 1993–1998 | Military rule (Abacha) |
| 1998–1999 | Military transition (Abdulsalami) |
| 1999–present | Fourth Republic (presidential) |

---

## 3.3 The Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970)

**Causes:**
- Political instability and ethnic tension after independence
- January 1966 military coup (perceived as Igbo-led)
- July 1966 counter-coup; massacres of Igbo in the North
- Eastern Region's declaration of the Republic of Biafra (May 1967) under **Lt. Col. Odumegwu Ojukwu**

**Key facts:**
- Nigeria fought to keep the country united; led by **General Yakubu Gowon**
- Lasted 30 months (July 1967 – January 1970)
- Ended with Biafra's surrender on **15 January 1970**
- Reconstruction policy: "No victor, no vanquished" (Gowon)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** State TWO causes of the Nigerian Civil War and describe how it ended.

**Solution:**
**Two causes:**
1. **Political instability and ethnic tension** — The military coup of January 1966, perceived as an Igbo-dominated coup that killed mainly Northern and Western leaders, deepened ethnic suspicion and led to mass killings of Igbo people in the North.
2. **Declaration of Biafra** — After the massacres, Eastern Region military governor Odumegwu Ojukwu declared the Republic of Biafra in May 1967. The Federal Government viewed this as illegal secession and went to war to preserve national unity.

**End of the war:**
The Civil War ended on 15 January 1970 when Biafra surrendered to the Federal Government. General Gowon announced a policy of reconciliation — "No victor, no vanquished" — and began a Reconstruction, Rehabilitation, and Reconciliation (3Rs) programme to reintegrate the Igbo back into Nigeria.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests independence (1960), major political transitions, and the Civil War.

> **Common trap:** Nigeria became INDEPENDENT on 1 October **1960** but became a REPUBLIC on 1 October **1963**. These are two different events. Azikiwe was the first PRESIDENT (of the Republic); Tafawa Balewa was the first PRIME MINISTER (under independence).

# TOPIC 4: Great Nigerian Leaders and Heroes
**Topic ID:** JSS-HIS-004-01

## 4.1 Pre-Colonial and Colonial Heroes

| Person | Period | Achievement |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Usman dan Fodio** | 1804 | Founded the Sokoto Caliphate through the Fulani jihad; Islamic scholar and reformer |
| **Queen Amina of Zazzau** | 16th century | Warrior queen who expanded Zaria; known for military campaigns and trade |
| **Jaja of Opobo** | 19th century | Founded Opobo; resisted British attempts to control Niger Delta trade; deported 1887 |
| **Moremi Ajasoro** | Yoruba legend | Legendary heroine who sacrificed to save Ife from Igbo (Ugbo) raids |
| **Oba Ovonramwen** | Late 19th century | Last free Oba of Benin; resisted British invasion of 1897 |

---

## 4.2 Nationalist Leaders

| Person | Significance |
|--------|-------------|
| **Herbert Macaulay** (1864–1946) | "Father of Nigerian nationalism"; founded the Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP) in 1923; first organised political opposition to British rule |
| **Nnamdi Azikiwe** (1904–1996) | Pan-Africanist; editor; co-founded NCNC; first President of Nigeria (1963–1966) |
| **Obafemi Awolowo** (1909–1987) | Premier of Western Region; introduced FREE PRIMARY EDUCATION in the West (1955); free healthcare; modernised the region; built Liberty Stadium Ibadan |
| **Ahmadu Bello** (1910–1966) | Sardauna of Sokoto; Premier of Northern Region; developed the North educationally and politically |
| **Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti** (1900–1978) | Women's rights activist; led Abeokuta Women's Union; fought against taxation of women; "Mother of Africa" |

---

## 4.3 Post-Independence Leaders

| Person | Period | Contribution |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa** | 1960–1966 | First Prime Minister; skilled diplomat; active in OAU and Commonwealth |
| **Murtala Muhammed** | 1975–1976 | Moved federal capital to Abuja; reduced civil service; assassinated February 1976 |
| **Olusegun Obasanjo** | 1976–1979; 1999–2007 | Peacefully transferred power to civilians in 1979; returned as elected President 1999 |
| **MKO Abiola** (Moshood Abiola) | 1993 | Won June 12, 1993 presidential election (annulled by Babangida); died in detention 1998; declared democracy hero posthumously |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Who is known as the "Father of Nigerian Nationalism" and what did he do to deserve that title?

**Solution:**
**Herbert Samuel Heelas Macaulay** (1864–1946) is known as the "Father of Nigerian Nationalism."

He earned this title through:
1. **Political organisation** — He founded the **Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP)** in 1923, the first organised political party in Nigeria, which advocated for Nigerian rights and representation in the colonial government.
2. **Media activism** — He used his newspaper, the *Lagos Daily News*, to criticise British colonial policies, expose injustices, and rally Nigerians.
3. **Legal and civil rights battles** — He fought consistently in courts and public campaigns against British land appropriation and the erosion of traditional rights, notably winning a famous case about Lagos land ownership.
4. **Founding of NCNC** — In 1944, he co-founded the **National Council of Nigeria and the Cameroons (NCNC)** with Nnamdi Azikiwe, creating a broader nationalist movement that eventually led to independence.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests major pre-colonial, colonial, and post-independence leaders — their names, periods, and specific contributions.

> **Common trap:** Herbert Macaulay is the "Father of Nigerian Nationalism," but Azikiwe is associated with the independence movement more broadly. Awolowo introduced FREE PRIMARY EDUCATION in the West in 1955 — one of Africa's earliest such programmes.

# TOPIC 5: Nigeria's Neighbours and Regional History
**Topic ID:** JSS-HIS-005-01

## 5.1 Nigeria's Geographical Position

Nigeria is located in **West Africa**, bordered by:
- **North:** Niger Republic and Chad
- **East:** Cameroon
- **West:** Benin Republic
- **South:** Atlantic Ocean (Gulf of Guinea)

---

## 5.2 Nigeria's Neighbours — Brief Profiles

### Benin Republic (formerly Dahomey):
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Capital** | Porto-Novo (official); Cotonou (economic) |
| **Independence** | 1960 (from France) |
| **Language** | French (official); Fon, Yoruba |
| **Historical connection** | Ancient Dahomey Kingdom; shares Yoruba cultural ties with Nigeria |

### Niger Republic:
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Capital** | Niamey |
| **Independence** | 1960 (from France) |
| **Language** | French (official); Hausa, Zarma |
| **Historical connection** | Hausa-Fulani cultural ties; part of the Sokoto Caliphate's sphere |

### Chad:
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Capital** | N'Djamena |
| **Independence** | 1960 (from France) |
| **Language** | French and Arabic (official); Sara, others |
| **Historical connection** | Kanem-Bornu Empire extended into Chad; Lake Chad basin shared |

### Cameroon:
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Capital** | Yaoundé |
| **Independence** | 1960 (from France); British Southern Cameroons joined in 1961 |
| **Language** | French and English (bilingual) |
| **Historical connection** | Nigeria–Cameroon boundary dispute (Bakassi Peninsula); settled by ICJ 2002 in Cameroon's favour |

---

## 5.3 ECOWAS — Regional Integration

**Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)**:
- Founded: **28 May 1975** (Lagos Treaty)
- Headquarters: Abuja, Nigeria
- Members: 15 West African states
- Nigeria's role: Largest economy; provides most funding; founding and leading member

**ECOWAS objectives:**
- Promote free trade within West Africa
- Allow free movement of people, goods, and capital
- Achieve economic integration
- Maintain peace and security (ECOMOG military arm)

---

## 5.4 Trans-Saharan Trade Routes

**Before colonial rule**, the Sahara Desert was NOT a barrier — it was a trade highway.

**Goods traded:**
| North → South | South → North |
|--------------|--------------|
| Salt (Saharan), horses, textiles, books | Gold, kola nuts, slaves, leather goods |

**Key routes:**
- Trans-Saharan routes linked Kano, Katsina, and Bornu to North Africa and the Middle East
- Islam spread along these trade routes

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Name Nigeria's four land neighbours and state ONE historical or cultural connection with any TWO of them.

**Solution:**
Nigeria's four land neighbours are:
1. **Benin Republic** (West)
2. **Niger Republic** (North)
3. **Chad** (Northeast)
4. **Cameroon** (East)

**Historical connections:**
1. **Niger Republic** — The Hausa-Fulani people straddle the Nigeria-Niger border. The Sokoto Caliphate, founded in 1804, extended into what is now Niger Republic. Many communities on both sides share the same language (Hausa), cultural practices, and religion (Islam).

2. **Cameroon** — The Bakassi Peninsula dispute between Nigeria and Cameroon was one of the most significant bilateral conflicts in post-independence Nigerian history. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) ruled in 2002 that Bakassi belonged to Cameroon, and Nigeria transferred the territory in 2008 after diplomatic resolution.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests Nigeria's neighbours, their capitals, ECOWAS objectives and founding, and key trans-Saharan trade connections.

> **Common trap:** ECOWAS was founded in **1975** in Lagos (Lagos Treaty), NOT in Abuja — though Abuja is now its secretariat headquarters. Nigeria was a founding member and remains the most influential.
