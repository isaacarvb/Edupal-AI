# TOPIC 1: Cell Biology — Structure and Function
**Topic ID:** SSS-BIO-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of the Cell

The **cell** is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms. The cell theory (Schleiden and Schwann, 1838–39) states:
1. All living organisms are made of cells
2. The cell is the basic unit of life
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells

---

## 1.2 Types of Cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic Cell | Eukaryotic Cell |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| Examples | Bacteria, Blue-green algae | Plants, Animals, Fungi |
| Nucleus | Absent (nucleoid region only) | Present (membrane-bound) |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Size | Smaller (~1–10 μm) | Larger (~10–100 μm) |
| DNA form | Circular, no histone protein | Linear, with histones |

---

## 1.3 Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

| Feature | Plant Cell | Animal Cell |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Cell wall | Present (cellulose) | Absent |
| Chloroplast | Present (in green cells) | Absent |
| Vacuole | Large central vacuole | Small or absent |
| Centrioles | Absent | Present |
| Glycogen | Absent | Present (for storage) |
| Starch granules | Present | Absent |

---

## 1.4 Cell Organelles and Functions

| Organelle | Function |
|-----------|---------|
| **Cell membrane** | Selectively permeable barrier; controls entry/exit of substances |
| **Nucleus** | Controls cell activities; contains DNA (genetic material) |
| **Mitochondria** | Site of aerobic respiration; "powerhouse of the cell" |
| **Chloroplasts** | Site of photosynthesis (in plant cells) |
| **Ribosomes** | Site of protein synthesis |
| **Endoplasmic reticulum** | Transport network; rough ER has ribosomes; smooth ER synthesises lipids |
| **Golgi apparatus** | Packages and secretes proteins; modifies proteins |
| **Vacuole** | Storage; turgor pressure in plants |
| **Lysosomes** | Contain digestive enzymes; destroy worn-out organelles |
| **Cell wall** | Provides support and shape; prevents over-expansion |

---

## 1.5 Cell Transport

| Type | Description | Energy required | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------------|---------|
| **Diffusion** | Movement of substances from high to low concentration | No (passive) | O₂ and CO₂ exchange |
| **Osmosis** | Movement of water from high water potential (dilute) to low water potential (concentrated) through a semi-permeable membrane | No (passive) | Water in plant roots |
| **Active transport** | Movement against concentration gradient | Yes (ATP) | Mineral ion absorption in roots |
| **Phagocytosis** | Engulfing particles by cell membrane folding inward | Yes | Macrophages engulfing bacteria |
| **Pinocytosis** | Taking in liquid droplets | Yes | Cells absorbing nutrients |

---

## 1.6 Cell Division

### Mitosis (for growth and repair):
- Produces 2 genetically identical daughter cells
- Maintains chromosome number
- Phases: Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase

### Meiosis (for sexual reproduction):
- Produces 4 genetically different daughter cells (gametes)
- Halves chromosome number (2n → n)
- Two divisions: Meiosis I and Meiosis II

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Explain why a cell placed in a concentrated salt solution will lose water and become plasmolysed.

**Solution:**
The salt solution has a **lower water potential** (higher solute concentration) than the cell contents. Water moves by **osmosis** from the cell cytoplasm (higher water potential) through the semi-permeable cell membrane into the surrounding solution (lower water potential). The cell loses water, the vacuole shrinks, and the cytoplasm pulls away from the cell wall — this is called **plasmolysis**.

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** Which organelle is responsible for the synthesis of proteins?

**Solution:** **Ribosomes** — they read messenger RNA (mRNA) and assemble amino acids into proteins. They are found free in the cytoplasm and on the rough endoplasmic reticulum.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests differences between plant and animal cells, organelle functions, and osmosis/diffusion experiments.

> **JAMB** tests identification of organelles from diagrams and their functions.

> **Common trap:** Osmosis involves movement of WATER only (not solutes) through a semi-permeable membrane. Diffusion is the movement of any molecule from high to low concentration.

> The word **plasmolysis** = plant cell loses water (flaccid). **Turgor** = plant cell gains water (firm, swollen). **Crenation** = animal cell shrinks in concentrated solution.

# TOPIC 2: Genetics and Heredity
**Topic ID:** SSS-BIO-001-02

## 2.1 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Gene** | A segment of DNA that codes for a specific protein/characteristic |
| **Allele** | Alternative forms of a gene (e.g., T = tall, t = short) |
| **Genotype** | Genetic makeup of an organism (e.g., Tt, TT) |
| **Phenotype** | Physical expression of the genotype (e.g., tall, short) |
| **Homozygous** | Both alleles identical (TT or tt) |
| **Heterozygous** | Alleles different (Tt) |
| **Dominant** | Allele expressed even when only one copy is present (usually capital letter) |
| **Recessive** | Allele expressed only when two copies are present (lower case) |
| **Locus** | Position of a gene on a chromosome |
| **Diploid (2n)** | Two sets of chromosomes (body cells) |
| **Haploid (n)** | One set of chromosomes (gametes) |

---

## 2.2 Mendel's Laws

### Law 1 — Law of Segregation:
During gamete formation, the two alleles for each trait separate (segregate), so each gamete carries only one allele.

### Law 2 — Law of Independent Assortment:
Genes for different traits assort independently during gamete formation (applies to genes on different chromosomes).

---

## 2.3 Monohybrid Cross

**Example:** Tall (T) is dominant over short (t).

Cross: Tt × Tt

| | T | t |
|-|---|---|
| **T** | TT | Tt |
| **t** | Tt | tt |

Genotypic ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
Phenotypic ratio: **3 tall : 1 short**

---

## 2.4 Dihybrid Cross

**Example:** Tall (T) dominant over short (t); Round seed (R) dominant over wrinkled (r).

TTRR × ttrr → all F₁ = TtRr (tall, round)
F₂ cross: TtRr × TtRr → **9 tall round : 3 tall wrinkled : 3 short round : 1 short wrinkled**

(Standard dihybrid phenotypic ratio = **9:3:3:1**)

---

## 2.5 Sex Determination

In humans:
- **Females:** XX (homogametic)
- **Males:** XY (heterogametic)

Males determine the sex of offspring — they can pass either X (daughter) or Y (son) chromosome.

Sex-linked (X-linked) traits: genes located on the X chromosome.
Examples: haemophilia, red-green colour blindness.

**Carrier female:** X^H X^h — carries the haemophilia allele but is not affected.

---

## 2.6 DNA Structure

**DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid)** is the genetic material. It is a double helix of two antiparallel strands made of nucleotides.

Each nucleotide has:
- A deoxyribose sugar
- A phosphate group
- A nitrogenous base (A, T, G, or C)

**Base pairing rules:**
- Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T) — 2 hydrogen bonds
- Guanine (G) pairs with Cytosine (C) — 3 hydrogen bonds

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** In guinea pigs, black coat (B) is dominant over white coat (b). Two heterozygous black guinea pigs are crossed. Show the genotypic and phenotypic ratios of offspring.

**Solution:**
Parents: Bb × Bb

| | B | b |
|-|---|---|
| **B** | BB | Bb |
| **b** | Bb | bb |

Genotypic ratio: **1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb**
Phenotypic ratio: **3 black (BB + Bb) : 1 white (bb)**

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** A colour-blind man (X^c Y) marries a woman who is a carrier (X^C X^c). What is the probability of their sons being colour-blind?

**Solution:**
X^C X^c × X^c Y

| | X^C | X^c |
|-|-----|-----|
| **X^c** | X^C X^c | X^c X^c |
| **Y** | X^C Y | X^c Y |

Sons: X^C Y (normal) and X^c Y (colour blind)
Probability of colour-blind son = 1/4 (of all offspring) = **1/2 (50%) of all sons**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests Punnett squares (mono- and dihybrid), sex linkage, and calculating genotypic/phenotypic ratios.

> **JAMB** tests identification of genotypes, carrier status, and basic probability.

> **Common trap:** Dominant alleles are NOT always more common in a population. They are simply expressed over recessive alleles when present.

> Phenotypic ratio for dihybrid = 9:3:3:1. Ratio for monohybrid = 3:1. Memorise these.

# TOPIC 3: Ecology and Ecosystems
**Topic ID:** SSS-BIO-002-01

## 3.1 Key Ecological Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Ecology** | The study of relationships between organisms and their environment |
| **Ecosystem** | A community of organisms interacting with their physical environment |
| **Habitat** | The place where an organism lives |
| **Niche** | The role and position a species has in its ecosystem |
| **Community** | All the populations of different species in an area |
| **Population** | All individuals of the same species in an area |
| **Biome** | Large-scale ecosystem defined by climate and vegetation (e.g., savanna, rainforest) |

---

## 3.2 Feeding Relationships

**Food chain:** Linear sequence showing energy transfer between organisms.
Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle

**Trophic levels:**
| Level | Name | Role |
|-------|------|------|
| Level 1 | Producers | Make food via photosynthesis (plants) |
| Level 2 | Primary consumers | Herbivores (eat plants) |
| Level 3 | Secondary consumers | Carnivores (eat herbivores) |
| Level 4 | Tertiary consumers | Top predators |
| — | Decomposers | Break down dead organic matter |

**Food web:** Interconnected food chains showing complex feeding relationships.

**Energy flow:** Only ~10% of energy is transferred to the next trophic level (90% lost as heat, movement, excretion).

---

## 3.3 Ecological Pyramids

| Type | What it shows |
|------|-------------|
| **Pyramid of numbers** | Number of organisms at each trophic level |
| **Pyramid of biomass** | Total dry mass at each trophic level |
| **Pyramid of energy** | Energy available at each level |

Pyramid of energy is ALWAYS a true pyramid (cannot be inverted). Pyramid of numbers can be inverted (e.g., many aphids on one tree).

---

## 3.4 Biogeochemical Cycles

### Nitrogen Cycle:
- **Nitrogen fixation:** N₂ → NH₄⁺ (by Rhizobium bacteria in legume root nodules, or lightning)
- **Nitrification:** NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ (by nitrifying bacteria)
- **Assimilation:** Plants absorb NO₃⁻ to make amino acids
- **Denitrification:** NO₃⁻ → N₂ (by denitrifying bacteria — returns N to atmosphere)

### Carbon Cycle:
- **Photosynthesis:** CO₂ → organic carbon (plants remove CO₂)
- **Respiration:** Organic carbon → CO₂ (all organisms release CO₂)
- **Combustion:** Fossil fuels release stored CO₂
- **Decomposition:** Decomposers release CO₂ from dead matter

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** In a food chain: Grass → Rabbit → Fox → Lion, if the grass contains 100,000 kJ of energy, how much energy is available to the lion?

**Solution:**
Using the 10% energy transfer rule:
- Rabbit: 10% of 100,000 = 10,000 kJ
- Fox: 10% of 10,000 = 1,000 kJ
- Lion: 10% of 1,000 = **100 kJ**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests energy flow (10% rule), the nitrogen cycle, food chains/webs, and ecological pyramids.

> **Common trap:** Producers are plants (or algae) — they do NOT include animals, even if they are at the bottom of a pyramid.

> **Decomposers** (bacteria and fungi) are NOT in food chains but are essential for cycling nutrients. They often appear in WAEC ecology questions.

# TOPIC 4: Plant Physiology — Transport and Photosynthesis
**Topic ID:** SSS-BIO-003-01

## 4.1 Photosynthesis

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants manufacture glucose from carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.

**Word equation:** Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen

**Chemical equation:** 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

**Two stages:**
| Stage | Location | Process |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Light reactions** (Light-dependent) | Thylakoid membranes | Water is split (photolysis); ATP and NADPH produced; O₂ released |
| **Calvin cycle** (Light-independent/Dark reactions) | Stroma | CO₂ fixed into glucose using ATP and NADPH |

**Factors affecting rate of photosynthesis:**
- Light intensity
- CO₂ concentration
- Temperature

---

## 4.2 Transport in Plants

### Water Transport (Xylem):
- **Root hair cells** absorb water by osmosis
- Water moves from soil → root cortex → xylem → leaves → atmosphere
- **Transpiration:** Loss of water vapour through stomata (leaf pores)
- **Transpiration stream:** Movement of water up the plant

**Factors increasing transpiration:** High temperature, low humidity, high wind speed, light (open stomata)

### Mineral Salts Transport:
- Absorbed by root hairs via active transport
- Transported up via xylem (dissolved in water)

### Organic Substances (Phloem):
- **Translocation:** Movement of dissolved sugars (sucrose) and amino acids from leaves (source) to other parts (sinks) via phloem
- Bidirectional (up AND down the plant)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Describe an experiment to show that light is necessary for photosynthesis.

**Solution (Iodine test with variegated leaf):**
1. Use a variegated leaf (green and white parts) from a plant kept in light
2. Remove chlorophyll by boiling in ethanol
3. Add iodine solution to the decolourised leaf
4. **Result:** Green parts turn **blue-black** (starch present — photosynthesis occurred); white parts remain **yellow-brown** (no starch — no chlorophyll, so no photosynthesis)
5. **Conclusion:** Chlorophyll (and light) are necessary for photosynthesis

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests experiments to demonstrate photosynthesis, factors affecting transpiration, and xylem vs phloem functions.

> **Common trap:** Xylem carries WATER and minerals (only upward). Phloem carries organic substances (glucose as sucrose) in BOTH directions.

> The starch test uses iodine solution: starch gives a **blue-black** colour. This is the standard test for photosynthesis in WAEC practicals.

# TOPIC 5: Reproduction in Humans
**Topic ID:** SSS-BIO-004-01

## 5.1 The Male Reproductive System

| Structure | Function |
|-----------|---------|
| **Testes** | Produce sperm cells (spermatogenesis) and testosterone |
| **Epididymis** | Store and mature sperm |
| **Vas deferens** | Transport sperm from epididymis to urethra |
| **Seminal vesicles** | Produce fructose-rich fluid to nourish sperm |
| **Prostate gland** | Produces alkaline fluid (neutralises vaginal acidity) |
| **Urethra** | Carries both urine and semen out of the body |
| **Penis** | Organ of copulation; delivers sperm |

**Sperm structure:**
- Head: contains nucleus (23 chromosomes) + acrosome (enzymes to penetrate egg)
- Midpiece: packed with mitochondria (energy for movement)
- Tail (flagellum): propels the sperm

---

## 5.2 The Female Reproductive System

| Structure | Function |
|-----------|---------|
| **Ovaries** | Produce ova (eggs) and female sex hormones (oestrogen, progesterone) |
| **Fallopian tubes (oviducts)** | Transport egg from ovary to uterus; site of fertilisation |
| **Uterus** | Site of implantation and development of the foetus |
| **Cervix** | Lower part of uterus; separates uterus from vagina |
| **Vagina** | Birth canal; receives penis during copulation |

**Oogenesis:** Production of egg cells. One egg (ovum) is released per menstrual cycle (ovulation).

---

## 5.3 The Menstrual Cycle

The **menstrual cycle** is approximately 28 days and prepares the uterus for a possible pregnancy.

| Days | Event | Hormones |
|------|-------|---------|
| 1–5 | Menstruation (uterus lining shed) | Low oestrogen and progesterone |
| 6–13 | Repair and thickening of uterus lining | FSH → oestrogen rises |
| 14 | Ovulation (egg released) | LH surge; peak oestrogen |
| 15–28 | Corpus luteum secretes progesterone; lining maintained | Progesterone high |
| Day 28 | If no fertilisation, progesterone drops → cycle repeats | — |

---

## 5.4 Fertilisation and Development

**Fertilisation:** Sperm meets egg in the fallopian tube → forms **zygote** (2n)

**Implantation:** Zygote divides → blastocyst → implants into uterus wall (~day 6–9)

**Placenta:** Develops from blastocyst; allows exchange of nutrients, O₂, CO₂, and waste between mother and foetus. Produces hCG hormone (basis of pregnancy tests).

**Gestation period in humans:** ~40 weeks (9 months)

---

## 5.5 Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

| STI | Causative agent | Symptoms |
|-----|----------------|---------|
| HIV/AIDS | HIV (virus) | Immune system destruction |
| Gonorrhoea | Neisseria gonorrhoeae (bacteria) | Discharge, burning sensation |
| Syphilis | Treponema pallidum (bacteria) | Sores, rash |
| Chlamydia | Chlamydia trachomatis (bacteria) | Often asymptomatic; discharge |

**Prevention:** Abstinence, faithful partnership, condom use (ABC method)

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (WAEC type):** Explain what happens during ovulation and describe the events that follow if fertilisation does NOT occur.

**Solution:**
At approximately day 14 of the menstrual cycle, a surge of **luteinising hormone (LH)** triggers the release of a mature egg from the ovary — this is **ovulation**. The egg travels down the fallopian tube.

If fertilisation does NOT occur, the corpus luteum degenerates and stops producing **progesterone**. The drop in progesterone causes the thickened uterus lining (endometrium) to break down and be shed through the vagina. This process (days 1–5) is called **menstruation**. The cycle then repeats.

---

**Example 2 (JAMB type):** Where does fertilisation normally take place in the human female?

**Solution:** Fertilisation normally takes place in the **fallopian tube (oviduct)** — specifically in the upper third (ampulla region), not in the uterus.

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **WAEC** tests the menstrual cycle (day-by-day changes), male and female reproductive organ functions, and fertilisation.

> **JAMB** tests identification of reproductive structures, the site of fertilisation, and STI causative agents.

> **Common trap:** The site of fertilisation is the **fallopian tube**, NOT the uterus. Implantation occurs in the uterus.

> Know the three functions of the placenta: (1) exchange of oxygen/CO₂, (2) transfer of nutrients, (3) removal of foetal waste products. Also note the placenta DOES NOT allow mixing of maternal and foetal blood.
