## Oral English

### Speech Sounds, Word Stress and Conversational English
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-ENG-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify vowel and consonant sounds in English
- Apply correct stress in two- and three-syllable words
- Distinguish minimal pairs to demonstrate phonemic awareness
- Use appropriate conversational register in school and social contexts
- Apply turn-taking and listening skills in discussions

#### 📖 Notes
- Vowel sounds: short and long vowels; e.g. /ɪ/ (sit) vs. /iː/ (seat), /æ/ (cat) vs. /ɑː/ (cart)
- Consonant sounds: initial, medial and final positions; voiced vs. voiceless pairs (/b/ vs. /p/, /d/ vs. /t/, /g/ vs. /k/)
- Minimal pairs: two words differing in only one sound; e.g. bat/bad, fan/van, ship/chip
- Word stress in two-syllable words: most nouns stressed on 1st syllable (TABLE, MUsic), most verbs on 2nd (reLAX, obEY)
- Sentence stress: content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) stressed; function words (articles, prepositions) unstressed
- Intonation: falling (statements, commands), rising (yes/no questions), fall-rise (doubt, encouragement)
- Silent letters: kneel (k silent), write (w silent), know (k silent), hour (h silent), lamb (b silent)
- Formal greetings: 'Good morning, sir/ma', 'How are you?', 'May I come in?'
- Informal greetings and responses: age-appropriate casual language
- Taking turns in conversation: listening signals, interrupting politely ('Excuse me, may I add...'), summarising what was said
- Role play and conversation practice: at the market, at school, giving directions

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Practise minimal pairs in pairs: one student says 'ship' or 'sheep', the other points to the correct picture
2. Record and play back a reading; identify words with incorrect stress and practise the correct pattern
3. Role-play a conversation at a bus stop asking for directions to a hospital — practise formal politeness

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests sound identification, stress patterns, and appropriate conversational responses
> Know common silent letter words: kneel, write, know, knock, lamb, thumb, hour, honest
> Trap: word stress changes meaning — REcord (noun) vs. reCORD (verb); this is tested in BECE

## Reading and Comprehension

### Reading Skills and Vocabulary Development
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-ENG-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Apply skimming and scanning in comprehension tasks
- Determine word meanings from context
- Identify the main idea and supporting details of a passage
- Summarise a passage in own words
- Infer meaning from figurative language

#### 📖 Notes
- Skimming: reading for general idea; eyes move quickly; note headings, topic sentences, first and last paragraphs
- Scanning: searching for specific information; keywords, dates, names; used in research
- Topic sentence: sentence that states the main idea of a paragraph; usually first sentence
- Main idea: what the whole passage is about; vs. supporting details which give evidence/examples
- Context clues: use surrounding words to understand unfamiliar vocabulary
- Figurative language: simile (like/as), metaphor (direct comparison), personification (giving human traits to non-human things)
- Vocabulary in context: multiple-choice vocabulary tests use the word in a sentence to guide meaning selection
- Summary writing: identify 3–5 key points; write in own words; no opinions; one-third of original length
- Inference: reading between the lines; use text clues + general knowledge
- Text types in comprehension: narrative (story), expository (information), descriptive (describes a scene or person)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Time-limited skim of a 400-word passage (2 minutes); answer 3 main-idea questions without re-reading
2. Identify the topic sentence and 3 supporting details in each paragraph of a given text
3. Write a 100-word summary of a 300-word passage; compare with a classmate's version

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE comprehension tests: ability to find answers in the text (literal), infer meaning (inferential), and understand vocabulary in context
> Answer comprehension questions in complete sentences; begin with the key word from the question
> Trap: do not copy long chunks from the passage — paraphrase in your own words for full credit

## Writing Skills

### Paragraphing, Letter Writing and Narrative Essays
**Class:** JSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-ENG-003-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Write structured paragraphs with a clear topic sentence
- Write a narrative essay with introduction, body and conclusion
- Write formal and informal letters with correct layout
- Apply correct punctuation and spelling in writing
- Identify and correct common errors in written English

#### 📖 Notes
- Paragraph: unified block of writing on one idea; topic sentence + supporting sentences + concluding/linking sentence
- Narrative essay: tells a story; first or third person; chronological order; vivid details; dialogue; climax
- Descriptive essay: describes a person, place or event; sensory language; spatial organisation
- Informal letter: to a friend or family member; sender's address, date, salutation (Dear Chidi), body, closing (Your friend, ...); chatty, personal tone
- Formal letter: to an authority; sender's address, date, recipient's address, subject line, Dear Sir/Madam, body paragraphs, Yours faithfully, full name
- Common punctuation: full stop (.), comma (,), question mark (?), exclamation mark (!), apostrophe (don't = do not), colon (:), semi-colon (;)
- Spelling rules: ie vs. ei (believe, receive), double consonant (running, sitting), dropping final e before vowel suffix (love → loving)
- Common errors in JSS writing: comma splice, run-on sentences, lack of subject-verb agreement, inconsistent tense
- Paragraphing: new idea = new paragraph; indent or skip a line
- Sentence variety: short punchy sentences for effect; longer for description; questions to engage reader

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Write a 3-paragraph narrative: paragraph 1 (introduce situation), paragraph 2 (event/action), paragraph 3 (resolution/lesson) on 'A day I will never forget'
2. Write an informal letter to a friend describing your school's last Sports Day
3. Peer-edit a classmate's letter: check layout, punctuation, paragraph breaks and tone

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests formal/informal letter writing (layout is heavily marked), narrative essay, and correct punctuation
> Formal letter: start 'Dear Sir/Madam', close with 'Yours faithfully'; informal: start 'Dear [name]', close with 'Yours sincerely' or 'Your friend'
> Trap: mixing formal and informal language in one letter — decide the register and maintain it throughout

## Grammar

### Parts of Speech, Tenses and Sentence Construction
**Class:** JSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `JSS-ENG-004-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Identify the eight parts of speech with examples
- Use tenses correctly in oral and written contexts
- Construct grammatically correct sentences
- Distinguish between common errors and correct usage
- Apply subject-verb agreement rules

#### 📖 Notes
- Eight parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection
- Nouns: proper (Lagos), common (city), abstract (happiness), collective (flock)
- Pronouns: personal (I, you, he, she), possessive (my, your), reflexive (myself), demonstrative (this, that)
- Verbs: action verbs (run, eat), linking verbs (is, was, seems), auxiliary/helping verbs (have, will, can)
- Adjectives: describe nouns; comparative (-er) and superlative (-est) forms
- Adverbs: modify verbs, adjectives or other adverbs; often end in -ly
- Prepositions: in, on, at, by, for, with, under, between, through
- Conjunctions: coordinating (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so — FANBOYS), subordinating (because, although, when, if)
- Simple present tense: habitual/regular actions; Subject + Verb(+s) for 3rd person singular
- Simple past tense: completed actions; regular verbs add -ed; irregular verbs (go→went, eat→ate, see→saw)
- Present continuous: action happening now; Subject + is/are/am + Verb-ing
- Subject-verb agreement: singular subject → singular verb; plural subject → plural verb

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Underline and label every word in 5 sentences by its part of speech
2. Change 10 sentences from present to past tense; identify which verbs are regular and which are irregular
3. Spot the error: teacher provides 10 sentences with one grammatical error each; students find and correct them

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> BECE tests part of speech identification, tense usage, and subject-verb agreement — practise with real sentences
> Know the 25 most common irregular verbs: go/went, have/had, do/did, say/said, get/got, come/came, make/made, etc.
> Trap: 'Neither the students nor the teacher ARE here' — verb agrees with the NEARER subject (teacher — singular); should be 'is'
