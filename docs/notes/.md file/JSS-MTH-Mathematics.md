# TOPIC 1: Whole Numbers — Place Value, Operations and Number Properties
**Topic ID:** JSS-MTH-001-01

## 1.1 Definition of Whole Numbers

**Whole numbers** are the set of all non-negative integers: {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}. They have no fractions or decimal parts.

---

## 1.2 Place Value

Every digit in a number has a **place value** depending on its position.

| Position | Name | Value |
|----------|------|-------|
| 1st from right | Ones | × 1 |
| 2nd from right | Tens | × 10 |
| 3rd | Hundreds | × 100 |
| 4th | Thousands | × 1,000 |
| 5th | Ten-thousands | × 10,000 |
| 6th | Hundred-thousands | × 100,000 |
| 7th | Millions | × 1,000,000 |

**Example:** In 4,572,836:
- 4 is in the millions position = 4,000,000
- 5 is in the hundred-thousands = 500,000
- 7 is in the ten-thousands = 70,000

---

## 1.3 Operations on Whole Numbers

### Order of Operations (BODMAS/BIDMAS):
| Letter | Operation |
|--------|-----------|
| **B** | Brackets first |
| **O/I** | Orders (powers/indices)/Indices |
| **D** | Division |
| **M** | Multiplication |
| **A** | Addition |
| **S** | Subtraction |

Work left to right for same-level operations.

---

## 1.4 Number Properties

| Property | Rule | Example |
|----------|------|---------|
| **Commutative** (+ and ×) | a + b = b + a | 3 + 5 = 5 + 3 |
| **Associative** (+ and ×) | (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) | (2 + 3) + 4 = 2 + (3 + 4) |
| **Distributive** | a(b + c) = ab + ac | 3(4 + 5) = 12 + 15 |
| **Identity (addition)** | a + 0 = a | 7 + 0 = 7 |
| **Identity (multiplication)** | a × 1 = a | 7 × 1 = 7 |

---

## 1.5 Factors, Multiples, HCF and LCM

**Factors** of a number: All numbers that divide it exactly.
Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

**Multiples** of a number: The number multiplied by positive integers.
Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, ...

**Highest Common Factor (HCF):** The largest number that divides all given numbers exactly.
HCF of 12 and 18:
- Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, **6**, 12
- Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, **6**, 9, 18
- HCF = **6**

**Lowest Common Multiple (LCM):** The smallest number that is a multiple of all given numbers.
LCM of 4 and 6:
- Multiples of 4: 4, 8, **12**, 16...
- Multiples of 6: 6, **12**, 18...
- LCM = **12**

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Calculate: 15 + 3 × (8 − 2) ÷ 6

**Solution:** Using BODMAS:
Brackets first: (8 − 2) = 6
Then: 15 + 3 × 6 ÷ 6
Division first: 6 ÷ 6 = 1
Then multiply: 3 × 1 = 3
Finally add: 15 + 3 = **18**

---

**Example 2 (BECE type):** Find the HCF and LCM of 24 and 36.

**Solution (prime factorisation):**
24 = 2³ × 3
36 = 2² × 3²

HCF = 2² × 3 = 4 × 3 = **12**
LCM = 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = **72**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests BODMAS/BIDMAS calculations, HCF, LCM, and place value.

> **Common trap:** In BODMAS, Multiplication and Division are at the SAME level — work left to right. Same for Addition and Subtraction.

# TOPIC 2: Integers, Fractions, Percentages and Decimals
**Topic ID:** JSS-MTH-001-02

## 2.1 Integers

**Integers** include all whole numbers and their negatives: {..., −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}

**Number line rules:**
- Adding a positive number moves RIGHT
- Adding a negative number moves LEFT
- Subtracting a negative = adding a positive: 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8

**Sign rules for multiplication and division:**
- (+) × (+) = (+)
- (−) × (−) = (+)
- (+) × (−) = (−)
- (−) × (+) = (−)

---

## 2.2 Fractions

**Fraction types:**

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Proper** | Numerator < Denominator | 3/5 |
| **Improper** | Numerator ≥ Denominator | 7/4 |
| **Mixed number** | Whole number + fraction | 2¾ |

**Operations:**
- Addition/Subtraction: Find common denominator first
- Multiplication: Multiply numerators × numerators, denominators × denominators
- Division: Multiply by the reciprocal (flip the second fraction)

---

## 2.3 Decimals

**Decimal places:** Numbers after the decimal point.

| Operation | Rule |
|----------|------|
| Adding/Subtracting | Line up decimal points |
| Multiplying | Multiply ignoring decimal, then count total decimal places in both numbers |
| Dividing | Make divisor a whole number by multiplying both by 10ⁿ |

**Converting:** Fraction to decimal → divide numerator by denominator
3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = **0.75**

---

## 2.4 Percentages

**Percentage** means "out of 100."

| Conversion | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| Fraction to % | Multiply by 100 |
| Decimal to % | Multiply by 100 |
| % of a number | (% ÷ 100) × number |
| % increase | (Increase / Original) × 100 |
| % decrease | (Decrease / Original) × 100 |

**Percentage profit and loss:**
- % Profit = (Profit / Cost price) × 100
- % Loss = (Loss / Cost price) × 100

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Calculate: ¾ + 2/5

**Solution:**
LCM of 4 and 5 = 20
¾ = 15/20 | 2/5 = 8/20
15/20 + 8/20 = **23/20 = 1 3/20**

---

**Example 2 (BECE type):** A trader buys a bag for ₦800 and sells it for ₦1,000. Calculate the percentage profit.

**Solution:**
Profit = 1,000 − 800 = ₦200
% Profit = (200/800) × 100 = **25%**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests fraction arithmetic, percentage calculations (profit/loss/discount), and integer operations.

> **Common trap:** Percentage profit is ALWAYS calculated on COST PRICE, not selling price. % Loss is also based on cost price.

# TOPIC 3: Algebra — Expressions, Equations and Inequalities
**Topic ID:** JSS-MTH-002-01

## 3.1 Algebraic Expressions

An **algebraic expression** contains numbers, variables (letters), and operations.

**Parts of an expression:**
- **Term:** A single number or variable or product (e.g., 3x, 5, 2y²)
- **Coefficient:** The number part of a term (in 4x, coefficient = 4)
- **Like terms:** Terms with the same variable and power (3x and 5x are like terms)

**Simplifying:** Collect like terms.
3x + 5y − 2x + y = x + 6y

---

## 3.2 Expansion and Factorisation

**Expanding brackets:** Multiply each term inside by the term outside.
3(2x + 4) = 6x + 12

**Factorising (common factor):** Reverse of expansion.
6x + 12 = 3(2x + 4) — take out the HCF

---

## 3.3 Simple Linear Equations

**Equation:** Two expressions equal to each other.
**Solving:** Find the value of the unknown that makes both sides equal.

**Steps:**
1. Expand brackets if needed
2. Collect variable terms on one side
3. Collect number terms on the other side
4. Divide both sides by the coefficient of the variable

**Example:** 3x + 5 = 14
3x = 14 − 5 = 9
x = 9/3 = **3**

---

## 3.4 Simultaneous Equations (JSS level)

Two equations with two unknowns.

**Methods:**
1. **Substitution:** Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation; substitute into the second.
2. **Elimination:** Make coefficients equal; add/subtract to eliminate one variable.

---

## 3.5 Inequalities

An **inequality** shows a relationship where one side is greater or less than the other.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| > | Greater than |
| < | Less than |
| ≥ | Greater than or equal to |
| ≤ | Less than or equal to |

**Solving inequalities:** Same rules as equations EXCEPT — when you multiply or divide by a NEGATIVE number, the inequality sign FLIPS.

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Solve: 2(3x − 1) = 4x + 8

**Solution:**
Expand: 6x − 2 = 4x + 8
Collect x terms: 6x − 4x = 8 + 2
2x = 10
x = **5**

Check: 2(3×5 − 1) = 2(14) = 28; 4(5) + 8 = 28 ✓

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests simplifying expressions, solving linear equations, and basic simultaneous equations.

> **Common trap:** When moving a term across the = sign, its SIGN changes. 3x + 5 = 14 → 3x = 14 − 5 (the +5 becomes −5 when moved).

# TOPIC 4: Plane Geometry — Angles, Triangles and Polygons
**Topic ID:** JSS-MTH-003-01

## 4.1 Angles

**Angle** = The amount of turn between two lines meeting at a point.

| Type | Range |
|------|-------|
| **Acute** | 0° < x < 90° |
| **Right angle** | x = 90° |
| **Obtuse** | 90° < x < 180° |
| **Straight** | x = 180° |
| **Reflex** | 180° < x < 360° |

### Angle Relationships:
| Relationship | Rule |
|-------------|------|
| Complementary angles | Add to 90° |
| Supplementary angles | Add to 180° |
| Angles on a straight line | Add to 180° |
| Angles at a point | Add to 360° |
| Vertically opposite angles | Equal |
| Corresponding angles (parallel lines) | Equal |
| Alternate angles (parallel lines) | Equal |
| Co-interior angles (parallel lines) | Add to 180° |

---

## 4.2 Triangles

**Sum of angles in any triangle = 180°**

| Type | Property |
|------|---------|
| **Equilateral** | All sides equal; all angles 60° |
| **Isosceles** | Two sides equal; base angles equal |
| **Scalene** | No sides equal; no angles equal |
| **Right-angled** | One angle = 90° |

**Pythagoras' Theorem** (for right-angled triangles):
a² + b² = c²
where c is the hypotenuse (longest side, opposite the right angle).

---

## 4.3 Polygons — Interior and Exterior Angles

| Polygon | Sides | Sum of interior angles |
|---------|-------|----------------------|
| Triangle | 3 | 180° |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | 360° |
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° |
| Octagon | 8 | 1080° |
| n-gon | n | (n − 2) × 180° |

**For a regular polygon:**
- Each interior angle = (n − 2) × 180° / n
- Each exterior angle = 360° / n
- Interior + Exterior angle = 180°

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** A triangle has angles x, 2x, and 3x. Find x.

**Solution:**
x + 2x + 3x = 180°
6x = 180°
x = **30°** → angles are 30°, 60°, and 90°.

---

**Example 2 (BECE type):** Calculate the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with legs 6 cm and 8 cm.

**Solution:**
c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100
c = √100 = **10 cm**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests angle calculations (parallel lines), triangle angle sum, Pythagoras' theorem, and polygon interior angles.

> **Common trap:** The hypotenuse is always OPPOSITE the right angle and is always the LONGEST side. Do not confuse it with any other side.

# TOPIC 5: Perimeter, Area and Volume
**Topic ID:** JSS-MTH-004-01

## 5.1 Perimeter

**Perimeter** = total length of the boundary of a 2D shape.

| Shape | Perimeter formula |
|-------|-----------------|
| Rectangle | P = 2(l + b) |
| Square | P = 4s |
| Triangle | P = a + b + c |
| Circle (circumference) | C = 2πr = πd |

---

## 5.2 Area

**Area** = measure of the surface enclosed by a 2D shape.

| Shape | Area formula |
|-------|-------------|
| Rectangle | A = l × b |
| Square | A = s² |
| Triangle | A = ½ × base × height |
| Parallelogram | A = base × height |
| Trapezium | A = ½(a + b) × h |
| Circle | A = πr² |

**π (pi) ≈ 3.142 or 22/7**

---

## 5.3 Volume and Surface Area (3D shapes)

| Shape | Volume | Surface area |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| Cuboid | V = l × b × h | SA = 2(lb + lh + bh) |
| Cube | V = s³ | SA = 6s² |
| Cylinder | V = πr²h | SA = 2πr(r + h) |
| Sphere | V = 4/3 πr³ | SA = 4πr² |
| Cone | V = 1/3 πr²h | SA = πr(r + l), l = slant height |

---

## ✏️ Worked Examples

**Example 1 (BECE type):** Find the area and circumference of a circle with radius 7 cm. (Take π = 22/7)

**Solution:**
Area = πr² = (22/7) × 7² = (22/7) × 49 = 22 × 7 = **154 cm²**
Circumference = 2πr = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 2 × 22 = **44 cm**

---

**Example 2 (BECE type):** A rectangular tank is 5 m long, 3 m wide, and 2 m high. Find its volume.

**Solution:**
V = l × b × h = 5 × 3 × 2 = **30 m³**

---

## 🏆 Exam Focus

> **BECE** tests area and perimeter of all plane shapes, volume of 3D solids, and circle calculations.

> **Common trap:** Area is in square units (cm², m²); volume is in cubic units (cm³, m³). Never give area in cm or volume in cm² — the units must match the dimension.

> Always re-read whether the question asks for radius or diameter. π × diameter = circumference; π × radius² = area.
