## Exercise Physiology and Health

### Exercise Physiology — Cardiovascular and Respiratory Responses
**Class:** SSS1  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHE-001-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the structure and function of the cardiovascular and respiratory systems
- Explain the body's acute and chronic responses to exercise
- Measure and interpret resting and exercise heart rate, blood pressure and breathing rate
- Explain the concepts of aerobic and anaerobic exercise and their energy systems
- Relate regular exercise to cardiovascular health

#### 📖 Notes
- Cardiovascular system: heart (4 chambers — RA, RV, LA, LV), blood vessels (arteries carry blood away from heart; veins carry back; capillaries exchange gases/nutrients), blood (plasma, erythrocytes, leukocytes, platelets)
- Cardiac output: Q = heart rate × stroke volume; average resting: ~5 L/min; during intense exercise: up to 25 L/min
- Heart rate: normal resting 60–100 bpm; trained athletes may have resting HR of 40–50 bpm (bradycardia — adaptation)
- Blood pressure: systolic (contraction)/diastolic (relaxation); normal ≈120/80 mmHg
- Respiratory system: nose/mouth → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli; alveoli = site of gas exchange (O₂ in, CO₂ out)
- Tidal volume: air breathed in one breath (~500 mL at rest); increases during exercise
- Minute ventilation: tidal volume × breathing rate; increases dramatically during exercise
- Acute responses to exercise: increased HR, increased stroke volume, increased cardiac output, increased breathing rate, increased tidal volume, vasodilation of muscles, vasoconstriction of non-working organs
- Chronic adaptations to training: lower resting HR, larger stroke volume, stronger heart muscle (cardiac hypertrophy), increased lung capacity, increased blood volume, more efficient O₂ use
- Energy systems: ATP-PC system (0–10 seconds, explosive activities), glycolytic/lactic acid system (10 seconds–2 minutes, anaerobic), aerobic oxidative system (>2 minutes, endurance)
- VO₂ max: maximum rate of oxygen consumption; gold standard of cardiovascular fitness
- Lifestyle diseases prevented by exercise: hypertension, type 2 diabetes, obesity, coronary heart disease, some cancers

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Measure resting HR and BP for 5 classmates; have them jog for 5 minutes; re-measure immediately after and 5 minutes after; graph recovery
2. Design a 12-week aerobic training programme for a hypertensive adult, specifying mode, intensity (THR zone), duration and frequency per week
3. Calculate target heart rate (THR) zone using Karvonen formula: THR = (HRmax − HRrest) × intensity% + HRrest

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests cardiovascular anatomy, acute vs. chronic training adaptations, energy system identification from activity type
> Know: aerobic exercise uses oxygen (long duration, moderate intensity); anaerobic does not (short duration, high intensity)
> Trap: 'resting heart rate decreasing with training' is a CHRONIC adaptation (long-term change), not an acute response to a single exercise bout

### Health Promotion and Lifestyle Diseases
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHE-001-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Define health and explain the WHO definition of health
- Identify major lifestyle diseases prevalent in Nigeria and their risk factors
- Design and promote health behaviour change programmes
- Explain the Ottawa Charter for Health Promotion
- Evaluate nutrition's role in health and athletic performance

#### 📖 Notes
- WHO definition of health (1948): 'a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease or infirmity'
- Health dimensions: physical, mental/psychological, social, emotional, spiritual, environmental
- Health promotion: enabling people to increase control over their health; Ottawa Charter (1986) strategies: building healthy public policy, creating supportive environments, strengthening community action, developing personal skills, reorienting health services
- Lifestyle diseases (non-communicable diseases — NCDs): caused by behaviours; not infectious; major cause of death in Nigeria increasingly
- Hypertension: persistent BP >140/90 mmHg; risk factors: obesity, smoking, sedentary lifestyle, high sodium diet, stress; prevention: exercise, low-salt diet, reduce alcohol
- Type 2 diabetes: insufficient insulin response; risk factors: obesity, physical inactivity, poor diet; prevention: weight control, exercise, healthy diet
- Obesity: BMI ≥30; associated with hypertension, diabetes, heart disease, joint problems; causes: excessive caloric intake + insufficient physical activity
- Coronary Heart Disease (CHD): atherosclerosis (plaque in coronary arteries); risk factors: smoking, high LDL cholesterol, hypertension, obesity
- Cancer: uncontrolled cell growth; lifestyle-related: lung cancer (smoking), colorectal cancer (low fibre diet), skin cancer (UV exposure)
- Nutrition for athletes: macronutrients (CHO for energy — 60% of diet; protein for muscle repair — 1.2–2.0 g/kg; fat for energy and hormones), micronutrients, hydration (water loss through sweat)
- Carbohydrate loading: strategy to maximize glycogen stores before endurance events
- Eating disorders: anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa — serious mental health conditions with physical consequences

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Conduct a health risk factor survey in the school: measure BMI for 20 students; identify the percentage that fall in healthy, overweight and obese categories
2. Design a community health promotion campaign for Diabetes Day (November 14) targeting a Lagos secondary school population
3. Analyse a Nigerian athlete's training diet; identify nutritional deficiencies and recommend improvements using Nigeria's food composition table

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests WHO definition, lifestyle disease risk factors and prevention, and Ottawa Charter health promotion strategies
> Know BMI classification: Underweight <18.5, Normal 18.5–24.9, Overweight 25–29.9, Obese ≥30
> Trap: lifestyle diseases are NOT infectious and cannot be spread from person to person — they are caused by individual behaviour and genetics

## Sports Science and Administration

### Sports Administration and Organizations
**Class:** SSS2  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHE-002-01`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Describe the structure of sports administration at local, national and international levels
- Explain the roles of major Nigerian and international sports organisations
- Identify the principles of effective sports administration
- Plan and organise a school sports competition
- Evaluate the roles of government in sports development

#### 📖 Notes
- Sports administration: management of sports organisations, facilities, programmes and personnel for optimal performance and development
- Levels of sports administration: grassroots/school level, state, national (NSF — National Sports Federation), international (IFs — International Federations)
- Nigerian sports organisations: NSC (National Sports Commission), NFF (Nigeria Football Federation), NBBF (Nigeria Basketball Federation), Athletics Federation of Nigeria (AFN), NOC (National Olympic Committee of Nigeria)
- International organisations: IOC (International Olympic Committee — Paris; governs Olympic Games; founded 1894); FIFA (football — Zurich; founded 1904); IAAF/World Athletics (athletics); FIBA (basketball); CAF (Confederation of African Football)
- African sports organisations: CAF (African football), ANOCA (African National Olympic Committees), CANA (African aquatics)
- Principles of sports administration: planning, organising, directing, controlling, staffing (PODCS)
- Types of tournaments: round robin (league system — everyone plays everyone), knock-out/elimination, combination, double elimination
- Planning a sports competition: objectives, schedule, budget, venues, officials, rules, prizes, transport, safety
- NSSF (National School Sports Federation) — organises inter-school sports in Nigeria
- Government's role in sports: funding, policy, facilities, talent identification, international representation, anti-doping
- Sports funding in Nigeria: government allocation, sponsorship, sports lottery, FIFA/IOC grants
- Volunteerism and sports: role of volunteers in grassroots development

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Organise a class mini-tournament in any sport using a round-robin format: draw up fixtures, record results, calculate points and rank teams
2. Create an organisational chart of Nigerian football administration from FIFA → CAF → NFF → State FAs → Clubs
3. Write a proposal for a sports festival at your school: include objectives, programme, budget, officials needed, and safety plan

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests functions of sports organisations (NOC, NSF, NFF), tournament types, and principles of sports administration
> Know: IOC (Olympic Games), FIFA (football), IAAF (athletics), FIBA (basketball), ITF (tennis)
> Trap: NSC (National Sports Commission) is a government agency; NOC (National Olympic Committee) is a non-governmental body representing Nigeria at the Olympics — different entities

### Sports Medicine — Injuries, First Aid and Prevention
**Class:** SSS3  |  **Topic ID:** `SSS-PHE-002-02`

#### 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Classify sports injuries and explain their causes
- Apply basic first aid procedures for common sports injuries
- Explain the RICE/PRICE principle for soft tissue injury management
- Describe warm-up and cool-down as injury prevention strategies
- Identify banned substances and explain anti-doping policies

#### 📖 Notes
- Sports injuries classification: acute (sudden onset — fracture, strain, sprain, contusion, laceration) vs. chronic/overuse (gradual onset — shin splints, stress fracture, tendinitis, bursitis)
- Sprain: ligament injury (connects bone to bone); graded I (mild), II (partial tear), III (complete tear)
- Strain: muscle or tendon injury; most common: hamstring strain, calf strain
- Fracture types: closed (skin intact), open/compound (bone penetrates skin), stress (hairline crack from repeated loading)
- Dislocation: bones of a joint forced out of alignment; common: shoulder, fingers
- PRICE principle: Protect, Rest, Ice (20 min on/off), Compression, Elevation — for acute soft tissue injuries
- DR ABC in emergency: Danger, Response, Airway, Breathing, Circulation — emergency assessment order
- CPR: Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation; 30 compressions (5–6 cm depth, 100–120/min) : 2 breaths ratio
- Heat-related illness: heat cramps (muscle cramps due to dehydration), heat exhaustion (weakness, dizziness), heat stroke (medical emergency — confusion, collapse, high core temp)
- Warm-up: 10–15 minutes; gradually increases body temperature and heart rate; reduces injury risk; includes general (aerobic) and specific (sport-related) components
- Cool-down: returns body to resting state; prevents blood pooling; static stretching for flexibility
- Performance-enhancing drugs (PEDs): anabolic steroids, EPO (blood doping), stimulants, diuretics, HGH
- WADA (World Anti-Doping Agency): sets prohibited list; in-competition and out-of-competition testing; Nigeria Anti-Doping Agency (NADA)

#### ✏️ Activities
1. Simulate a sports first aid scenario: one student 'sprains their ankle' during a football game; class applies PRICE treatment step by step
2. Demonstrate and practise CPR on a mannequin or cushion: compression depth, rate and rescue breath technique
3. Create a poster for the school changing room: 'Top 5 ways to prevent sports injuries' — include warm-up, equipment, hydration, technique and rest

#### 🏆 Exam Focus
> WAEC tests injury classification, RICE/PRICE procedure, DR ABC order, and warm-up/cool-down purposes
> Know: sprain = ligament; strain = muscle/tendon — a common exam distinction
> Trap: ICE is for ACUTE injuries (within 48 hours); HEAT is for CHRONIC injuries (after 48 hours) — applying heat to a fresh sprain makes it worse
